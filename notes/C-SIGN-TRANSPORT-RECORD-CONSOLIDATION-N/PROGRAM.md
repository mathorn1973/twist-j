## The single program question

For prescribed positive integers d and h, prepare one apparatus in which
only a local source depends on an unknown sign a in {-1,+1}. The payer,
channel, receiver, layer order and reader must be identical for both inputs.
Apply one fixed continuing bijection. The receiver must first acquire the
sign after a declared delay and read it correctly for h complete ticks,
while every supplied resource remains inside the conserved full account.

This replaces a collection of module demonstrations with one joint
contract. A local writer and a channel cannot be joined by externally
reading the source, selecting a message-dependent channel preparation, or
changing the law after an observed event.

The [explicit construction](CONSTRUCTION.md) connects the unknown local sign
to two travelling occupations using their separation as the message. It
does not pretend that the predecessor already loaded its separately
prepared one-high position code. The receiver is a fixed occupied delay
region with a fixed local pattern reader and a counted autonomous B clock.
It is not a stationary one-cell latch or a literally empty receiver.

## What the new connection changes

The inherited four-cell writer uses a common payer and a sign-dependent
cycle. A fixed interface to a prepared B rail converts that difference into
different emission timing. After the short encoding prefix there are two
high occupations on the same rail. Their separation, rather than an
externally selected initial position, carries the sign.

The detailed law, coordinates and proof are in CONSTRUCTION.md. Its first
use has three decisive properties:

1. Only the source coordinate is sign-dependent initially.
2. The same ordered composition of local B gates continues before, during
   and after the read interval.
3. A fixed receiver-only reader recognizes the sign without access to the
   source, current time, a hidden sign label or an external controller.

The data rail uses a counted occupied region as a delay memory. The rail length also keeps the receiver at graph distance at least 2d from the launch site along either route around the ring. Its
prescribed retention is the interval for which the moving code remains
inside that region. Increasing the hold time increases the region and
initial stock; retention is not free.

The all-time version also includes a common finite B clock. Put

    L=2d+h+3,  P=2L+4,  tau=d+2.

The analytical data return periods are P for the positive input and P/2 for
the negative input. The clock has period P and is prepared identically for
both signs. Its local corridor qualifies the fixed data reader only during

    tau <= n mod P <= tau+h-1.

This is a state-dependent reader of the receiver and its local clock
corridor, not a reader that is handed the current time. The continuing law
itself never depends on the sign, reader output, or external time.

The joint construction has

    raw cells = 12L+20,
    H = 12L+28-6phi,
    E = 24L+50,
    S = 2a.

For d=4 and h=8 this gives L=19, P=42, 248 raw cells and E=506. The fixed
reader returns the sign at full boundaries 6 through 13 modulo 42 and
BLANK at all other full boundaries. The construction also specifies its
intermediate-layer contract. This instance is included in the executed
finite audit. Its three-period check supports that bounded inventory;
the all-time and all-parameter assertions have their separate proof.

The clock qualification matters: later unqualified returning patterns can
imitate the wrong sign. The longer complete cycle is part of the proof, not
an optional numerical extension of the first successful window.

The finite-window and all-time requirements must remain separate:

| Requirement | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| First-window correctness | Both signs are read correctly on the same prescribed h-tick interval after first arrival |
| Intermediate-layer correctness | The interpretation remains correct at the declared substep boundaries, if claimed |
| Full-carrier continuation | Every later occupied state still has a unique conservative successor and predecessor |
| All-time truthful reader | Every later nonblank reading is correct, including after wrap and recurrence |
| Permanent archive | The sign remains available indefinitely in the declared receiver |
| Fresh renewal | A later output contains a new independent input rather than a recurrence of the original one |

The first three do not imply the last three. In particular, bijective
continuation does not make a pattern reader truthful on later returning
patterns. Here the counted clock and the exact data recurrence support the
fourth requirement analytically. Permanent storage and fresh renewal are
not claimed. Later good windows carry the original sign again.
