## 1. Cylinder coordinate and local Blaschke factor

For a rational prime `p`, put

```text
r = p^(-1/2),
z_p(s) = p^(s-1/2).
```

On the critical line `s=1/2+i xi`, `|z_p|=1`. The standard local ratio

```text
gamma_p(s)=(1-p^(-s))^(-1),
rho_p(s)=gamma_p(s)/gamma_p(1-s)
```

satisfies exactly

```text
rho_p(s) = z_p(s) / b_r(z_p(s)),
b_r(z)   = (z-r)/(1-r z).
```

The map `b_r` is the degree-one Blaschke factor with zero `r` and is the
transfer function of the explicit `2x2` orthogonal colligation with a
one-dimensional state already recorded in `EULER-LOCAL-COMPLETION.md`.

## 2. Exact identification with Connes--Consani B_p

Connes--Consani use the conformal map

```text
psi(v)=1/2+(v+1)/(v-1)
```

from the disk to the left critical half-plane, and write

```text
kappa_p(v)=rho_p(psi(v)).
```

Their Proposition 3.7 defines the Blaschke product `B_p(v)` from the full pole
orbit of `kappa_p` and proves

```text
kappa_p(v) B_p(v) = -p^((v+1)/(v-1)).
```

But

```text
p^((v+1)/(v-1)) = p^(psi(v)-1/2) = z_p(psi(v)),
```

while the preceding local identity gives

```text
kappa_p(v) = z_p(psi(v))/b_r(z_p(psi(v))).
```

Therefore, identically on the disk,

```text
B_p(v) = -b_r(z_p(psi(v))).
```

Thus the degree-one local Blaschke factor on the prime cylinder and the
infinite Blaschke product used by Connes--Consani on the simply connected
Hardy disk are the same inner object seen before and after unfolding the
periodic imaginary direction. The infinite zero set in their disk is the lift
of the single cylinder zero `z_p=r` through `p^(s-1/2)=r`.

**Status:** candidate-T, exact identity obtained by combining the two displayed
source formulas.

## 4. Exact two-leg update when a prime is added

For a finite place set `F`, let

```text
u_F = rho_inf * product_(p in F) rho_p.
```

For a new prime `p` define

```text
D_p(s)=1-p^(-s),
N_p(s)=1-p^(s-1),
rho_p=N_p/D_p.
```

The Connes--Consani Sonin filtration proof uses exactly

```text
xi' = D_p xi,
u_(F union {p}) xi' = N_p u_F xi.
```

Thus adding a complete prime acts diagonally on the input/output scattering
graph by

```text

```

On the critical line

```text
N_p(1/2+i xi) = conjugate(D_p(1/2+i xi)),
|N_p|=|D_p|.
```

Hence the new local factor changes the two graph legs with exactly matched
pointwise modulus. This is the Hardy/scattering analogue of the matched
positive/negative diagonal increment found in the frozen cutoff law when a
new complete prime tower first enters outside the old support.

## 5. Local multiplier identity behind the prime update

Still with `z=z_p(s)` and `r=p^(-1/2)`, one has exactly

```text
D_p = 1-r z^(-1),
N_p = 1-r z,
b_r(z) = z D_p / N_p.
```

Therefore for any boundary vector `eta`,

```text
z D_p eta = b_r(z) N_p eta.
```

The local multiplier pair `(D_p eta,N_p eta)` becomes a free/scattered pair
after applying the coordinate shift `z` to the first leg; those two local legs
are related by the same lossless transfer function `b_r` whose scalar
quadratures occur in the complete-Euler norm symbols.

The actual semilocal graph update in Section 4 is

```text

```

It retains the old multiplier `u_F`. Since boundary multipliers commute, its
exact relation is

```text
N_p u_F xi = u_F b_r^(-1) z D_p xi,
```

not a bare `b_r` intertwining of the two updated graph legs.

Equivalently, if

```text

Y = z D_p eta,
```

then

```text

||Y||=||X||  on the critical boundary,
```

For the local pair, the parity combinations


bypass. An explicit outer reweighting/spectral-factor map would be required to
identify these norms.

Thus the Connes prime-set injection and the local `2x2`, one-state colligation

## 6. Infinitesimal link to the Weil symbol



