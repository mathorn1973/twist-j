# Uniform signed-slice Xi bound for arbitrary even planar product bundles

**PUBLIC, NON-CANONICAL.**
**Status:** candidate-T written derivation plus candidate-C one-architecture audit.
Owner: #1188.
Author: A. M. Thorn.
Date: 26 September 2026.

This note extends the signed-cycle program from fixed-width ribbons and square
tubes to a product-bundle family with arbitrary even simple planar
cross-section. Extra unit-distance chord contacts inside the cross-section are
allowed.

The mechanism is entropy rather than low contact count: all strands are still
generated by one base path.

## 1. Geometry and source price

Let the cross-section be a simple even square-lattice polygon of length `k`,
and let the base path have length `D`. Alternate strand orientation around
the polygon and connect successive strands at alternating path endpoints.

For every admitted simple assembled cycle,

`
m=k(D+1).
`

Let `c_P` be the number of turns of the base path. Then

`
c=k c_P+2k.
`

Let `E_Q` be the number of nearest-neighbor pairs among all cross-section
vertices, including polygon edges and unit-distance chords. The induced square
lattice graph has degree at most four, so

`
E_Q<=2k.
`

Each such pair gives one reinforcing strand contact per base-path edge.
Connector contacts contribute at most `k` more opposite-edge pairs. Hence

`
c+O_++O_-
 <= k c_P+2kD+3k.                                    (1)
`

At the exact source choice `h=log2`,

`
a=15625/131072,
r=34/25,
s=16/25.
`

Dropping the beneficial `s<r` distinction for connector contacts gives for
one fixed description, including both current signs,

`
P(E_gamma union E_-gamma)
 <=2 a^[k(D+1)] r^[2kD+k c_P+3k].                    (2)
`

## 2. Description entropy

Root the cross-section polygon. A nonbacktracking square-lattice polygon
description has at most four first steps and three later choices:

`
#Q_descriptions <=4*3^(k-1).
`

For fixed first base-path direction, each later step is either straight or one
of at most two turns. A base-path turn is replicated on all `k` strands and
costs `r^k`.

Define

`
q_k
 =a^k r^(2k)(1+2r^k)
 =(a r^2)^k+2(a r^3)^k.
`

The exact constants are

`
a r^2 = 7225/32768 <1,
a r^3 = 4913/16384 <1.
`

Therefore, for every `k>=4`,

`
q_k<=q_4,
q_4=

<1.                                                     (3)
`

The cross-section-size factor is

`
b=3a^2r^5
 =106489275/536870912
 <1.                                                     (4)
`

Thus both the base length and arbitrary even cross-section length are
summable.

## 4. What was checked

The frozen exact audit passed. It reconstructed all rational source constants
and both generating-series identities.

It built 33 explicit four-dimensional bundle cycles from cross-sections

`
1x1, 1x2, 2x2
`

and straight or one-turn base paths through length six. It derived the actual
edge orientations, turn counts, `O_+,O_-`, connector contacts and signed
slice primitive.

The `1x2` cross-section contains a nearest-neighbor chord beyond the polygon
edges and therefore explicitly audits the extra-contact case.

## 5. Boundary

This is not a covering theorem for all simple cycles.

Controlled now are several genuinely high-contact, all-distance classes:
signed low-contact cycles, sign-compensated cycles, ribbons, square tubes and
arbitrary even planar product bundles.

Still open are simple cycles with no single product decomposition of this
kind, non-simple or branched current networks, components containing several
current cycles, full `Xi_L`, `Xi_L^(2)`, P1 and the massless phase.

Public Canon v92 is unchanged.
