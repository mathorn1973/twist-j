## A. Finite-cycle and funded-record attacks

1. Prove or refute the equivalence by summing a proposed coboundary around
   each complete cycle, then constructing a potential by partial sums. Do not
   divide by cycle length: lengths divisible by five may conceal otherwise
   necessary constraints. Verify the exact coefficient field, constancy of
   E on a cycle, and the importance of a complete permutation orbit.
2. Derive the funded source inverse from the exposed equations. Treat
   accepted and rejected steps separately and inspect bank-zero boundaries,
   the direction flip, and the advancing controller phase. A false claim of
   bijectivity would invalidate the complete-cycle application.
3. Check whether the scalar a=q Delta p equals Delta u and Delta v on every
   branch, including rejection. Distinguish an exact integer coboundary from
   a residue-only coincidence, and distinguish complete-state return from
   return only in q,p. Check for hidden increment conventions.
4. Hand-derive short complete cycles with energies zero and one, preserving
   both signs and c. Use their lengths modulo five to test the claimed
   universal restriction. A bounded inventory cannot by itself imply a
   universal classification; a proof must supply universal sufficiency and
   globally admitted witnesses for necessity.
5. Independently trace the two specified cold preparations, with B=0 and
   B=4, by exact arithmetic on paper. Check that the alleged period is the
   least complete-state return, and that each orbit's energy and length
   provide independent linear constraints over F5. Try to defeat the
   restricted-family conclusion by a cycle length divisible by five or
   coincident energy residues.
6. Test interpretation boundaries: a removable scalar residue phase does not
   by itself classify a full Weyl operator, a U(1) temporal phase, an energy
   reading on a different carrier, or a continuous generator. The generic
   finite-cycle criterion has no physical conclusion without those bridges.

## B. Normalized Dirac-step attacks

1. Derive W*W, determinant and trace directly from the 2 by 2 matrix, taking
   X to be the Pauli exchange matrix. Check the normalization and derive both
   eigenvalues. Check m>0 avoids band degeneracies and specifies the positive
   principal eigenphase on all real k.
2. Differentiate the spectral equation analytically, with branch and sign
   checks at k=0, k=pi/2 and k=pi. Test the rest phase, its curvature and the
   claimed ratio using the chain rule for the shared A,B calibration.
3. Examine whether an energy offset, band change, nonpositive scaling or an
   m-dependent clock can alter the conclusion, and identify which are outside
   the frozen reading class. Do not identify dispersion curvature with an
   independently measured force-response mass.
4. Derive the nonlinear shell identity without numerical fitting. Test the
   equality among spectral drift, phat/Ehat and dEhat/dphat where phat is a
   local coordinate. Inspect cos(k)=0 where the coordinate map is singular.
5. Test the global ambiguity at k and pi-k, including the exceptional fixed
   points. Determine exactly which data the nonlinear map preserves and
   loses; a shell equation alone does not imply a global phase, canonical
   coordinate map, Lorentz symmetry or physical dispersion law.
6. Derive the weak-step rest limit m=epsilon M from an integral identity for
   arctan. Obtain an explicit signed bound valid for every epsilon,M>0 and
   record the scaling used for the rest phase and curvature. Check that a
