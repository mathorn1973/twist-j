## Fixed union and actual nonzero modes

Put \(Z=R^3/X^2\) and \(H_R=R^2/X\). For \(B=1\),
\(\sqrt Z=R^{3/2}/X\le H_R\) in every inherited shell. At \(B=2\)
the threshold is \(Z\le H_R\) when \(R\le X\), exactly the range used by
the fixed successor. All these thresholds are at least one on the
inherited shells. The theorem is uniform under subsets, so assigning
overlap to the first class controls the remainder of the second class
at its own threshold. The triangle inequality pays the logarithmic
number of shells within \(O_\varepsilon(X^{2+\varepsilon})\) squared
norm, by taking an appropriately smaller input epsilon. The report's
union and \(G_7\) therefore have a fixed meaning independent of the
later choice of \(\varepsilon\).

For the second class, a shell \(R\asymp X^{7/10}\) gives
\(Z\asymp X^{1/10}\). The split-prime number theorem gives log product
\((1/2+o(1))(\log X)^2\) for the primes in
\((\log X,(\log X)^2]\), enough to cross \(Z\). The first crossing
has \(Z\le d\le Z(\log X)^2\). The two new primes in
\([\sqrt{6R/(5d)},\sqrt{7R/(5d)}]\) have size \(X^{3/10+o(1)}\).
Their product with \(d\) lies in \([6R/5,7R/5]\); their fixed relative
interval contains \(\gg\sqrt{R/d}/\log X\) split primes. Unordered
distinct pairs give \(\gg R/[d(\log X)^2]\ge X^{3/5-o(1)}\)
distinct \(r\). The construction gives \(u_{\log X}(r)=1<\sqrt Z\)
and \(u_{(\log X)^2}(r)=d\ge Z\), so all these conductors are
deleted by the second class only. Their largest primes meet both
core thresholds. At \(a=1\), the original numerator cut permits
one and the height \(X/r\asymp X^{3/10}\) lies below the frozen star
cap. The cofactor sum is nonzero after clearing odd denominators,
the \(a=1\) character coefficient approaches its inherited nonzero
limit, and the smoothing multiplier approaches one. The count is
therefore for actual nonzero modes. The corresponding first-class
count uses \(R\asymp X^{3/4}\), \(d=X^{1/8+o(1)}\), and two primes
of size \(X^{5/16+o(1)}\), giving \(X^{5/8-o(1)}\) actual conductors
by the same fixed-relative-interval count. Neither count estimates
a signed norm.

The absolute pair errors survive the same conductor mask and yield

and the correction norm are then the exact ones in \(Q_7\). Triangle
inequality against \(T\) preserves the criterion with exponent slack.
There is no target-scale estimate for a difference of the two complete
signed quadratic forms implied by this argument.

For any fixed small \(\varepsilon>0\), the deleted norm is
\(O_\varepsilon(X^{1+\varepsilon})\), whereas the norms of the leading
profile and core are \(O(X^{3/2}/(\log X)^2)\). Cauchy–Schwarz bounds
the change in the pure core energy and mixed term by
\(O_\varepsilon(X^{5/2+\varepsilon}/(\log X)^2+X^{2+2\varepsilon})\).
For fixed \(\varepsilon<1/2\) this is smaller than each fixed
\(X^3/(\log X)^A\) scale. The full leading coefficients \(1,-2,1\)
therefore carry over to \(G_7\) with the same \(C_z\). The inherited
height truncation bound is uniform in conductor masks. Its norm cost
\(O(X^{3/2}/(\log X)^4)\) preserves the leading coefficients at the
stated limited heights, without extending the every-fixed-log
cancellation assertion to that height restriction.

For the slow time observation, the taper is the convolution of the
buffered indicator with a symmetric probability supported in
\([-kh,kh]\), so \(w\ge1/2\) on \(I^+\). The original block has distance
at least \(kh\ge X/32\) from the discarded shifts. The \(\ell^2\) norm

of \(G_{\rm core}\) by \(G_7\) costs
\(O_\varepsilon(X^{1/2+\varepsilon}\sqrt K)\) pointwise by
Cauchy–Schwarz, uniformly up to both ends of the block. With fixed

smaller than the proved subexponential bound for fixed

finite-time joint profile. The synthesis keeps the original Fourier

above the prime-pair argument ceiling and below the single-prime
