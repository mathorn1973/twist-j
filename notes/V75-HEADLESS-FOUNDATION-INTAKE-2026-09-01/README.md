### 4.2 Four-phase target

The four-phase probe uses `K_(TM,4)` and proves symbolically, not by a
prefix cutoff:

\[
\pi:K_{({\rm TM},4)}\to K_{\rm TM},
\qquad
\pi(r)_m=r_m\bmod2,
\qquad
\Phi(r)=2^{r_0}\in\mathbb F_5^\times.
\]

Rotation by two preserves the hull and satisfies

\[
\pi A_2=\pi,
\qquad
\Phi(A_2r)=-\Phi(r),
\]

so full \(\Phi\) cannot descend through \(\pi\). The binary \(C_2\) quotient
does descend. The all-length finite-fibre statement must be proved using
rotation closure. The one-block reversal classification must distinguish:

- eight affine bijections, four survivors, all slope \(-1\);
- sixteen parity-preserving letter maps, exactly two survivors;
- all twenty-four alphabet permutations, exactly four survivors.

Literal multiplier inversion is the normalized rotation-zero representative.
The four affine survivors give inversion only up to the displayed global
rotation. No conclusion extends automatically to higher-block codes.
