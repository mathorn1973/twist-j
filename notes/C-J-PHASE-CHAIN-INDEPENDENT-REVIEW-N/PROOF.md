## 1. Five-root source family and quotient-envelope blindness (`#1577` vs. `#1579`/`#1581`)

### Proposition 1.1 (Five fifth-root piston preparations and common quotient)
Let $K = \mathbb Q(\zeta_5)$ with basis $B_0 = (1, \zeta_5, \zeta_5^2, \zeta_5^3)$ (`DEF-QDD-AMPLITUDE-B0`, written $(1, j, j^2, j^3)$ in the target specifications) and balanced lift $\ell(0,1,2,3,4) = (0, 1, 2, -2, -1)$ (`DEF-QDD-BALANCED-PISTON`).
1. The five fifth roots of unity $\zeta_5^k$ ($k \in \{0,1,2,3,4\}$) have unique balanced piston representations $P_k \in \mathbb F_5^4$:
   $$P_0 = (1,0,0,0),\quad P_1 = (0,1,0,0),\quad P_2 = (0,0,1,0),\quad P_3 = (0,0,0,1),\quad P_4 = (4,4,4,4),$$
   since $\beta_{\mathrm{QDD}}(P_k) = e_k$ for $k=0,1,2,3$ and $\beta_{\mathrm{QDD}}(4,4,4,4) = (-1,-1,-1,-1)$ corresponds to $-1-\zeta_5-\zeta_5^2-\zeta_5^3 = \zeta_5^4$.
2. Under the Galois automorphisms $\tau_m(\zeta_5) = \zeta_5^m$ ($m \in (\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z)^\times$), every $\alpha = \zeta_5^k$ satisfies
   $$\mathrm{aug}(\alpha) = 1,\qquad A(\alpha) = \alpha \tau_4(\alpha) = \zeta_5^{k + 4k} = 1,\qquad B(\alpha) = \tau_2(\alpha)\tau_3(\alpha) = \zeta_5^{2k + 3k} = 1,$$
   while the mixed cross-place term is
   $$C(\alpha) = \alpha \tau_3(\alpha) = \zeta_5^{4k} = \zeta_5^{-k},$$
   which takes five distinct values $(1, \zeta_5^4, \zeta_5^3, \zeta_5^2, \zeta_5)$ on $k=0,1,2,3,4$.
3. In $\mathbb F_5$, the coordinate sum of every $P_k$ is $\sum_{i=0}^3 P_{k,i} \equiv 1 \pmod 5$ (since $4+4+4+4 = 16 \equiv 1 \pmod 5$). Therefore, with common ready control $(q,r) = (0,1)$, all five native source cells $x^{(k)} = (P_k, 0, 1) \in \mathbb F_5^6$ share the identical native quotient
   $$Q(x^{(k)}) = \left(\sum_{i=0}^5 x^{(k)}_i \bmod 5,\; q,\; r\right) = (2, 0, 1).$$

### Theorem 1.2 (Quotient-compatible envelope $\mathcal E_Q$ and unchanged-port blindness)
On a cell $x = (a,b,c,d,q,r) \in \mathbb F_5^6$, let $Q(x) = (z, q, r) \in \mathbb F_5^3$ with $z = (a+b+c+d+q+r) \bmod 5$.
1. The five native generators $g_0, g_1, g_2, g_3, g_4$ on $\mathbb F_5^6$:
   $$\begin{aligned}
   g_0(a,b,c,d,q,r) &= (b, a, d, c, q, r),\\
   g_1(a,b,c,d,q,r) &= (-c, -d, -a, -b, -q, -r) \pmod 5,\\
   g_2(a,b,c,d,q,r) &= (2-c, 1+r-d, 2-a, 1-r-b, 1-q, -r) \pmod 5,\\
   g_3(a,b,c,d,q,r) &= (2-a, 1-b, 3-c, 4-d, 1-q, 1-r) \pmod 5,\\
   g_4(a,b,c,d,q,r) &= (2-a, 1-b, 3-c, 4-d, 2-q, 1-r) \pmod 5
   \end{aligned}$$
   are involutions ($g_m^2 = \mathrm{id}_{\mathbb F_5^6}$) and descend to $Q(x) = (z,q,r)$ via
   $$\bar g_0(z,q,r) = (z,q,r),\quad \bar g_1(z,q,r) = (-z,-q,-r),\quad \bar g_2(z,q,r) = (2-z,1-q,-r),$$
   $$\bar g_3(z,q,r) = (2-z,1-q,1-r),\quad \bar g_4(z,q,r) = (3-z,2-q,1-r) \pmod 5.$$
   *Proof.* For $g_2$, $(2-c)+(1+r-d)+(2-a)+(1-r-b)+(1-q)+(-r) = 7 - (a+b+c+d+q+r) \equiv 2 - z \pmod 5$; for $g_3$, $10 - (a+b+c+d) + (1-q) + (1-r) = 12 - z \equiv 2 - z \pmod 5$; for $g_4$, $10 - (a+b+c+d) + (2-q) + (1-r) = 13 - z \equiv 3 - z \pmod 5$. Involutive closure is immediate from the linear/affine definitions. $\square$
2. The reading coupling $\mathcal U_i(p, q, r) = (p, q, P_q(r))$ preserves $p$ and $q$ and replaces $r$ by $P_q(r)$, so $z' = (z - r + P_q(r)) \bmod 5$; hence $\mathcal U_i$ descends to $Q$ via $\bar{\mathcal U}(z,q,r) = ((z - r + P_q(r)) \bmod 5, q, P_q(r))$.
3. Whole-cell `CSUM` maps $(x, y) \mapsto (x, y \pm x)$ or $(x \pm y, y)$ are $\mathbb F_5$-linear coordinatewise additions/subtractions, so $Q(y \pm x) = Q(y) \pm Q(x) \pmod 5$, and whole-cell exchange $(x, y) \mapsto (y, x)$ swaps $(Q(x), Q(y))$.
4. On any multi-cell carrier $(\mathbb F_5^6)^m$, let $\mathcal E_Q$ be the groupoid of words generated by slotwise $g_0,\dots,g_4$, autonomous native steps $U(n, x) = (n+1, g_{(z_6(x) + 2\theta_n) \bmod 5}(x))$ at common $(n, \theta_n)$, slotwise $\mathcal U_i$, whole-cell `CSUM` and its inverse, and whole-cell exchange, together with any controller/apparatus updates depending solely on the $(Q(x_1),\dots,Q(x_m))$ history and common receiver memory. By induction on word length, any two initial states with equal quotient tuples $(Q(x_1),\dots,Q(x_m))$ and equal common receiver memory maintain identical quotient tuples and identical receiver states at every step of any word in $\mathcal E_Q$.
5. In particular, in the unchanged two-use apparatus (`#1577`), all $25$ ordered root pairs $(P_{k_A}, P_{k_B})$ (and specifically the $4$ pairs in $\{P_1, P_4\}^2$) start with $Q(x_A) = Q(x_B) = (2,0,1)$ and identical receiver substate $Y_0 = (0, 0, X_{\mathrm{cold}}, 0, B, B, +1, +1, (0,0))$. ## 2. Hyperplane bijection, 625-reference mask census, and three-reference code (`#1581`)

### Theorem 2.1 (Determinant polarization hyperplane bijection and exact mask census)
For row-major $P = (a,b,c,d) \in \mathbb F_5^4$ and $Y = (u,v,w,z) \in \mathbb F_5^4$, the polarized determinant form is
$$h_Y(P) = \frac{\det(P+Y) - \det(P) - \det(Y)}{2} = \frac{az + du - bw - cv}{2} \equiv 3(az + du) + 2(bw + cv) \pmod 5,$$
and $\delta_Y(P) = 1 - h_Y(P)^4 \in \{0,1\}$ satisfies $\delta_Y(P) = 1 \iff h_Y(P) = 0$.
1. Evaluating $h_Y$ on the five root pistons $P_0, P_1, P_2, P_3, P_4$ gives the linear map $\Phi : \mathbb F_5^4 \to \mathbb F_5^5$:
   $$\Phi(u,v,w,z) = \big(h_Y(P_0),\dots,h_Y(P_4)\big) = 3\big(z,\; -w,\; -v,\; u,\; v+w-u-z\big) \pmod 5.$$
   Since the first four components $(3z, -3w, -3v, 3u)$ recover $(u,v,w,z) = 2(\Phi_3, -\Phi_2, -\Phi_1, \Phi_0) \pmod 5$ uniquely and $\sum_{k=0}^4 \Phi(Y)_k = 3(z - w - v + u + v + w - u - z) = 0 \pmod 5$, $\Phi$ is a bijection from $\mathbb F_5^4$ onto the sum-zero hyperplane
   $$H_0 = \left\{\mathbf v \in \mathbb F_5^5 : \sum_{k=0}^4 v_k \equiv 0 \pmod 5\right\}.$$
2. For any binary mask $m = (m_0,m_1,m_2,m_3,m_4) \in \{0,1\}^5$ with Hamming weight $s = \sum_{k=0}^4 m_k$, a vector $\mathbf v \in H_0$ has zero-indicator mask $m$ iff $v_k = 0$ on the $s$ indices where $m_k = 1$ and the remaining $r = 5 - s$ coordinates lie in $(\mathbb F_5^\times)^r$ with sum $0 \pmod 5$. By character orthogonality over $\mathbb F_5$, the number of zero-sum tuples in $(\mathbb F_5^\times)^r$ is
   $$N(r) = \frac{1}{5} \sum_{\chi \in \widehat{\mathbb F_5}} \left(\sum_{x \in \mathbb F_5^\times} \chi(x)\right)^r = \frac{4^r + 4(-1)^r}{5}.$$
   Evaluating $N(5-s)$ for $s = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5$ gives:
   - $s=0$ ($r=5$): $N(5) = (1024 - 4)/5 = 204$ ($1$ mask, total $204$).
   - $s=1$ ($r=4$): $N(4) = (256 + 4)/5 = 52$ ($\binom{5}{1}=5$ masks, total $260$).
   - $s=2$ ($r=3$): $N(3) = (64 - 4)/5 = 12$ ($\binom{5}{2}=10$ masks, total $120$).
   - $s=3$ ($r=2$): $N(2) = (16 + 4)/5 = 4$ ($\binom{5}{3}=10$ masks, total $40$).
   - $s=4$ ($r=1$): $N(1) = (4 - 4)/5 = 0$ ($\binom{5}{4}=5$ masks, total $0$; no single nonzero element sums to $0$).
   - $s=5$ ($r=0$): $N(0) = (1 + 4)/5 = 1$ ($1$ mask $Y=0$, total $1$).
   Thus exactly $27$ of the $32$ masks in $\{0,1\}^5$ are nonempty, and $\sum_{s=0}^5 \binom{5}{s} N(5-s) = 204 + 260 + 120 + 40 + 0 + 1 = 625$.
3. **Pair separation counts:**
   - A reference $Y$ separates $P_1$ ($j$) from $P_4$ ($j^4$) iff $m_1 \ne m_4$, i.e., exactly one of $(v_1, v_4)$ is $0$ ($2$ choices) and the other lies in $\mathbb F_5^\times$ ($4$ choices), while $(v_0, v_2, v_3) \in \mathbb F_5^3$ satisfies $v_0 + v_2 + v_3 = -(v_1 + v_4)$ ($5^2 = 25$ choices). Hence $2 \times 4 \times 25 = 200$ references separate $j$ from $j^4$.
   - A reference $Y$ separates both conjugate pairs $(P_1, P_4)$ and $(P_2, P_3)$ iff $m_1 \ne m_4$ ($2 \times 4 = 8$ choices for $(v_1, v_4)$) and $m_2 \ne m_3$ ($2 \times 4 = 8$ choices for $(v_2, v_3)$), which uniquely determines $v_0 = -(v_1+v_2+v_3+v_4) \in \mathbb F_5$. Hence $8 \times 8 = 64$ references separate both conjugate pairs, partitioned into $4$ patterns $(m_1,m_2,m_3,m_4) \in \{(1,1,0,0), (1,0,1,0), (0,1,0,1), (0,0,1,1)\}$ with $16$ references each, matching the positive-imaginary-half indicators of the $4$ complex embeddings $\sigma_1, \sigma_2, \sigma_3, \sigma_4 : \mathbb Q(\zeta_5) \hookrightarrow \mathbb C$.
   - Constant masks have $s=0$ ($204$) or $s=5$ ($1$), totaling $205$. Since $h_{-Y}(P) = -h_Y(P)$, $\delta_{-Y} = \delta_Y$ identically.

### Proposition 2.2 (Three determinant-one references, binary-output lower bound, and zero-piston alias)
1. For $Y_A = (0,2,2,0)$, $Y_B = (4,2,2,0)$, $Y_C = (1,1,0,1)$:
   - $\det(Y_A) = 0 - 4 \equiv 1 \pmod 5$, $\det(Y_B) = 0 - 4 \equiv 1 \pmod 5$, $\det(Y_C) = 1 - 0 = 1 \pmod 5$.
   - By Theorem 2.1, $\Phi(Y_A) = (0,4,4,0,2) \implies \delta_{Y_A} = (1,0,0,1,0)$; $\Phi(Y_B) = (0,4,4,2,0) \implies \delta_{Y_B} = (1,0,0,0,1)$; $\Phi(Y_C) = (3,0,2,3,2) \implies \delta_{Y_C} = (0,1,0,0,0)$.
   - The weighted sum $M(P_k) = (\delta_{Y_A}(P_k) + 2\delta_{Y_B}(P_k) + 4\delta_{Y_C}(P_k)) \bmod 5$ takes the values $(3, 4, 0, 1, 2)$ for $k=0,1,2,3,4$, so $k = (M(P_k) + 2) \bmod 5$ is an exact inverse on $\{0,1,2,3,4\}$.
2. **Scope of the lower bound $3$:** Any map whose source dependence factors strictly through $m$ binary outputs $(\delta_1, \dots, \delta_m) \in \{0,1\}^m$ has at most $2^m$ distinct images. Since $2^2 = 4 < 5 \le 8 = 2^3$, $m \ge 3$ binary outputs are necessary and sufficient *within that binary-output class*. This does **not** imply that three physical reference cells are required in general (a single $\mathbb F_5$ coordinate has $5$ states, and one reference cell reused with different transforms or a non-binary reader is outside the frozen binary-flag class).
3. **Present identity phase vs. zero/absent source:** Present identity phase $k=0$ prepares $x^{(0)} = (1,0,0,0,0,1)$ with $\delta(P_0) = (1,1,0)$ and $M(P_0) = 3$. By contrast, the outside-family zero piston $P_{\mathrm{zero}} = (0,0,0,0)$ has $h_Y(0) = 0$ for every $Y$, so $\delta(0) = (1,1,1)$ and $M(0) = (1+2+4) \bmod 5 = 2 = M(P_4)$, aliasing $k=4$ ($j^4$). Thus $k=0$ is an occupied nonzero signal, and the 3-reference code on $\{P_0,\dots,P_4\}$ does not serve as an absence detector on $\mathbb F_5^4$.

## 3. Complete contact/CSUM restoration, collector state, and syntactic W-deletion (`#1579`, `#1581`)

### Theorem 3.1 (Literal contact-block conjugation and occupied-reference restoration)
On $(x, y, z_{\mathrm{coll}}, \eta) \in (\mathbb F_5^6)^3 \times \{0,1\}$, let $A_y$ act on $(x, y, \eta)$ as in `CONTACT-RECORD` [T]:
$$A_y(p_x, q_x, r_x;\; p_y, q_y, r_y;\; \eta) = \big(p_x, q_x, r_x;\; p_y, (q_y + 3\delta) \bmod 5, P_{(q_y + 3\delta) \bmod 5}(P_{q_y}(r_y));\; \eta\big),$$
where $\delta = \delta_{p_y}(p_x) \in \{0,1\}$, and let $A_y^{-1}$ be the reversed 14-leaf word. Let $\mathrm{CSUM}_{y \to z}(x, y, z, \eta) = (x, y, (z + y) \bmod 5, \eta)$.
1. For any weight $w \in \mathbb N$ and any occupied state $(x, y, z, \eta)$, the conjugated block $B_w = A_y^{-1} \circ \mathrm{CSUM}_{y \to z}^w \circ A_y$ satisfies
   $$B_w(x, y, z, \eta) = \big(x,\; y,\; (z + w \cdot y') \bmod 5,\; \eta\big),$$
   where $y' = (p_y, (q_y + 3\delta) \bmod 5, P_{(q_y+3\delta) \bmod 5}(P_{q_y}(r_y)))$, and its reversed word $B_w^{-1} = A_y^{-1} \circ (\mathrm{CSUM}_{y \to z}^{-1})^w \circ A_y$ is the exact two-sided inverse on the full carrier.
   *Proof.* $A_y$ fixes $z$ and sends $(x, y, \eta) \mapsto (x, y', \eta)$. Then $\mathrm{CSUM}_{y \to z}^w$ fixes $(x, y', \eta)$ and sends $z \mapsto (z + w y') \bmod 5$. Finally $A_y^{-1}$ fixes the updated $z$ and maps $(x, y', \eta) \mapsto (x, y, \eta)$ because $A_y^{-1} \circ A_y = \mathrm{id}$ on all of $\mathbb F_5^{12} \times \{0,1\}$. $\square$
2. When $y$ is initialized at $(Y_i, 0, 0)$ ($i \in \{A,B,C\}$), $P_{3\delta_i}(P_0(0)) = P_{3\delta_i}(0) = \delta_i$, so $y' = (Y_i, 3\delta_i \bmod 5, \delta_i)$. Starting from collector head $Z_0 = (4,0,4,2,0,0)$ and applying $B_1^{(A)}, B_2^{(B)}, B_4^{(C)}$ in sequence restores $(x, R_A, R_B, R_C, \eta)$ at the end of every block and updates the collector to
   $$Z_{\mathrm{final}} = Z_0 + 1 \cdot (Y_A, 3\delta_A, \delta_A) + 2 \cdot (Y_B, 3\delta_B, \delta_B) + 4 \cdot (Y_C, 3\delta_C, \delta_C) \pmod 5 = (1, 0, 0, 1, 3M \bmod 5, M),$$
   since $(4,0,4,2) + (0,2,2,0) + 2(4,2,2,0) + 4(1,1,0,1) = (16, 10, 10, 6) \equiv (1,0,0,1) \pmod 5$ and $1\delta_A + 2\delta_B + 4\delta_C \equiv M \pmod 5$.
3. **Syntactic $W_b$-deletion control:** Deleting the four `W` leaves from $A_y$ leaves $(\mathrm{Uy}, \mathrm{ey}, \mathrm{dy}, \mathrm{bx}, \mathrm{by}, \mathrm{dy}, \mathrm{ey}, \mathrm{by}, \mathrm{bx}, \mathrm{Uy})$. Because $\tau_y^{-1} I_b \tau_y I_b$ maps $q_y \mapsto -(-(q_y - 1) + 1) = q_y - 2 \equiv q_y + 3 \pmod 5$ while fixing $x, p_y, r_y, \eta$ unconditionally, the $W$-deleted word acts identically to $\delta = 1$ for every input $x$. Consequently:
   - In `#1579`, deleting `W` sends $y_0 = (1,0,0,1,0,0) \mapsto (1,0,0,1,3,1)$ on both lanes for all $4$ input pairs, matching the $(j,j)$ trajectory.
   - In `#1581`, deleting `W` in $A_y$ and its paired inverse in each block sets $\delta_A = \delta_B = \delta_C = 1$ unconditionally, producing $M = (1+2+4) \bmod 5 = 2$ on both collector heads for all $25$ input pairs, matching the $(j^4, j^4)$ trajectory.

### Proposition 4.3 (Energy conservation, prospective bound, and explicit operational boundaries)
1. Because $F$ is an exact quadratic-energy-preserving involution conjugate (`wave_energy(u,v) + 2||tapes||^2` is invariant under `F_step`) and each accepted contact shifts $W \leftarrow W + \Delta E$, $b \leftarrow b - \Delta E$, every boundary satisfies $E(X) = W$ and $b + W = B$.

   $$E_{\mathrm{final}} \le \left(\sqrt{e_A} + \sqrt{e_B}\right)^2 \le 4 \max(e_A, e_B).$$
