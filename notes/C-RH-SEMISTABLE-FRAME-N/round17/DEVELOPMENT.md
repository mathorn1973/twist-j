## Concentration consequence on the full family

**Candidate-D.** Decompose $F=\oplus_b F_b$ by both level-prime labels. Let $J_{u,b}=J_u\cap F_b$ and $\kappa=R^{-1}\sum_u\dim\ker P_u$. By the invariance of all $J_u$ under the joint projectors, a largest maximizing subspace splits across the $F_b$ (round 14, Section 1). Every twist preserves that maximizer, so its cell dimensions repeat along each joint orbit. In particular, if $b_O$ is a representative in orbit $O$,
$$
\epsilon(P)=\kappa+\sum_O |O|\max_{W\subset F_{b_O}}
\left[\frac{\sum_u\dim(J_{u,b_O}\cap W)}R-\frac{\dim W}D\right].
$$
The stabilizer of $b_O$ poses no choice problem: the largest maximizing subspace in that one cell is unique and invariant. The normalized denominators are the original $R,D$. Every representative has $\dim F_{b_O}\le D/T$, and all positive cell contributions to the displayed formula have integer numerators divisible by $T$ after multiplication by $\operatorname{lcm}(R,D)$. This reduces the search to one member per orbit while retaining arbitrary partial word directions inside that member. Doubling all cells by an exact repetition of the same unbalanced model does not reduce its normalized internal defect; the orbit identity alone supplies no exponential bound.

The repeated factor is an actual matrix algebra on the common target. Within an orbit choose any multiplier $V_b$ transporting the base cell to $b$ and use the identity for the base cell. Let $e$ be its projector. On the orbit set $E_{bc}=V_b e V_c^*$. Then $E_{bc}^*=E_{cb}$ and $E_{bc}E_{df}=\delta_{cd}E_{bf}$ by orthogonality of the cell ranges. The diagonal units sum to the orbit projector. Every $E_{bc}$ preserves each image. Identifying $v\in F_{b_O}$ in the $b$-th copy with $V_bv$ gives
$$
F_O\simeq\mathbb C^{|O|}\otimes F_{b_O},\qquad
J_u\cap F_O\simeq\mathbb C^{|O|}\otimes J_{u,b_O}.
$$
This construction needs no coherent group law among the chosen lifts. The canonical maximizing subspace has the same tensor form in this orbit; an arbitrary $W\subset F_{b_O}$ remains possible. Since $|O|\le D$ and $\operatorname{lcm}(R,D)\ge R$, the resulting spacing $T/\operatorname{lcm}(R,D)$ is at most $D/R$. Thus in the large-redundancy regime it cannot round the square-root bound to zero.

Acting by $t$ on this line and identically on its orthogonal complement gives an $F$-defined section of the determinant $H_u\to T$. In particular the image on finite adeles surjects onto $T(\mathbb A_{F,f})$. The finite class set for columns is $H_u(F)\backslash H_u(\mathbb A_{F,f})/K_u$. The map to $C$ is $h\mapsto[\det(hx_u)]$ and is well-defined since $x_u^{-1}K_ux_u\subset K_2$. Translate quotient Haar measure on $H_u(F)\backslash H_u(\mathbb A_{F,f})$ by any lift of an element of $C$. The quotient measure is finite by the finite class decomposition; the usual measure of the image of $hK_u$ is proportional to $1/|H_u(F)\cap hK_uh^{-1}|$. In the full geometric realization $C$ in fact labels the entire ambient finite class set. First $\det K_2=U_0$. There is an additional exact transport at the two split levels. Right translation by a lift of the smaller scalar $zI_k$ normalizes the generic unipotent and its character. \frac{\dim\ker(P_u|_{E_{u,c}})}{\dim E_{u,c}}
\le \min\{1,e_0 [C:B^k]\},\qquad \dim E_{u,c}=n_u/|C|.

Indeed the numerator repeated $|B^k|$ times is bounded by the full kernel, which has dimension at most $e_0n_u$. In particular this applies to the entire 2-primary quotient because the actual $n_G=k+m-1$ is odd. If a remaining character had a trace larger than the target allowance, it would falsify that allowance for the full periods. No such trace is established here.

There is a **mixed-word** strengthening on these same actual ambient classes. Fix $0<\delta<2$ and $a>0$. For all sufficiently large family parameters, simultaneously for every nonempty subset $I$ of at most $\Gamma^{2-\delta}$ words and every $c\in C$, the concatenation
$$

 \qquad (v_u)\longmapsto\sum_{u\in I} A_u v_u
$$
has rank at least $(1-\exp(-afL))\sum_{u\in I}n_u/|C|$. Zero-dimensional domains are allowed. Here each $A_u$ is the normalized period map and the nonzero wordwise scalar normalizations leave all concatenation ranks unchanged.

Indeed each diagonal error and each normalized cross Gram $A_u^*A_v$ respects the component partition. Its restrictions at two components differing by an element of $B^k$ are conjugate by the wordwise unitaries coming from the smaller scalar lifts; for distinct words this is a left and a right unitary change. Thus its squared Hilbert–Schmidt norm at $c$ is at most its full squared norm divided by $|B^k|$. $$
\frac{\dim\ker A_{I,c}}{M_I/|C|}
\le\frac{\|A_{I,c}^*A_{I,c}-I\|_{HS}^2}{M_I/|C|}
\le [C:B^k]\left(e_F+(1+\eta_0)\frac{M_I^2-\sum_{u\in I}n_u^2}{D_*M_I}\right).

The size proviso gives $n_u/D_*\le C_0/\Gamma^2$. The parenthesis is therefore at most $e_F+(1+\eta_0)C_0\Gamma^{-\delta}$. The class-index and growth estimates above, with bounded $\eta_0$, prove the assertion for each fixed $\delta,a$. \sum_{u\in I}\dim (A_u|_{E_{u,c}})^{-1}(W)
\le \dim W+\exp(-afL) M_I/|C|.
$$
This follows by applying the concatenation to the direct sum of the indicated preimages. The second factor multiplies by an actual indicator, that of the quotient determinant class with evaluations $\chi(c)=\theta(\chi)$. It commutes with $p_\alpha$. Inversion of the character convention merely relabels $\theta$.

In every original word the dimension of the corresponding source cell is **exactly** $n_u a_\alpha/|H_\alpha|$. The trace of a convolution followed by any coordinate mask equals its identity coefficient times the masked dimension, by the diagonal entries of the regular representation. Divide the result for $p_\alpha$ by that for the original unipotent projector. The ratio is the same $a_\alpha$ on every mask. The original domain has uniform mask mass by the component count above. Commutation for this particular mask turns the trace into the asserted joint dimension. For the target use the absolute diagonal estimate of the opposition separately at the *individual* admissible replacement $p_\alpha$, with the opposite level prime retained. The orthonormal diagonal on its range integrates on each indicated component mask to $d_{\alpha,\theta}=\dim Q_{\alpha,\theta}F$, since the mask commutes with its range projector. Haar mass is uniform over $\widehat H_\alpha$. The main term is $a_\alpha D_*/|H_\alpha|$ and the sum of absolute errors over these masks is at most $(A_2+2)E_1 a_\alpha D_*+A_1E_2\dim p_\alpha F$, in the notation of the opposition. Summing over $\alpha$ therefore gives
$$
\sum_{\alpha,\theta}|d_{\alpha,\theta}-a_\alpha D_*/|H_\alpha||\le B_0.
$$
The total variation after normalization by $D$ is at most $B_0/D$ since $|D-D_*|\le B_0$. ## Simultaneous independent arithmetic columns

For any nonempty collection $I$ with positive total retained dimension, put $m=\sum_{u\in I}n_u$, $s=\sum_{u\in I}s_u$ and
$$
v=e m+(1+h)(m^2-\sum_{u\in I}n_u^2)/D_*.
$$
Concatenate the normalized full maps restricted to the retained isometric source embeddings, before projecting away any rows. Call its positive Gram matrix $G$. Every off-diagonal squared Hilbert–Schmidt norm is bounded by its uncompressed value, including both orders of the pair. The sum of the squared diagonal errors is at most $em$ by orthogonal compression. Consequently
$$
\|G-I_s\|_{HS}^2\le v,\qquad
|\operatorname{tr}G-s|\le\sqrt{sem}.
$$
Indeed only the diagonal blocks contribute to the trace. Null directions each cost one unit of squared error. Also $(\operatorname{tr}G)^2\le\operatorname{rank}(G)\operatorname{tr}(G^2)$ and $\operatorname{tr}(G^2)=s+2\operatorname{tr}(G-I_s)+\|G-I_s\|_{HS}^2$. Hence a simultaneous rank lower bound for the actual arithmetic images on this cut is
$$
L(I)=\max\left(0,\left\lceil\max\left(s-v,
\frac{(s-\sqrt{sem})_+^2}{s+2\sqrt{sem}+v}\right)\right\rceil-b\right).
$$
For $s=0$ use zero. The single common projection loses at most $b$ dimensions for the whole concatenation. Wordwise column changes, nonzero scalars, a common invertible row change, conjugation if used, and extension from the coefficient field preserve the rank of every concatenation. This gives $\dim_L\sum_{u\in I} J_u\ge L(I)$ directly for the arithmetic maps, without evaluating a unit in a local theta expansion.

Take any nonnegative integer quotas $q_u$ satisfying $\sum_{u\in I}q_u\le L(I)$ for every nonempty $I$. Then one can simultaneously choose $q_u$ columns from any fixed coefficient-field matrix of each actual word so that their union is independent. To prove it, make $q_u$ copies of the finite set of columns of word $u$. Each collection of copies spans a space of dimension at least the number of copies by the subset bounds. Induct on the number of copies. If a proper nonempty subcollection is tight, first select its independent representatives. Quotient by their span. Applying the original inequalities to unions with the tight collection gives the inequalities for all remaining copies in the quotient. If there is no proper tight subcollection, choose any nonzero member of one copy, quotient by its line, and delete that copy. Every nonempty remaining collection had at least one unit of slack before quotienting. The singleton case follows directly. Lifting the chosen representatives gives the desired columns. Their independence gives one nonzero mixed maximal minor of the selected columns whenever at least two quotas are positive. The coordinates selected by this argument can depend on the instance. This is a minor of an ordinary concatenation, with no claim that the same indices give a large auxiliary blow-up minor.

For example, with uncut domains put $e<1$, $a=(1-\sqrt e)^2$, $c=(1+\sqrt e)^2$, and let $N=\sum_u n_u$. For each subset before row projection,
$$
\dim\sum_{u\in I}\operatorname{im}P_u
\ \ge\ \frac{a m}{c+(1+h)m/D_*}
\ \ge\ \frac{a m}{c+(1+h)N/D_*}.
$$
Thus the full period family admits independent columns of all word quotas $\lfloor a n_u/(c+(1+h)N/D_*)\rfloor$ simultaneously. For the entire family the *realized* span has
$$
\frac{D-\dim\sum_u\operatorname{im}P_u}{D}
\le 1-\frac{(1-\sqrt e)^2}{(1+\eta_1)[1+h+(1+\sqrt e)^2D_*/N]}.
$$
Here $D\le(1+\eta_1)D_*$. Under the separate comparison inputs [the source establishes](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GlobalSchurBounds/TopDensityNGeSixteen.lean#L538-L562) $N\ge D_*/\mathrm{sg}$, so $D_*/N$ in this upper bound may be replaced by $\mathrm{sg}$. A common row cut costs at most $b$ in total independence, after an independent family is selected. Source cuts require the earlier compressed estimate or an independent accounting of their loss.

For the actual arithmetic cut one can find sizable mixed minors without requiring its source or target projectors to preserve determinant classes. For any $I$ let $M=\sum_{u\in I} n_u$, $S=\sum_{u\in I}s_u$ and assume $M>0$. The unrestricted concatenation has nullity at most $M(e_F+(1+\eta_0)M/D_*)$. Restricting to the retained columns does not increase this absolute nullity. Projecting to the common retained rows costs at most $b$ additional dimensions once. The actual common block comparison consequently yields
$$
\operatorname{rank}_L[B_k(u)]_{u\in I}
\ge S-M(e_F+(1+\eta_0)M/D_*)-b.
$$
For example put $v=C_0/\Gamma^2$, $t=\sqrt{b/D_*+v}$. If the full family has mass at least $tD_*$, add words in any order until $M\ge tD_*$. Since every $n_u\le v D_*$, the first crossing satisfies $t\le M/D_*\le t+v$, and $b/M\le t$. If the chosen words satisfy $S\ge(1-d)M$, the arithmetic rank is at least
$$
 M\big[1-d-e_F-(1+\eta_0)(t+v)-t\big].
$$
Whenever $d,e_F,t,v$ tend to zero under the cut and size hypotheses this is a near-injective ordinary concatenation. Its rank is of order $tD_*$ if $t\to0$, since $v\le t^2$. Also $M/\max_u n_u\ge t/v\ge1/\sqrt v\to\infty$. Any rank-size minor must therefore use columns from many distinct words. An aggregate loss $\sum_u(n_u-s_u)\le d_{\rm all}\sum_u n_u$ suffices for the selection: when $0<d_{\rm all}<1$ discard words for which $n_u-s_u>\sqrt{d_{\rm all}}\,n_u$. Their total full mass is at most $\sqrt{d_{\rm all}}\sum_u n_u$. The remainder satisfies the per-word bound with $d=\sqrt{d_{\rm all}}$. For $d_{\rm all}=0$ retain all words. Whenever the remaining full mass is at least $tD_*$, the greedy construction applies entirely to the remaining words. In the standing high-redundancy regime with vanishing aggregate cuts this condition holds eventually. No per-word loss estimate is then needed. This proves mixed field nonvanishing for a substantive arithmetic cut; the rank scale here is smaller than the full target and the matrix is an ordinary concatenation.
