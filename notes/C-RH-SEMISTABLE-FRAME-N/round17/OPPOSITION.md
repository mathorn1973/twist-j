$$T=\sum_{u\in S}(s_u-e_u\sqrt{s_u})_+,\qquad H=\sum_{u\in S}(\sqrt{s_u}+e_u)^2+\sum_{u\ne v\in S}h_{uv}.$$

$$L_S=\max(0,\lceil T^2/H\rceil-b).$$

## Why small heights do not turn a frame estimate into semistability

A small archimedean entry height gives no larger universal gap. In dimension two, take $m+1$ copies of the first primitive coordinate vector and $m-1$ of the second, with $m\ge2$. Each nonzero Plücker coordinate in the indicated bases has absolute value one. Over any field, choose echelon pivots $P$ for a $w$-plane $W$. Projection to the pivot coordinates is injective on $W$ and maps $W\cap I_u$ into the coordinate space supported on $P\cap I_u$. Thus $\sum_u\dim(W\cap I_u)\le\sum_{y\in P}K_y$, where $K_y$ counts the words containing coordinate $y$. The plane spanned by the coordinates with $K_y>s$ attains the maximum. Consequently round 9's exact value $(1-\rho)/(2\sqrt{s})$ persists over every residue field and extension. For $m=2t^2$, $s=t^2$, this is $1/(4t)$ with $D/R=1/t^2$. ### All projections in the one-prime noncommuting algebra

**Candidate-D under the same one-prime premises.** Let $A=\widehat C$, let $L$ be the finite set of generic labels at the opened level prime, and use their actual determinant twist action. Both full row and full word source carry representations of the finite crossed product $\mathcal B=\mathbb C^L\rtimes A$. The point idempotents of $\mathbb C^L$ act as generic central Hecke projectors and $\chi\in A$ acts by the respective determinant multiplier. These are genuine representations of the global character group: no compatible choices of lifts of local characters are needed here. The relations $M_\chi p_\alpha M_\chi^{-1}=p_{\chi\alpha}$ follow from the normalized finite Hecke lifts on each model. The actual $P_u$ intertwines both sets of generators. In particular its kernel is a subrepresentation; an individual source or target representation may have a kernel as a representation of the abstract algebra.

For an orbit $O$, take $\alpha\in O$ and $H=\mathrm{Stab}_A(\alpha)$. The corner $p_\alpha\mathcal Bp_\alpha$ is $\mathbb C[H]$: only a character fixing $\alpha$ has a nonzero corner, and these elements form its group basis. Translating this corner among the labels with a choice of representatives of $A/H$ gives matrix units and identifies $p_O\mathcal B$ with $M_{|O|}(\mathbb C[H])$. Since $A$ is abelian, $H$ fixes all labels in $O$. For $\theta\in\widehat H$ its central summand projection is

$$ Z_{O,\theta}=p_O\frac1{|H|}\sum_{\chi\in H}\overline{\theta(\chi)}M_\chi. $$

On the row and column models the character average is the component mask for the union of $c$ satisfying $\chi(c)=\theta(\chi)$ for all $\chi\in H$. Thus each of these central blocks is a union of the preceding joint cells. Its matrix algebra has degree $|O|$.

Let $Q=Q^*=Q^2\in\mathcal B$. In each such central summand $Z$ its matrix rank divided by the matrix degree is a number $\tau_Z\in[0,1]$. Every copy of the irreducible matrix representation has this same rank fraction. Write $n_Z,k_Z,d_Z$ for total column, kernel and row dimensions in $Z$. Wordwise intertwining gives $P_u^{-1}(\mathrm{im}\,Q_{\rm row})=\mathrm{im}\,Q_u\oplus(\ker P_u\cap\ker Q_u)$. Therefore

$$\Phi(\mathrm{im}\,Q_{\rm row})=\kappa+\sum_Z\tau_Z\left((n_Z-k_Z)/R-d_Z/D\right).$$

This is maximized over **every orthogonal projection in the noncommuting algebra** by taking $Q$ to be the sum of all central $Z$ with positive coefficient. The estimate for unions of joint cells proves the same bound $\kappa+B_0/D$ with no dimension, character-count or matrix-degree factor. It includes arbitrary subspaces in the representation matrix factor selected by such projections. A canonical maximizer over *all* subspaces, by contrast, is invariant under $\mathcal B$; in a block $\mathbb C^{|O|}\otimes U$ it can be $\mathbb C^{|O|}\otimes W$ for arbitrary $W\subseteq U$. The established statement does not cover these arbitrary multiplicity-space subspaces.

For a model meeting the abstract noncommuting and individual kernel-density conditions, take $\mathbb C^{C_h}\otimes\mathbb C^Y$ with uniform finite measure on a cyclic group of order $h\ge2$ times the round 9 coordinate set. Use maps $I_h\otimes A_u$ with the round 9 coordinate maps. Component masks are coordinate projectors on the cyclic factor; determinant characters multiply pointwise. Generic-label analogues are its individual Fourier-line projections. The multipliers permute Fourier lines transitively, and the resulting algebra is $M_h(\mathbb C)$ on that factor. Every Fourier-line projection tensored with identity has exactly constant kernel diagonal, because its normalized Fourier vector has equal coordinate absolute values. Every source and target has the same normalized marginal on this matrix algebra. All word maps remain isometries and every sharp off-diagonal bound is preserved: the squared Hilbert–Schmidt norms and dimension expressions both multiply by $h$. Applying the multiplicity-space argument above to a largest source maximizing subspace gives exactly the same positive defect as the original round 9 family. This statement tests the abstract inference only and does not identify those matrices with an automorphic family.

### Some determinant traces vanish exactly

**Candidate-D for the full row model.** Right translation by a finite central adele $zI_r$, $z\in T(\mathbb A_{F,f})$, commutes with the finite level convolution and the infinite projector. It is a unitary of $HH$. Conjugation changes $M_\chi$ by the scalar $\chi(z^r)$, up to inversion according to the translation convention. Trace invariance forces $\operatorname{tr}_{HH}M_\chi=0$ whenever $\chi^r\ne1$. Equivalently $d_c$ is constant on each coset of $C^r$. Since $r=k+m-1$ is odd, every nontrivial quadratic character has trace zero. At either split level prime, scalar matrices in a smaller $\mathrm{GL}_k$ factor also act by unitary right translations preserving its generic column and row corner: in the intermediate-level model they normalize the Bessel character and commute with the generic unipotent idempotent. Their determinant shifts supply the $k$th powers of the corresponding local units on component classes. Thus a nonzero trace must also have local restriction of order dividing $k$ at each selected residual factor. At these factors its order divides $\gcd(r,k)=\gcd(m-1,k)$. This leaves the other allowed residual characters, for which the absolute-kernel estimate applies. In particular the argument does not deduce exact equality of all target component dimensions without a further statement that the indicated shifts generate $C$.

### Cross-review of uniform relative multiplicity balance

**Candidate-D under the same refined counting and standing family premises.** For a single label $\alpha$ of positive regular source weight $a_\alpha$, the determinant multipliers in its stabilizer $H_\alpha\subseteq\widehat C$ commute with $p_\alpha$. Their character masks partition the physical determinant classes into $h_\alpha=|H_\alpha|$ equal Haar-mass fibres. Apply the refined kernel estimate to the single admissible $p_\alpha$, now integrating on one such fibre; all other determinant masks need not commute with it. With $b_1=(A_2+2)E_1$ and $b_2=A_1E_2$, the total estimate first gives $D_\alpha\le (1+b_1)a_\alpha D_* /(1-b_2)$ if $b_2<1$. Hence the absolute error in the fibre dimension relative to $a_\alpha D_*/h_\alpha$ is bounded, as a fraction of that mass, by $h_\alpha(b_1+b_2)/(1-b_2)$. The same constant-diagonal regular-fibre trace argument gives exact source weight $a_\alpha/h_\alpha$. The factor $1/a_\alpha$ cancels; small source weight therefore poses no extra hypothesis.

Under [the finite-unramified, self-dual and level conditions audited in the source note](SOURCE.md), $\det K_2$ is the full principal kernel $U_0$. At nonsplit unramified places the reduced Hermitian form has a nonisotropic vector, including in residue characteristic two by the nondegenerate trace pairing, and an integral lift splits off a unit-norm line (norm a unit). On this line an arbitrary local norm-one unit supplies its determinant. At split level places the analogous action by a principal unit and its paired inverse is congruent to identity on the lattice. Thus $|C|=h_T|A|/|\operatorname{red}\mu(E)|$, with $\log|C|=O(fL)$ by the ideal-class bound, the standing discriminant estimate and $\log q_v=O(L)$. The standing $k\ge cL$ and $k\to\infty$ imply $k\log k/L\to\infty$, while $r\ge k$ and $r^2\ge r\log r$. Therefore the factor $h_\alpha\le |C|$ is absorbed uniformly by the two exponentials, with relative error $O(\exp(-c' rf\log r))$. In particular the target multiplicity is positive for every simple type of positive source weight. Relative error for a projection in the algebra is a nonnegative weighted average of its simple-type relative errors, hence obeys the same bound. Replacing $D_*$ by $D$ uses the same conclusion for identity and normalization by a factor tending to one.

In one simple block $\mathbb C^s\otimes M$, the orthogonal projections covered by this latter assertion are $Q\otimes I_M$. A subspace invariant under the whole algebra has projection $I_s\otimes P_W$ with $W\le M$. The only projections common to these two collections in a simple block are zero and identity. Exact occurrence of a source type in the target, even with relative multiplicity balance, does not give a lower bound on the ranks of periods on such a residual direction $W$.
