## 1. A finite set of split places separates the Arthur parameters

Let $E/F$ be the CM extension and $G$ the ambient unitary group of dimension $n_G=k+2r_S+1$. Let $\mathcal B$ be the finite set of discrete irreducible constituents contributing to $HH$. Under round 13 each $\pi\in\mathcal B$ is cuspidal, occurs once and has an assigned generic elliptic global Arthur parameter $\psi(\pi)=\sum_i\phi_i\boxtimes S_1$. The $\phi_i$ are pairwise distinct conjugate self-dual cuspidal representations of general linear groups over $E$. Write $\mathcal T_\psi=\boxplus_i\phi_i$ for their isobaric sum. [Round 13, Sections 1–2](../round13/PROOF.md) · [Chen–Zou, §2.1 and Theorems 2.1 and 2.6, pp. 4775–4780](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/51452#page=7).

Choose a finite set of excluded places containing those over $g$, the exceptional places in Chen–Zou's localization for this finite family, the finite ramification sets of its constituents and factors, and any bad places of the lattice or of the local identifications. Any prescribed finite set of further exclusions can be added. At a split place outside this set, $G(F_v)\simeq\mathrm{GL}_{n_G}(F_v)$ and the ambient level factor $K_v$ is hyperspecial. The actual row cut in each $\pi$ contains only the spherical factor at $v$. The spherical Hecke character at $v$ therefore determines the local normalized Satake parameter. By Chen–Zou, this gives the local component of $\mathcal T_\psi$ at the chosen place of $E$ over $v$. Conjugate self-duality gives the component at the other place. The decomposition of the row projection used here is the one from round 11, including the actual finite convolution and the actual real row vector. [Chen–Zou, Theorem 2.1 and (2.4), p. 4777](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/51452#page=9) · [Round 11, Section 4](../round11/PROOF.md#4-from-the-geometric-row-space-to-the-local-satake-bound).

If two rows have equal spherical Hecke characters at every such split place, their isobaric transfers agree at all but finitely many degree-one places of $E/F$. Ramakrishnan's Theorem A applies because $E/F$ is quadratic and hence normal. It gives equality of the isobaric transfers, and uniqueness of the cuspidal isobaric factors gives equality of the assigned Arthur parameters. The isobaric version matters when the transfer has several cuspidal factors. The degree-one hypothesis matters because the omitted inert places of $E$ generally form an infinite set. [Ramakrishnan, *A Theorem on* $\mathrm{GL}(n)$ *à la Tchebotarev*, Theorem A, pp. 1–2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.08429#page=1).

Consequently one can select a finite set of allowed split places that separates the distinct parameters occurring in $HH$. For each unordered pair of distinct parameters take one place and one spherical Hecke element with different eigenvalues. The algebra of their actions on $HH$ contains the orthogonal projector onto each Arthur-parameter block. Indeed, for a given parameter one can multiply, over all other parameters, a separating operator minus its other eigenvalue, divided by the eigenvalue difference. This gives the indicator function of the given parameter on the finite spectrum.

Two constituents assigned the same $\psi$ give identical characters at all the chosen good places. Their individual multiplicity one concerns the number of copies of each representation. Separation inside one packet is a local uniqueness question at the remaining places for the actual fixed infinity type and finite level. At the completely split $g$ places, local general linear LLC fixes the factor from the packet parameter. At a nonsplit place with hyperspecial spherical uniqueness, the same conclusion follows from the spherical parameter. The source describes a full lattice stabilizer off $g$. This round leaves open the required local uniqueness check at any exceptional nonsplit places. It exhibits no two distinct packet members in the actual $HH$. The generic packet formula expresses the global selection as a product of local component characters. For example, when two fixed-level local replacements on the same forms have inverse character ratios on the global component group, their simultaneous replacement preserves that condition. The availability of such replacements at the actual exceptional places needs separate proof. [Chen–Zou, §2.2, (2.5), and §2.3, pp. 4778–4780](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/51452#page=10).

## 2. The two level-prime decompositions

Under the round 8 hypotheses, at each $g_j$ the actual column space and the actual row space have the action of the finite generic corner used there. Its central projectors intertwine with $P_u$ by integral frame lifts supported at $g_j$ and absolute folding. The target projectors at $g_0$ and $g_1$ commute, since their actions are at distinct places. Their products give joint orthogonal projectors, and every $\operatorname{im}P_u$ is invariant under each product. The corresponding assertions concern the central block cuts even when a block or a complete word image is zero. The one-prime source and target balance estimates are marginal estimates. A bound on their joint weights is a separate obligation. Good-place spherical operators commute on $HH$ with these two level-prime cuts. Their action on individual word images requires an additional argument. [Candidate proof, Sections 2.2–2.3](../PROOF.md#22-exact-source-balance-for-either-one-prime-algebra).

## 4. Exact preservation criterion after folding

Fix a valid transversal $D_t$ for a word. Let $\mathcal S_u$ be the span of $J_u(C_u)$ and all its finite right translates at the selected good places. Define $\mathsf F_u J(x)=\sum_{\delta\in D_t}J(\delta x)$ on this space, as a class in $L^2(G(F)\backslash G(\mathbb A_F))$. The original `InHH` conditions give absolute summability for every $x$ and square integrability for $J_u(T)$. For a translate by $a$, absolute summability follows by evaluation at $xa$, and its sum is the right translate of the original square-integrable fold. These facts persist under finite linear combinations. On right $K_v$-invariant seeds, each spherical Hecke operator uses a finite right-coset sum, so its action lies in $\mathcal S_u$ and commutes with $\mathsf F_u$. The Hecke action preserves $HH$ because it preserves the original good-place level and commutes with the finite level cut at other places and the real row projection. [PoincareInHH.lean, lines 172–210](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/SiegelLatticeTheory/HaarTransversals/PoincareInHH.lean#L172-L210) · [RowsDef.lean, lines 19–20 and 31–47](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/SiegelLatticeTheory/HaarTransversals/RowsDef.lean#L19-L47).

Since $P_u=\mathsf F_u J_u$, the exact criterion for a finite spherical Hecke operator $A$ is

$$
A\operatorname{im}P_u\subseteq\operatorname{im}P_u
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
A J_u(C_u)\subseteq J_u(C_u)+\ker(\mathsf F_u|_{\mathcal S_u}).
$$

If $AP_uT=P_uT'$, then $A J_u(T)-J_u(T')$ has zero fold. Conversely, applying $\mathsf F_u$ to the seed containment gives the image containment. Establishing the containment modulo this kernel for a separating algebra would give the desired packet block preservation. The Jacquet–Rallis relative Satake fundamental lemma identifies orbital integrals of matching test functions in its adjacent rank setting. That identity supplies no map on the present fixed seed quotient. [Leslie, *A fundamental lemma for the spherical Hecke algebra*, Theorem 1.1](https://spencerleslie.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/jr-fundamental-lemma-for-hecke-algebra.pdf#page=2).
