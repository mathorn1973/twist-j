# Which Hecke symmetries preserve the actual period images?

**NON-CANONICAL. No authority. No Canon status change.**

**Candidate-D.** On the full geometric row space, a finite graph determines exactly which operators in the represented diagonal Hecke algebra preserve every period image. Its vertices record both of the round 8 residue-prime labels and the generic Arthur parameter. Its edge weights depend on the actual period maps. A second result bounds the error in testing only subspaces that split under a proposed decomposition, even when the decomposition fails the exact symmetry test.

Fix one genuine instance and the common identification of its concrete Poincare maps with $P_u:C_u\to F$, where $F=HH$. Work over $\mathbb C$. Put $J_u=\operatorname{im}P_u$, $D=\dim F>0$, $R=\sum_u\dim C_u>0$, and $\kappa=R^{-1}\sum_u\dim\ker P_u$. For $W\le F$, the concentration functional and its maximum are

$$
\Phi(W)=\kappa+\frac1R\sum_u\dim(J_u\cap W)-\frac{\dim W}{D},
\qquad \epsilon(P)=\max_{W\le F}\Phi(W). \tag{14.1}
$$

Indeed $P_u^{-1}(W)$ maps onto $J_u\cap W$ with kernel $\ker P_u$. In the adjoint-rank formulation the tested subspace is $V=W^\perp$. Round 12 allows the same calculation using its fixed, unaveraged maps since their images equal $J_u$. The earlier exact splitting for preserved blocks and the earlier matrix-rank certificates continue to apply. [Round 8, §§2.3, 3 and 5](../PROOF.md) · [Round 12, §§6–7](../round12/PROOF.md)

## 1. Canonical maximizers and the common stabilizer

Let $\mathcal E=\{X\in\operatorname{End}(F):XJ_u\subseteq J_u\text{ for all }u\}$. This is the full algebra preserving the labeled images. It includes operators that need not preserve their orthogonal complements.

**Candidate-D.** There is a unique largest and a unique smallest maximizer of (14.1), ordered by inclusion. Both are invariant under $\mathcal E$. To see this, the possible values of (14.1) form a nonempty finite set. For any $W,W'$ and fixed $J$, the sum of $J\cap W$ and $J\cap W'$ is contained in $J\cap(W+W')$, and their intersection is $J\cap W\cap W'$. The dimension formula gives

$$
\Phi(W)+\Phi(W')\leq\Phi(W+W')+\Phi(W\cap W').
$$

It follows that the sum and intersection of two maximizers are maximizers. Finite dimension then gives the largest and smallest by taking the sum and intersection of all maximizers. Every invertible element of $\mathcal E$ carries each $J_u$ onto itself and permutes the maximizers. It fixes both canonical ones. For arbitrary $X\in\mathcal E$, choose $z\in\mathbb C$ with $X+zI$ invertible. Invariance under $X+zI$ gives invariance under $X$. On taking orthogonal complements, the corresponding canonical maximizers in the adjoint-rank problem are invariant under $\mathcal E^*$.

There is an exact description from the column relations. Restrict each source to $C_u^\circ=(\ker P_u)^\perp$ and let $T:\bigoplus_u C_u^\circ\to S=\sum_u J_u$ be the sum of the restricted maps. Write $\mathcal R=\ker T=\ker(T^*T)$. On $S$, the full image-preserving algebra is isomorphic to

$$
\{\,B=\operatorname{diag}(B_u): B_u\in\operatorname{End}(C_u^\circ),
                         \ B\mathcal R\subseteq\mathcal R\,\}.
$$

Given $X$ on $S$, the isomorphisms $P_u:C_u^\circ\to J_u$ give the unique lifts $B_u$ with $XP_u=P_uB_u$. Conversely, preserving $\mathcal R$ makes $X(Tv)=TBv$ well defined. On $F=S\oplus S^\perp$ one may additionally choose arbitrary maps $S^\perp\to S$ and $S^\perp\to S^\perp$.

Let $\Pi_u$ be orthogonal projection onto $J_u$. The largest adjoint-closed subalgebra of $\mathcal E$ is the commutant $\{\Pi_u:u\}'$. Indeed preservation of $J_u$ by both $X$ and $X^*$ is equivalent to commutation with $\Pi_u$. The algebra generated by the projections themselves can move one image out of another. These elementary descriptions do not by themselves determine the dimension of the actual stabilizer.

For any known unital adjoint-closed subalgebra $\mathcal A\subseteq\mathcal E$, write its representation as $F=\bigoplus_j V_j\otimes M_j$, where $\mathcal A$ acts by the full matrix algebra on each irreducible $V_j$ and by the identity on $M_j$. Its invariant subspaces have the form $\bigoplus_j V_j\otimes L_j$. Indeed the central projections isolate the summands, and the matrix units in a summand force the same subspace $L_j$ on every basis coordinate of $V_j$. Apply this to each $J_u$, with multiplicity subspaces $J_{u,j}\leq M_j$, and to a canonical maximizer. The independent choices in distinct summands give

$$
\epsilon(P)=\kappa+\sum_j\dim V_j\,
 \max_{L\le M_j}\left[
   \frac1R\sum_u\dim(J_{u,j}\cap L)-\frac{\dim L}{D}
 \right].
$$

This formula identifies the mixed subspaces still requiring a test: arbitrary subspaces of each $M_j$. Automorphic multiplicity one in round 13 does not compute these dimensions for a finite-level algebra. In particular, a scalar Hecke character acting on a space of several rows leaves all subspaces of those rows available in the maximum.

## 2. The preserving part of the represented Hecke algebra

Use the round 8 geometric hypotheses for the two local corner actions at $g_0,g_1$ on the **same** realization. Each individual residue-prime spectral projector intertwines $P_u$ with a column operator. The two actions commute because they have disjoint local support. Their products therefore preserve every $J_u$. Write $p_b$ for the joint projections, where $b$ is a pair of residue-prime labels, each including all its split-place factors. This conclusion uses no joint counting estimate. The round 8 marginal dimension estimates alone do not determine joint dimensions.

On the regular family of round 13, use also the spherical Hecke operators at the good places of the totally real base field split in its CM extension. Their represented characters distinguish the distinct generic Arthur parameters $\psi$ occurring in the row space $F$. At such places occurring spherical members of one packet give the same character. Write $e_\psi$ for the projections onto these joint character spaces. The separation uses the isobaric transfer and equality at almost all relative-degree-one places, as detailed in the companion note. Only finitely many characters occur in this row space. [Hecke source and separation](HECKE_SOURCE.md) · [Round 13, §§2–3](../round13/PROOF.md)

The unital commutative algebra generated by these good operators and the two sets of residue-prime projectors is the algebra of all scalar functions on the nonzero cells

$$
e_c=p_b e_\psi,\qquad c=(b,\psi),\qquad F_c=e_cF.
$$

The actions commute. Finite interpolation separates their distinct joint characters and supplies each cell projection. This diagonal action on $F$ still leaves open whether the individual cell projections preserve the period images.

**Candidate-D.** Join distinct cells $c,d$ if $e_c\Pi_u e_d\ne0$ for at least one word $u$. The operators in the represented algebra preserving every $J_u$ are exactly the functions constant on each connected component. Indeed, for $h=\sum_c h_c e_c$, its adjoint is a polynomial in $h$ by finite interpolation on the distinct values $h_c$. Thus $h\in\mathcal E$ implies $h^*\in\mathcal E$. By §1 this is equivalent to $[h,\Pi_u]=0$ for every word. Its $(c,d)$ entry is $(h_c-h_d)e_c\Pi_u e_d$, which proves the assertion in both directions. Summing $e_c$ over a component gives the corresponding preserving projector. A canonical maximizer splits under these component projections.

Every edge stays inside one label $b$, since $p_b$ commutes with all $\Pi_u$. Consequently two Arthur parameters may be separable within one residue-prime block even if they are joined within another. Using only globally good-place operators would require the same scalar for a parameter across all $b$. The graph gives the exact distinction on the combined algebra without assuming that any individual packet image is preserved.

## 3. Edge weights from the actual period matrices

On $C_u^\circ$ put $i_u=P_u(P_u^*P_u|_{C_u^\circ})^{-1/2}$, using the restricted map. This is an isometry onto $J_u$. For a zero image use the zero-dimensional space. Define

$$
A_{u,c}=i_u^*e_ci_u,\qquad A_{u,c}\geq0,\qquad \sum_c A_{u,c}=I.
$$

Since $\Pi_u=i_ui_u^*$, cyclicity of the trace gives

$$
\|e_c\Pi_u e_d\|_{\rm HS}^2
   =\operatorname{Tr}(A_{u,c}A_{u,d}). \tag{14.2}
$$

The right side is real and nonnegative even if the two compressed matrices do not commute. Summing (14.2) over words gives the edge weight. An edge is absent exactly when this sum vanishes. Merely observing that the same word has components in both cells does not suffice to join them. Those components can arise from orthogonal column directions. Formula (14.2) resolves this using each word's actual inner products.

For instance, the sum of the squared Hilbert–Schmidt norms of $[h,\Pi_u]$ equals $\sum_{c,d}|h_c-h_d|^2\sum_u\operatorname{Tr}(A_{u,c}A_{u,d})$. This follows by orthogonality of the matrix blocks and includes both ordered pairs for each edge. The vanishing criterion uses the kernel of this nonnegative quadratic form. Individual positive weights may be arbitrarily small without changing the component partition.

## 4. The error from testing split subspaces

Choose any orthogonal decomposition $F=\bigoplus_c F_c$ with projections $e_c$. It may be finer than the proven symmetry decomposition. Define $\epsilon_{\rm split}$ by maximizing the **original** functional (14.1) over $W=\bigoplus_c(W\cap F_c)$. For each word form

$$
J_u^-=\bigoplus_c(J_u\cap F_c)\ \subseteq J_u\subseteq\
J_u^+=\bigoplus_c e_cJ_u,\qquad
\ell_u^-=\dim J_u-\dim J_u^-,\quad
\ell_u^+=\dim J_u^+-\dim J_u .
$$

The inner space gives the largest split subspace contained in $J_u$; the outer gives the smallest split space containing it. The actual source subspace $P_u^{-1}(J_u^-)$ has codimension $\ell_u^-$. In particular, restricting to it retains the entire original kernel.

**Candidate-D.** With all dimensions and normalizations as in (14.1),

$$
0\leq\epsilon(P)-\epsilon_{\rm split}
   \leq \frac1R\sum_u\min(\ell_u^-,\ell_u^+). \tag{14.3}
$$

For each word choose $Y_u=J_u^-$ when $\ell_u^-\leq\ell_u^+$ and choose $Y_u=J_u^+$ otherwise. Let $\Phi_Y$ denote (14.1) with these images substituted, keeping $\kappa,R,D$ fixed. For an inner choice the difference $\dim(J_u\cap W)-\dim(Y_u\cap W)$ lies between zero and $\ell_u^-$. For an outer choice it lies between $-\ell_u^+$ and zero. Both assertions follow by mapping the larger intersection into the quotient by the smaller space. These bounds hold for every $W$.

The auxiliary functional $\Phi_Y$ has a split maximizer. For two blocks replace $W$ by $(W\cap F_1)\oplus e_2W$. Its dimension stays fixed. Projection onto $F_2$ shows that the dimension of intersection with each split $Y_u$ can only increase. Iterating over the blocks proves the claim. Thus the largest possible value of $\Phi$ is at most the split maximum of $\Phi_Y$ plus $R^{-1}\sum_{\rm inner}\ell_u^-$. At a split maximizer of $\Phi_Y$, its value is at most $\epsilon_{\rm split}+R^{-1}\sum_{\rm outer}\ell_u^+$. Together these are (14.3). The lower inequality follows by inclusion of the tested subspaces.

Both costs can be recovered from §3 without changing the finite tests. If $d_u=\dim C_u^\circ$, then

$$
\ell_u^+=\sum_c\operatorname{rank}A_{u,c}-d_u,\qquad
\ell_u^-=\operatorname{rank}\!\left(I-\sum_c A_{u,c}^2\right).
\tag{14.4}
$$

For the first formula, $\operatorname{rank} A_{u,c}=\dim e_cJ_u$. For the second, $A_{u,c}-A_{u,c}^2=i_u^*e_c(I-\Pi_u)e_ci_u$ is positive. Its kernel consists of those $v$ with $e_ci_uv\in J_u$. The kernel of the sum imposes this for every $c$, equivalently $i_uv\in J_u^-$. Rank-nullity proves (14.4).

The coefficient one in (14.3) is sharp even for spanning integer columns. In $\mathbb C^D$, $D\ge2$, use the coordinate lines as blocks. Take $s\ge1$ copies of each basis column and $D$ copies of $(1,\ldots,1)$, spanning a line $L$. Then $R=(s+1)D$, $\kappa=0$, the joint adjoint is injective, and the cost in (14.3) is $\lambda=1/(s+1)$. A subspace missing $L$ has nonpositive defect since it contains at most $\dim W$ coordinate lines. The only split space containing $L$ is the whole target, so $\epsilon_{\rm split}=0$. A proper subspace containing $L$ and $j$ coordinate lines has dimension at least $j+1$, giving defect at most $\lambda-1/D-j\lambda/D$. The line $L$ attains $\lambda-1/D$. Thus $\epsilon(P)=\max(0,\lambda-1/D)$, arbitrarily close to the full cost even at fixed small $\lambda$.

Small off-diagonal norms do not bound the rank cost. In $\mathbb C^2$ a single column $(1,t)$ with $t\ne0$ has mixed excess $1/2$ however close its line gets to the first axis. Both examples concern abstract linear maps.

**Open for the actual periods.** The combined residue-prime decomposition is available under its stated hypotheses. A finer decomposition preserves every image exactly when all its cross-block weights (14.2) vanish. An individual maximizer can happen to split without that symmetry. A split-rank estimate and a sufficiently small total cost in (14.3) would also bound the full defect. The actual finer graph, these rank costs and the required subspace estimate have not been evaluated here.
