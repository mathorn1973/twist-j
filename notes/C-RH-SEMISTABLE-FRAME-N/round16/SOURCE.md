**Candidate-D.** For fixed $B_k(u)$, replacing a full source lattice by a finite-index sublattice preserves its $L$-span. ## 3. A coefficient-sensitive formula for the next reduction

**Candidate-D under the stated period, row and rationality ties.** Use algebraic coordinates in the fixed target and in each full source. Work with one tuple $\mu$ for the whole matrix at the chosen place. Use $\mu=\mu_0$ for the reference model or $\mu=\mathrm{muAt}(w)$ for the twisted local coefficients. In the latter case let $N_u$ denote `Tw.e0 u μ ∘ nuIn u` into the base member of the twisted chain. In the reference tuple use `nuIn`. Choose a basis $x_i$ of the localized row lattice, take its dual as the target basis, and choose test rows $F_i\in V0(\mu)$ with $\iota_\mu F_i=jEE(x_i)$. The points input gives these lifts through $Qsp(\mu)\subset V0(\mu)$. When using the lattice pairing at $\mu=\mathrm{muAt}(w)$, `RRDef` expresses each $x_i$ as a local integral combination of vectors whose $jEE$ images lie in `RRset`. By the definitions of `RRset` and `LamSet`, each generator lifts to a projection of an element of `Mtilde.carrier ∩ V0`. The identity `iota_proj` lets one use that element itself. Taking the same local combination of these lifts extends the pairing from its integral row generators. All words use the same row coordinates. [Localized row lattice][rrdef]. Let $K_{u,i}$ be the descended adjacent `KL` maps and $P_{u,\mathrm{end}}$ the descended `PtL`. $$
M_u=\mathrm{ze}(u,\mu)^{-1}\,E_{u,\mu}\,P_{u,\mathrm{end}}\,K_{u,m-1}\cdots K_{u,0}\,N_u.

Here the meaning is entrywise after the specified embeddings when necessary. For $r=\operatorname{rank}_L M_u>0$, choose $r$ independent columns $J$ and a row set $I$ minimizing the valuation of the nonzero $r$-row minors of $M_u[:,J]$. The matrix

$$
G_u=M_u[:,J]M_u[I,J]^{-1}
$$

has entries in $O$ by Cramer's rule, since each entry is a ratio of minors to the chosen pivot minor, up to sign. The rows $I$ are the identity. Hence its columns form an $O$-basis of the saturated image $A_u$. They span $S_u$ over $L$, and if a combination lies in $\Lambda$, those pivot rows give integral coefficients. This also fixes a direct precision requirement. If the chosen pivot minor has valuation $a$, the minors divided by $\pi^a$ modulo $\pi^2$ determine $G_u$ modulo $\pi^2$. In integral matrix coordinates, the minors modulo $\pi^{a+2}$ suffice after the pivot has been certified. At rank zero use the zero module.

Let $E_u=A_u/\pi A_u\subset\Lambda/\pi\Lambda$ and let $W\subset\Lambda/\pi\Lambda$ be a common test subspace. Fix a direct-summand lift $\widetilde W\subset\Lambda$. For $x\in E_u\cap W$, lift $x$ to $\tilde x\in\widetilde W$. With $p_I$ the pivot row projection, the first graph displacement modulo the two residue spaces can be written

$$
f_u(x)=\left[\overline{\pi^{-1}(G_u p_I\tilde x-\tilde x)}\right]
\in (\Lambda/\pi\Lambda)/(E_u+W).
$$

The numerator is divisible by $\pi$ because both vectors reduce to $x$. Changing the lift by $\pi y$ with $y\in\widetilde W$ changes the reduction by a vector of $E_u+W$. These classes are the first-order data to test simultaneously for a common lift of $W$, using compatible row coordinates for every word. They depend on the actual entries through ratios of minors. The input lattice lengths, the valuation inequalities, and the fact that a multiplier has norm one do not assign these classes. ## 4. A rank lower bound from the actual diagonal estimate

**Candidate-D under `BesselId` and the diagonal estimate in `LinkS_at` with its size premise.** Put $n_u=\texttt{duS}(u)$, $s_u=\texttt{A.du}(u)$, and $b=\texttt{Dall}-\texttt{Drows}$. In the nonzero dimension case $n_u>0$ and $0\le e_0<1$, `unfolding_constant_pos_of_diagonal` gives a positive normalization $c_u$. The actual matrix identity gives $K_{uu}=c_u^{-1}P_u^*P_u$. The square of the Hilbert–Schmidt norm of $K_{uu}-1$ is at most $e_0 n_u$. Each orthonormal basis vector of $\ker P_u$ contributes one to that sum. The kernel after restriction to the ramified columns has dimension at most $\lfloor e_0 n_u\rfloor$. Projection off the $b$ discarded rows loses at most $b$ additional rank. The complex basis changes are invertible, and scalar extension preserves matrix rank. Therefore

$$
\operatorname{rank}_L B_k(u)\ge
\max\{0,s_u-b-\lfloor e_0 n_u\rfloor\}.
$$

At $n_u=0$ the lower bound is zero from the domain dimension. At every $w$ where the target is full, the saturated residue image has dimension exactly the generic rank, so the same lower bound holds for it. For the raw integral matrix, a rank-size nonzero minor is a unit outside finitely many coefficient places for each fixed finite family with fixed full lattices. This gives neither a specified served inert place nor a uniform exceptional set along a varying family. It also leaves the directions and joint intersections of the primitive images undetermined.
