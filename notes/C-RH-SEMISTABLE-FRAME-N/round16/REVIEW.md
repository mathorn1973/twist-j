## 1. What the source lattice can determine

For a word map $B_u:E_u\to F$ write $J_u=B_u(E_u)$, $d_u=\dim J_u$, $r_u=\dim E_u$, $R=\sum r_u>0$, $D=\dim F>0$. The primitive image is the reduction of $M_u=J_u\cap\Lambda$ inside $\bar F=\Lambda/\pi\Lambda$. This reduction has dimension $d_u$. For every full source lattice $N_u$ with $B_uN_u\subseteq\Lambda$, the saturation of $B_uN_u$ in $\Lambda$ equals $M_u$. Indeed the two modules span the same $K$-space and $M_u$ is saturated.

Even prescribing the full local Smith type of each word leaves its primitive direction free. For a given $J_u$, take a basis $z_1,\ldots,z_{d_u}$ of $M_u$, lift it to $E_u$, and add a basis of $\ker B_u$. Given any nonnegative integers $a_1\le\cdots\le a_{d_u}$, multiply the lifted vectors by $\pi^{a_j}$ and take their $O$-span together with the kernel basis. This full source lattice gives exactly the prescribed exponents on the nonzero image. This describes freedom in the abstract local problem. The actual source prescribes its own lattice.

## 2. Equal word and joined Smith forms, different defects

**Candidate-D.** Use three rank-two integral word maps into $\mathbb Z^3$. For one family their images are the hyperplanes with normals
$$
 e_1-e_2,\quad e_2-e_3,\quad e_3-e_1 .
$$
For the other family the normals are
$$
 e_1+e_2,\quad e_2+e_3,\quad e_3+e_1 .
$$
For the hyperplane with equation $x_i\pm x_j=0$, use the columns $e_h$ and $e_i\mp e_j$, with $h$ the omitted index. In either family each word image is the full integral hyperplane with Smith exponents zero. The two columns are orthogonal with squared lengths $1,2$. All words therefore have the same individual singular values and the same determinant norm on the image. Every two distinct word images already generate $\mathbb Z^3$. For example the first pair supplies $e_3,e_1$ and a vector $e_1\mp e_2$. A cyclic permutation gives the other pairs and permutes the three words in both families. Consequently the concatenation for **every subset** has identical integral Smith form in the two families. All pairs of distinct unit normal directions have the same absolute inner product $1/2$ in both families.

Over $\mathbb Q$ and over every residue field of odd characteristic, the three minus normals have a common annihilator line, while the determinant of the three plus normals is $2$. For three distinct planes, a test plane meets them in total dimension at most four, and a test line contributes the number of planes containing it. With $R=6,D=3$, the two defects are respectively $1/6$ and zero. Thus ranks, all subset concatenation Smith forms, individual Euclidean norms as above and cyclic symmetry together fail to force the answer. A common scalar multiple of all the matrices in both families preserves equality of their raw subset Smith forms and preserves both primitive arrangements. Independent changes of source lattices can impose arbitrary *individual* exponents as in Section 1. We assert no equality of joined Smith forms under those independent changes.

More generally, for $m$ injective hyperplane words with nonzero normals $n_u$ in dimension $D\ge2$, $R=m(D-1)$. For $L=W^\circ$ the exact excess at $W$ is
$$
 \frac{\#\{u:n_u\in L\}-(m/D)\dim L}{R}.
$$
Indeed $\dim(H_u\cap W)=\dim W-1+\mathbf1_{n_u\in L}$. Replacing $L$ by the span of its contained normals preserves their count and can only raise the excess. Zero defect is equivalent to $D|S|\le m\,\mathrm{rank}(n_u:u\in S)$ for every subset $S$ of words. This is one positive test based on simultaneous *dual* pairings. [Round 15 for arbitrary mixed layers](../round15/PROOF.md#4-a-certificate-on-successive-quotients).

**Candidate-D.** With only $r_u,d_u,D$ and full joint span given, there is also a sharp general upper envelope. Put $T=\sum d_u$, $\kappa=1-T/R$. Over any field the largest permissible defect among such arrangements is
$$
 \kappa+\max_{0\le w\le D}
 \left(\frac{T-\max\{D-w,\sum_u(d_u-w)_+\}}{R}-\frac wD\right),
$$
with integer $w$. To see the bound fix $W$ of dimension $w$. The quotient dimensions $q_u=\dim((J_u+W)/W)$ satisfy $q_u\ge(d_u-w)_+$ and $\sum q_u\ge D-w$. Hence the sum of intersection dimensions is at most the stated numerator. For equality at a maximizing $w$, prescribe $\sum q_u=\max\{D-w,\sum(d_u-w)_+\}$ with $(d_u-w)_+\le q_u\le\min(d_u,D-w)$. There is sufficient total upper capacity because $T\ge D$. Then $p_u=d_u-q_u$ satisfies $0\le p_u\le w$ and $\sum p_u\ge w$, by $T\ge D$ and $\sum\min(d_u,w)\ge w$. Choose coordinate subsets of sizes $p_u$ covering $W$, and separately of sizes $q_u$ covering a complement. In either block one can use successive portions of a cyclically repeated coordinate list, with empty blocks requiring no choices. The direct sums of the two selected coordinate spaces attain the bound. Arbitrary stated kernels are added in the sources. A substantially better uniform estimate than this envelope requires information beyond these dimensions and full span.

## 3. The first-order obstruction on the smallest maximizer

**Candidate-D.** Define $H(S)=\sum_u\dim(\bar J_u\cap S)-(R/D)\dim S$ on $\bar F$, with $\bar J_u$ the primitive images. Then $\epsilon=\kappa+\max H/R$ for this residue arrangement. Supermodularity makes the sum and intersection of two maximizing test spaces maximizing. Let $V,W$ be the smallest and largest maximizers. Work over the residue field. For the number and finite fields of interest the largest geometric maximizer descends and the base-field maximum is the geometric maximum. [Field comparison in round 15](../round15/PROOF.md#3-the-field-of-the-maximizer-and-specialization).

Let $\Lambda'$ be the preimage of $W$ in $\Lambda$, $Q=\bar F/W$, and $F'=\Lambda'/\pi\Lambda'$. There is an exact sequence $0\to Q\to F'\to W\to0$, with the kernel identification induced by multiplication by $\pi$. Put $X_u=\bar J_u\cap W$ and $Y_u=(\bar J_u+W)/W$. Since $M'_u=J_u\cap\Lambda'$ is the preimage of $X_u$ in $M_u$, its reduction in $F'$ projects onto $X_u$ and its intersection with $Q$ equals $Y_u$. In a linear splitting it is described by a map $f_u:X_u\to Q/Y_u$.

For a new witness with kernel part $T\le Q$ and projection $Z\le W$, its value is at most $H(Z)+H(\mathrm{pr}^{-1}T)-H(W)$, where $\mathrm{pr}:\bar F\to Q$. This follows by projecting each word intersection and bounding the dimension of its kernel and image separately. For nonzero $T$ the second and third terms have strictly negative sum by maximality of $W$. Equality with the old maximum therefore requires $T=0$, $Z$ an old maximizer, and equality on the intersection of each word with $Z$. Such a subspace is a graph over $Z$, and $V\subseteq Z$. Restriction to $V$ gives a solution of
$$
 s\in\mathrm{Hom}(V,Q),\qquad
 s|_{X_u\cap V}\bmod Y_u=f_u|_{X_u\cap V}\quad\text{for every }u .
$$
Conversely every solution supplies a graph over $V$ attaining the old maximum. **Strict drop is equivalent to inconsistency of this one linear system.** Changing the splitting adds the restrictions of one common linear map, so the class in the cokernel of the displayed restriction map is intrinsic (up to the indicated identifications). A dual witness consists of tensors in $(X_u\cap V)\otimes Y_u^\circ$ whose sum in $V\otimes Q^*$ is zero and whose summed pairing with the $f_u$ is nonzero. This is the ordinary dual separation criterion for membership in the image of a linear map. Word kernels enter only through the fixed normalizations.

### Arbitrarily long persistence at one place

**Candidate-D.** For an integer $N\ge1$ use three primitive hyperplanes in $O^3$ with normals
$$
 (1,-1,0),\quad (0,1,-1),\quad (\pi^N-1,0,1).
$$
Their determinant is $\pi^N$. The generic defect is zero by the dual criterion in Section 2. Write $e=(1,1,1)$ and
$\Lambda_n=Oe\oplus\pi^n(Oe_1\oplus Oe_2)$ for $0\le n\le N$. In the displayed target basis their primitive normals have coordinates
$$
 (0,1,-1),\quad (0,0,1),\quad (\pi^{N-n},\pi^N-1,0).
$$
These follow by evaluating on the basis and dividing each covector by $\pi^n$. For $n<N$ the three residue planes are distinct and share exactly the line generated by the first target basis vector. It is their unique maximizer of defect $1/6$; taking its preimage gives exactly $\Lambda_{n+1}$. At $n=N$ the residue normals are independent and the defect is zero. Thus the system can be consistent for arbitrarily many successive steps even where the eventual drop is strict. At $n=N-1$ write a first-order lift of the common residue vector as $(1,\pi a,\pi b)$ in the basis of $\Lambda_n$, allowing multiplication by a unit to normalize the first coordinate. Dividing the three normal evaluations by $\pi$ gives the residue conditions $a-b=0$, $b=0$, and $1-a=0$. Their sum gives $1=0$. This row combination is an explicit nonzero dual obstruction in every residue characteristic. For $n<N-1$ the same system has zero constant term and admits $a=b=0$.

## 4. Why an infinite constant plateau is a generic witness

**Candidate-D.** Iterate the largest-maximizer modification of Section 3 starting over the original DVR. The finite set of defect values gives an eventual plateau. On such a plateau every new maximizing witness injects into the preceding $W$, so the dimensions of the largest maximizers eventually stabilize as well. Start the indices after both stabilizations. Each $W_{n+1}\to W_n$ is then an isomorphism. In the word intersection estimates equality of the sum forces termwise equality. Hence
$$
 \bar J_{u,n+1}\cap W_{n+1}\longrightarrow\bar J_{u,n}\cap W_n
$$
is an isomorphism for every word.

Here is an explicit completion argument. For a chain $L_{n+1}\subseteq L_n$ defined by preimages of subspaces $U_n\le L_n/\pi L_n$, suppose the transition maps $U_{n+1}\to U_n$ are isomorphisms and $\dim U_n=w$. Choose coordinates on $L_0$ with $U_0$ the first $w$ residue coordinates. There are matrices $A_n$ over $O$ such that for $n\ge1$
$$
 L_n=\{(x,y)\in O^w\oplus O^{d-w}: y\equiv A_n x\pmod{\pi^n}\},
 \qquad A_{n+1}\equiv A_n\pmod{\pi^n}.
$$
Take $A_1=0$. Given $A_n$, write $y=A_nx+\pi^nz$. The projection onto the residue of $x$ identifies $U_n$ with $k^w$ by the transition isomorphisms. Thus $U_n$ is the graph of some matrix $C_n$ in the residue coordinates $(x,z)$, and $A_{n+1}=A_n+\pi^n\widetilde C_n$ works for any lift. Over the completion the intersection of the extended lattices is the graph of $\lim A_n$, a free module of rank $w$. The two endpoint dimensions are included.

Apply this argument to the target chain and separately to each $M_{u,n}=J_u\cap\Lambda_n$. For the latter, each successive lattice is the preimage of $\bar J_{u,n}\cap W_n$, with precisely the transition isomorphisms proved above. Its limit lies inside the generic word space over the completion and inside the target limit. The resulting generic test space over the completed field therefore attains at least the plateau value. Field invariance of the generic maximum in round 15 and specialization in the other direction force equality. All finite lattice modifications used the original residue field and have preimages over the original DVR. The specific limiting subspace need only live over the completion. This proves existence of a common lattice attaining the generic value at any prescribed coefficient place. The example in Section 3 rules out a step bound depending only on these dimensions. Equality of two consecutive values by itself supplies no stopping criterion for exact optimality.

### Arbitrary minors, symbolic coefficients and primitive coordinates

**Candidate-D.** In the expanded [Section 4 of the proof](PROOF.md#4-a-conditional-depth-bound-and-a-sharp-precision-obstruction), let $A:P\to T$ be the auxiliary square map between free lattices of rank $n$, with $P',T'$ the one-step sublattices. In old Smith bases take the summand $C$ on the first $s$ nonzero invariant factors, put $Y=K A(C)$, and $S=Y\cap T$. The length of $S/A(C)$ is $k_s$. Write $c=\mathrm{length}(C/(C\cap P'))$ and $y=\mathrm{length}(S/(S\cap T'))$. The first quotient injects into $P/P'$, so $c\le L_{\rm in}$. The second quotient is the image of $S$ in $T/T'$. As $T/T'$ is killed by $\pi$, the saturated $s$-dimensional reduction of $S$ in $T/\pi T$ projects with codimension at most $n-s$ in $T/T'$. Therefore $y\ge L_{\rm out}-(n-s)$. Injectivity of $A|_C$ gives the exact new restricted index $k_s+c-y$. Both $C\cap P'$ and $Y\cap T'$ are saturated in their respective new lattices. Extending bases exhibits their restricted determinant among the size-$s$ minors in those bases. The minimum valuation is independent of bases by Cauchy–Binet in both directions. This proves (16.5) also when the auxiliary map has larger rank than $s$. A fixed free summand for each word kernel remains fixed during the modification, while those dimensions are included in $R$. At a stopping step $H=0$, one has $U=\kappa\le\eta$ whenever the minor exists, since the auxiliary map has rank at most $n(1-\kappa)$.

The same reasoning works over $O[x]_{(\pi)}$. Every nonzero polynomial is a power of $\pi$ times a polynomial with at least one unit coefficient, which is a unit in this localization. Each positive $n(U-\eta)$ is congruent to $s$ modulo $\ell$ because $nU$ and $n$ are divisible by $\ell$. Thus the step bound from valuation $t$ is $\lfloor t/c\rfloor$ where $c=1+((s-1)\bmod\ell)$. This includes the full-determinant case.
