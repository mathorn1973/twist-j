## 1. A rational cyclotomic block cannot by itself carry the geometric Frobenius

**Candidate-D.** Let $q$ be an odd inert prime, $K=\mathbb Q_q(j)$ the unramified quartic field, and $\sigma(j)=j^q$. Suppose a finite-dimensional $K$-space $V$ admits a $\mathbb Q_q$-linear operator $T$ with
$$
Ta=\sigma(a)T,\qquad T^4=-q^2 I .
$$
Then $m=\dim_K V$ is even. Indeed, in a $K$-basis write $T=C\sigma$ with $C$ invertible. Taking determinants in $C\sigma(C)\sigma^2(C)\sigma^3(C)=-q^2 I$ gives $N_{K/\mathbb Q_q}(\det C)=(-q^2)^m$. The valuation on $K$ normalized by $v(q)=1$ is invariant under $\sigma$. Thus $4v(\det C)=2m$. In particular a single rational four-dimensional copy of $\mathbb Q(j)$ cannot admit the pair either, by tensoring with $\mathbb Q_q$.

If a full $\mathbb Z_q$-lattice is stable under $j$ and $T$, its reduction is a vector space over $E=\mathbb F_q[j]=\mathbb F_{q^4}$, and $\bar T$ is semilinear over $E$ with $\bar T^4=0$. For $\bar T\ne0$, its nonzero proper kernel is an $E$-subspace stable under $\bar T$. If $\bar T=0$, every $E$-line is stable. In particular an irreducible nonzero module for these reduced relations has $E$-dimension one and $\bar T=0$. The residue-field automorphism $z\mapsto z^q$ has fourth power the identity. Substituting that automorphism for $\bar T$ changes the relations.

This dimension obstruction assumes the indicated $\mathbb Q_q$-linear realization. On a Tate module at $\ell\ne q$ the coefficient field is $\mathbb Q_\ell$. Crystalline Frobenius over an enlarged coefficient field also requires its own coefficient and semilinearity conventions. The argument neither contradicts the geometric endomorphism identity nor produces an endomorphism of a complex period row.

**Candidate-D.** Even an action of the full matrix algebra generated on the target gives no bound without a symmetry of the weighted image family. On $\mathbb C^4$ let $j$ act diagonally with the four primitive fifth roots as distinct eigenvalues, and let $S$ cyclically permute the eigenlines so that $Sj=j^qS$. Put $T=cS$ with $c^4=-q^2$. Interpolating in $j$ gives the four diagonal matrix units. Multiplying these by powers of $S$ gives all matrix units. Choose one column on each eigenline and one additional column on the first. The columns span the target and are individually injective. The first line has defect $2/5-1/4=3/20$. The set of distinct lines is a single orbit, but the cyclic shift does not preserve their multiplicities.

## 2. A finite ground field suffices for the unrestricted maximum

**Candidate-D.** For a family defined over a finite field $k$, the largest maximizer over $\bar k$ is fixed by its arithmetic Frobenius. Indeed, the intersection-dimension functional is supermodular, so the sum of all maximizing spaces is again maximizing, as in round 14. Frobenius permutes these spaces. The unique reduced row-echelon matrix for their sum in a fixed $k$-basis is therefore Frobenius-fixed, so its entries belong to $k$. This also works for a Galois-stable multiset of geometric images if their multiplicities and the fixed normalizations are preserved. Any $k$-linear group preserving that multiset fixes the largest maximizer. If its action on the $k$-target is irreducible, the global maximum occurs at an endpoint. For genuine maps these endpoint values are $\kappa$ at zero and zero at the whole target. An upper bound on $\kappa$ remains necessary.

At a *prescribed* dimension this descent can fail. In $V=k^2\oplus k^2$ take the coordinate planes, $\operatorname{graph}(I)$ and $\operatorname{graph}(A)$, where $A$ has irreducible quadratic characteristic polynomial over $k$. The four planes are pairwise complementary. To meet both coordinate planes in lines a two-plane must be $L\oplus M$. Meeting the third forces $L=M$, and meeting the fourth then forces $AL=L$. Such eigenlines exist over $\bar k$ and none exists over $k$. A two-plane equal to one of the four has total intersection dimension two. Every other two-plane has each intersection dimension at most one. The maximum at dimension two is four over $\bar k$ and three over $k$, the latter attained by $L\oplus L$ for a $k$-line. The endpoints retain the same value for the unrestricted problem.

## 3. Sharp boundary of the four-translate criterion

**Candidate-D.** Let $K=k(t)$, $t$ a primitive fifth root and $[K:k]=4$. Any four distinct elements of $1,t,t^2,t^3,t^4$ are $k$-independent since the unique relation is their sum. For any $k$-plane $U\subset K$, $U\cap t^aU\ne0$ for $a\not\equiv0\pmod5$ forces $U=v\langle1,t^a\rangle_k$. Consequently $U\cap t^{2a}U=0$, since $1,t^a,t^{2a},t^{3a}$ are independent. Thus any member of the five translated planes meets at most two other members in lines. A two-plane unequal to all four selected members meets each in dimension at most one. If it equals a selected member, the total intersection dimension is at most $2+1+1=4$ as well. For a test line at most two of the orbit planes contain it, by independence of any three translates of a nonzero vector. For a test hyperplane every orbit plane meets it in dimension at least one and at most two planes can be contained in it. The latter follows by applying the same line argument to the annihilator planes under the nondegenerate trace pairing. This gives the required totals $2,4,6$ at test dimensions $1,2,3$.

For $U$ a line, the four translated directions are independent, so at most $\dim W$ lie in $W$. For $U$ a hyperplane their four normals are independent by the trace pairing. For $W\ne0$ at most $4-\dim W$ translated hyperplanes contain it, and all others meet it in codimension one. The total is at most $3\dim W$. The other dimensions of $U$ and $W$ are immediate. These arguments hold for both $k=\mathbb Q$ and inert residue fields. They apply after scalar extension to a family defined over $k$ by descent of the global maximum (and, for $\mathbb Q$, the Grassmannian closed-point argument above followed by Galois descent). Allowing a new arbitrary geometric seed $U$ changes the statement. A single $t$-eigenline after scalar extension has four identical translates and defect $3/4$.

Three planes can already violate the bound with full span. For $U=\langle1,t\rangle_k$ take $t^{-1}U,U,tU$. Their sum contains four distinct powers and fills $K$. At $W=U$ their three intersections have dimensions $1,2,1$, giving defect $4/6-2/4=1/6$. Several copies of $K$ give a different failure even with four distinct translates. In $K^2$ take $U=k e_1\oplus K e_2$. Any four translates span $K^2$, but at $W=K e_2$ the defect is $16/20-4/8=3/10$.

For multiplicities $n_i$ with total $n>0$, the weights $n/4-n_i$ on packages omitting index $i$ reconstruct the given family when $\max_i n_i\le n/4$. These are nonnegative rational weights, without any divisibility requirement on $n$. Conversely, for a criterion valid for every $U$, a seed line and a most frequent translated line force $\max_i n_i\le n/4$.
