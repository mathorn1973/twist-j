## 1. The arithmetic model of the actual cut map

The pinned `D7` supplies a totally complex Galois number field $L$ with an embedding of $E$, a fixed complex embedding and a correspondence between coefficient places and prime ideals of $\mathcal O_L$. In `DivData`, the map
$$
B_k(u):N'_u\longrightarrow R_0^\vee.
$$
Here $N'_u$ is the vector space of `div.Nu u` and $R_0$ is the vector space of `div.RR`. This map is $L$-linear. An `HLat` has an $L$-basis, an $\mathcal O_L$-submodule and separate complex matrices for its archimedean metrics. Thus $B_k$ is a field-linear map even before checking its lattice containment. The source's `OneMapIntoCC` supplies $B_k(u)\Lambda_u\subseteq\Lambda_R^\vee$, where $\Lambda_u$ is the lattice in `div.Nu u` and $\Lambda_R^\vee$ is `div.RR.dualL`. With full lattices this gives an $\mathcal O_L$-linear pairing and a map of local free lattices at each coefficient prime. A chosen field basis need not be a local integral basis. ## 3. What a certificate at a coefficient prime controls

Use a prime $w$ of $\mathcal O_L$ above the chosen rational prime, local free lattices for all $N'_u$, and the **same** target lattice $\Lambda_R^\vee$. Once the integral containment holds, upper semicontinuity of intersection or preimage dimension gives a one-sided test. An upper bound for the residue-family concentration over an algebraic closure of the residue field also bounds the characteristic-zero concentration of $B_k$ over $\mathbb C$. The numerator must use the original source dimensions, and the residue statement must cover all tested subspaces or justify reduction to rational tests. This uses rank and incidence and requires no residue analogue of a Hermitian metric. The statement also holds with saturated image reduction. In that construction take each generic image's intersection with the common target lattice before reducing, and retain the **generic** kernel dimensions in the defect formula. The round 15 proof gives the specialization argument and the cut-loss comparison with $P_u$. A wordwise scalar factor divisible by the uniformizer can erase an entire raw residue image. Saturation removes that loss but preserves neither all incidences nor the archimedean norm bounds.

Even if an embedding $K_5\hookrightarrow L$ is supplied, scalar $j$ fixes each $L$-linear subspace, since $j^i\operatorname{im} B_k(u)=\operatorname{im} B_k(u)$. The same observation applies to the images over $\kappa(w)$ for the reduced unit. A nontrivial orbit of a smaller-field subspace, or a filtration yielding such orbits, needs a specified descent or a second action and identities with the actual word images. The read Frobenius statements concern host embedding indices, jet or local lattice data. None supplies that orbit structure for $B_k$, the uncut $B_f$ or $P_u$. Even when $q$ is inert in $K_5$ and $K_5$ embeds compatibly, a coefficient prime $w\mid q$ could have residue field larger than $\mathbb F_{q^4}$. Inertness in $K_5$ alone establishes no concentration bound at $w$. **O** for a useful certificate for the actual cut family at one such place and for its effect on the full-family concentration.

## 4. The two primary accounts

**Literature, Galbraith–Pujolàs.** Their Section 3 works with the good reduction of $y^2=x^5+A$ at a prime $p\equiv2,3\bmod5$. For $A=-1$ use odd $p$ in those classes. The fifth power is bijective on $\mathbb F_p$ and on $\mathbb F_{p^2}$. Lemma 3.1 takes the resulting point counts first, then obtains $X^4+p^2$ as the characteristic polynomial of the actual Frobenius endomorphism of the reduced Jacobian. The endomorphism equation and the square-root modulus follow from that polynomial. Lemma 3.4 gives the relation with the reduced automorphism $j$, equivalently $\pi_p j=j^p\pi_p$. Proposition 3.5 proves independence of the four cyclic-algebra components. Corollary 3.6 identifies the generated rational algebra with the sixteen-dimensional geometric endomorphism algebra of the reduced Jacobian. The paper subsequently needs a separate prime-to-$\ell$ denominator assumption for its distortion-map assertion involving the particular monomials on $\ell$-torsion. Rational endomorphism-algebra equality supplies no identification of integral orders. The reduced Jacobian and its endomorphisms supply no map to the host embedding permutations or to the period lattices above. The endomorphism $\pi_p$ and coefficient-field Frobenius are distinct operations. [Section 3, Lemma 3.1](https://www.math.auckland.ac.nz/~sgal018/jordi-paper.pdf#page=3) · [Lemma 3.4, Proposition 3.5 and Corollary 3.6](https://www.math.auckland.ac.nz/~sgal018/jordi-paper.pdf#page=5) · [Denominator assumption](https://www.math.auckland.ac.nz/~sgal018/jordi-paper.pdf#page=6).

**Literature, Alpöge.** The author's timeline starts with a Stepanov determinant and describes an invariant derivation on a CM curve satisfying $D^p\equiv0\bmod p$ at inert $p$, followed by divisibility of the theta derivatives normalized by a power of a period. The subsequent steps cite a finite-field Fourier count, GGP period determinants, a chain of definite unitary theta lifts, divisibility and norm comparisons, and a see-saw Bessel period. The note is a timeline and does not specify a genus-two derivative identity or give a coefficient model of the present uncut period map. In a separate precise higher-rank statement in the pinned source, `KaPInert` sums the ranks of the alternating Lie components of a Frobenius-like embedding permutation. Its inert weight is $((N-j)p+j)/(p^2-1)$, including the even-power contribution. Thus the author's scalar odd-power series should not be used as an entrywise assertion on $B_k$.
