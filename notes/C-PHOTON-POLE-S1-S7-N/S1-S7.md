## S3. Which pole is tested, and what "simple" means

### S3.1 Fixed infrared scaling

First take the specified infinite-volume state; only then let epsilon->0.
The comparison scaling of the fixed microscopic score is that of a
field-strength operator of dimension two. It is a proposed photon test, not
an adjustable anomalous exponent:

\[
 O_I^{(\varepsilon)}(f)=\varepsilon^2\sum_x
 f(\varepsilon c_{x,I})\,\delta O_I(x).
\]

For spatial Fourier correlations the corresponding spectral scaling is

\[
 \sigma_\varepsilon(B,q)=\varepsilon^{-1}
                  \sigma(\varepsilon B,\varepsilon q)
 \tag{S3-scale}
\]

for a Borel energy set B. The first epsilon means pushforward under
E_microscopic -> E_microscopic/epsilon, not an ordinary density substitution
without its Jacobian. If a spatial density is unavailable and dSigma(E,k) is
the joint spectral measure, the corresponding definition is

\[
 d\Sigma_\varepsilon=\varepsilon^{-4}
 (E,k\mapsto E/\varepsilon,k/\varepsilon)_*d\Sigma,
\]

restricted to the local momentum chart. The three spatial Jacobian powers
explain the difference from (S3-scale). The normalization follows the declared
dimension-two scaling. If that scaling yields zero, infinity, or no limit, no new exponent
may be chosen under this same profile.

Require convergence to a locally finite positive matrix measure sigma_0 on
compact energy intervals and spatial annuli 0<a<=|q|<=b<infinity. Zero
momentum is excluded: the parabolic apex is not counted as a polarization.

### S3.2 Extract the raw shell weight before imposing its target form

The proposed S6 dictionary fixes the candidate shell

\[
 \Sigma_\gamma=\{(E,q):q\ne0,\ E=|q|\}.
\]

Restrict the *actual limiting measure* to that shell. Require that its
momentum marginal is absolutely continuous on annuli. Define its matrix
density by

\[
 R(q)=\sigma_0(\{|q|\},q),\qquad
 d\sigma_{0,\rm pole}(E,q)=R(q)\,\delta_{|q|}(dE).
 \tag{S3-R}
\]

The right side is an extraction from sigma_0. It is not set equal to a
Maxwell tensor by definition. No atom gives R=0. A continuous spectrum
reaching E=0 is not automatically a one-particle pole.

An equivalent local definition, avoiding ultraviolet subtractions, uses
k=|q|>0 and the fixed cutoff

    chi(u)=0                          u<=1/2 or u>=3/2
           4u-2                       1/2<u<3/4
           1                          3/4<=u<=5/4
           6-4u                       5/4<u<3/2.

Set

\[
 F^+(z,q)=\int\frac{\chi(E/k)}{E-z}\,d\sigma_0(E,q),
 \quad \operatorname{Im}z>0,
 \qquad
 R(q)=\lim_{\eta\downarrow0}(-i\eta)F^+(k+i\eta,q).
 \tag{S3-res}
\]

For a locally finite positive spectral measure this isolates its atom.
It is a simple-pole *term*. An analytic gap separating it from a continuum
is not required by this minimal definition. If an isolated meromorphic pole
is later claimed, analyticity of the remainder in a neighborhood is an
additional obligation. A non-atomic threshold alone contributes no atom to
(S3-res).

The rest of sigma_0 may contain other particle or continuous sectors, but it
must be identified, must have no atom on this same shell, and must obey the
regular-remainder control in S7. This contract does not assert that the
entire reconstructed Hilbert space consists of photons.

A retarded commutator, when required, is defined from these same Hermitian
fields, not from a new scalar kernel:

\[
 D^R_{IJ}(t,q)=-i\,\mathbf1_{t>0}
 \langle[\mathcal O_I(t,q),\mathcal O_J(0,-q)]\rangle.
\]

Its Fourier convention is exp(+izt); the positive and negative energy
terms are sigma_IJ(dE,q)/(z-E) and -sigma_JI(dE,-q)/(z+E), respectively,
with distributions/contact terms fixed by the same operator definition.

**S3 output:** the actual massless-shell matrix R and a separately controlled

## S4. Residue and normalization without target fitting

Retain the S1 normalization of G. Let A_*={q:1<=|q|<=2}, and define the

\[
 Z_* = \frac{1}{\operatorname{Vol}(A_*)}
       \int_{A_*}\frac{\operatorname{tr}R_{BB}(q)}{|q|}\,d^3q.
 \tag{S4-Z}
\]

The fixed shell [1,2] is in *rescaled* momentum, not a physical ultraviolet
window. In the proposed target, this expression equals its residue Z_gamma.
In arbitrary data it is only a scalar diagnostic. The independent full
matrix test in S5 must still hold on every comparison annulus.

The residue condition is

\[
 0<Z_*<\infty,
\]

with no change of G and no separate direction-, polarization- or
momentum-dependent fitted factor. Z_* is determined by the model's raw
correlations. It is not set equal to one, a coupling constant, or a known

change of dictionary. Existence of a finite nonzero Z_* alone does not
establish polarization, linear dispersion or photon identity.

For bounded quantitative claims, independently controlled positive lower
and upper bounds on the residue over the declared annulus are required;
statistical resolution thresholds are not supplied by this definition.

**S4 output:** the model-derived Z_*, its existence/positivity disposition,

## S5. The physical rank-two tensor

### S5.1 Fixed target in the electric/magnetic convention

For q!=0 put k=|q|, n=q/k, and

\[
 P(n)=I_3-nn^T,\qquad C(n)v=n\times v.
\]

Define the six-dimensional orthogonal projector

\[
 \Pi_\gamma(n)=\frac12
 \begin{pmatrix}
  P(n)&-C(n)\\ C(n)&P(n)
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{S5-P}
\]

The proposed pole test is the complete matrix identity

\[
 \boxed{R(q)=Z_*\,|q|\,\Pi_\gamma(q/|q|).}
 \tag{S5-target}
\]

It includes all EE, BB, EB and BE blocks. A scalar trace or only the BB
rank does not establish it. In particular the E-B sign is fixed by S2's
orientation and plane-wave convention, not fitted after the result.

### S5.2 Conditional algebra explaining the number two

Choose any orthonormal basis e_1,e_2 of n-perp. No globally smooth choice
is required. Then

\[
 u_\lambda(q)=\begin{pmatrix}|q|e_\lambda\\q\times e_\lambda\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 R_\gamma(q)=\frac{Z_*}{2|q|}\sum_{\lambda=1}^2
 u_\lambda(q)u_\lambda(q)^\dagger.
\]

This equals (S5-target) and is independent of the chosen transverse basis.
Using C^T=-C, C^2=-P and PC=CP=C gives

\[
 \Pi_\gamma^2=\Pi_\gamma=\Pi_\gamma^\dagger,
 \qquad\operatorname{rank}\Pi_\gamma=2.
\]

Its range consists of the pairs

\[
 q\cdot E=q\cdot B=0,\qquad B=n\times E.
\]

Thus, conditional on the *actual* residue passing S5, it carries exactly two
independent complex positive-energy transverse amplitudes in this observed
pole sector. This is not the two frequency roots of the scalar recurrence,
not the rank of a transfer matrix, and not the LOW/HIGH outcome count.

A helicity +1/-1 statement additionally requires the reconstructed rotations
to act on these fibers as the corresponding vector action. The projector
formula supplies a natural candidate action; it does not prove emergence of
that action from the microscopic model.

### S5.3 Why the full Euclidean covariance must not be forced to rank two

For comparison, the standard Euclidean field-strength numerator is

\[
 N_{\mu\nu,\rho\sigma}(p)=
 p_\mu p_\rho\delta_{\nu\sigma}
 -p_\mu p_\sigma\delta_{\nu\rho}
 -p_\nu p_\rho\delta_{\mu\sigma}
 +p_\nu p_\sigma\delta_{\mu\rho}.
\]

Off shell, N(p)/p^2 acts on six antisymmetric components and has rank three
for nonzero real Euclidean p. This is a conditional linear-algebra comparison,
not a prediction for the raw lattice matrix. Physical polarization rank two
belongs to the positive-energy massless-shell residue after reconstruction.

For the accepted magnetic pole alone the Euclidean frequency expression is

\[
 \widetilde C^{E,\rm pole}_{BB}(\nu,q)
   =Z_*\frac{|q|^2I_3-qq^T}{\nu^2+|q|^2}.
 \tag{S5-BB}
\]

It has a quadratic numerator. Its bounded directional behavior as
(nu,q)->0 is not the scalar divergence 1/(nu^2+|q|^2). Electric and mixed
components must retain the reflection factors and contact prescription of
S2. A coordinate-space decay proportional to distance^-4, by itself, does
not identify the complete pole residue.

**S5 output:** full residue equality on the declared local domain, or the
precise failed block/rank/transversality condition. No offending component
may first be projected out to obtain a passing answer.

## S7. A definition of controlled errors

### S7.1 Limits and what is not assumed

The ordering is:

    periodic volume limit at fixed microscopic separations
      -> positive-time reconstruction
      -> infrared scaling on fixed nonzero momentum annuli
      -> extraction of the atomic shell contribution.

An alternative joint limit is allowed only with a proof of equivalence to
this prescription. In finite approximations epsilon L must grow without
bound; keeping epsilon L fixed defines a finite physical torus, not the
same infinite-volume pole.

No O(epsilon^2) interacting error is inferred from the free D3 bound. The
measure may have different irrelevant terms, logarithmic corrections,
continuum contributions, or no such scaling limit.

### S7.2 Raw spectral comparison

For a fixed annulus A_ab={a<=|q|<=b} and finite energy cutoff M, define
D_epsilon(a,b,M) as the bounded-Lipschitz distance between the full matrix
measures sigma_epsilon and sigma_0 on [0,M] x A_ab:

\[
 D_\varepsilon=
 \sup_{\Phi=\Phi^\dagger,\ \|\Phi\|_\infty+\operatorname{Lip}\Phi\le1}
 \left|\int\operatorname{tr}[\Phi(E,q)\,
       (d\sigma_\varepsilon-d\sigma_0)]\frac{d^3q}{(2\pi)^3}\right|.
\]

Tests are continuous and supported in that compact region. Here sigma denotes the energy measure at fixed q. When only the joint
measure Sigma is available, write the same definition directly as
integral tr[Phi(dSigma_epsilon-dSigma_0)], without inserting a second momentum
measure. No pointwise density is invented. The spectral
continuum is part of sigma_0; it is not an adjustable subtraction. Its
restriction off the extracted shell is uniquely fixed once sigma_0 is fixed.

A control certificate must give an explicit function eta(epsilon;a,b,M)
with limit zero and establish D_epsilon<=eta. Merely defining D_epsilon is
not such a certificate. Weak comparison is used deliberately: shifted
atomic energies do not converge in total variation even when their pole
locations converge correctly.

### S7.3 Pole-band resolution and angular/tensor error

For 0<delta<a/2 set

\[
 b_\delta(E,q)=\max\{1-|E-|q||/\delta,0\},\qquad
 R_{\varepsilon,\delta}(q)=\int b_\delta(E,q)\,d\sigma_\varepsilon(E,q).
\]

The pole is defined with epsilon->0 first and delta->0 second. It is NOT
estimated by choosing a single favorable finite-width bin after inspection.
Let sigma_reg=sigma_0-sigma_(0,pole). Require a controlled annular remainder

\[
 \sup_{q\in A_{ab}}\frac1{|q|}
 \left\|\int b_\delta(E,q)d\sigma_{\rm reg}(E,q)\right\|_{\rm op}
 \le\rho(\delta;a,b),\qquad \rho\to0,
\]

using essential suprema if the density is defined only almost everywhere.
This is a sufficient uniform comparison class chosen for this draft. A
non-atomic continuum whose threshold approaches the shell is not assumed
absent; it must satisfy this bound or be reported outside this uniform class.

Before angular averaging or transverse projection, and only after the
required momentum density is established, define

\[
 \Delta_{\varepsilon,\delta}(a,b)=
 \operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{q\in A_{ab}}
 \left\||q|^{-1}R_{\varepsilon,\delta}(q)
                 -Z_*\Pi_\gamma(q/|q|)\right\|_{\rm op}.
 \tag{S7-tensor}
\]

This tests unwanted longitudinal weight, wrong EB sign, unequal polarization
weights, and directional dependence as parts of one full-matrix error.
A successful quantitative attachment supplies a bound tending to zero in
the stipulated ordered limit. If uniform densities are unavailable, a
separately stated smeared-momentum version can be formulated before evidence;
it is not silently substituted into this uniform assertion.

A scalar anisotropy diagnostic may additionally report

\[
 \sup_{r\in[a,b],\ n\in S^2}
 \left|\operatorname{tr}R_{BB}(rn)/r-Z_*\right|,
\]

but cannot replace the matrix test. The full expected rotational tensor
still has direction dependence through n; that dependence is not lattice
anisotropy to be averaged away.

### S7.4 Finite-volume, statistical and reconstruction errors

Any later finite-data inference must separately bound finite-volume error,
infrared/scaling error, regular spectral contamination, time-reconstruction
error and statistical/sampler error. On a finite momentum grid compare only
actual grid values or explicitly defined smeared sums; do not invent an
interpolation that forces rotation symmetry.

Finite noiseless data do not uniquely prove an infinite-volume atom, and
finite noisy Euclidean data make analytic continuation an ill-conditioned
inverse problem. A later numerical claim needs an explicitly identified
inference class and error bounds. No production sizes, fit windows, error
thresholds, or #742 decision rules are changed by this draft.

**S7 output:** explicit control certificates at the claimed exact or numerical
scope. Missing controls mean STOP or unresolved finite-size evidence, not
proof of a mass gap and not confirmation of a photon.
