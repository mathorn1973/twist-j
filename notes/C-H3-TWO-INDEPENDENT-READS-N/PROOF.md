## 5. B: exact information and interface boundary

If a deterministic bijective closed update were
to map five distinct raw inputs with common other
initial slots to a fixed source, fixed worker and
only two final records, the complete image would
have size at most two. This contradicts injectivity.
One must retain further input distinctions somewhere,
for example in a restored data-containing source or
in a separate input-exchange reserve. This counting
implication is exact, independent of the above
bounded histories, and not a physical or entropy law.

The new negative result is more specific than the
general count: equality of all worker slots and
their accounts at an endpoint is insufficient to
establish repeatability of a local read operation.
The surrounding archive can change the next
incoming native CORR. For this device the explicit
11 counterexample occurs at relative6 and triggers
the parity refusal. A sufficient renewal condition
would be equality of the relevant future interface
inputs across prior archive histories for each new
input, together with the subsequent reset and
retained-record conditions. This condition is a
design target, not a proved necessary universal
clock architecture. A mere external reset of the
archive would erase or transfer the old information,
and is not evidence of native independent reuse.
