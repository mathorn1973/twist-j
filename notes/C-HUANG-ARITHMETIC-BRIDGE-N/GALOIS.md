## 1. Quadratic multiplication, conjugation and the cyclotomic action

Let \(j=\zeta_5\) in the distinguished embedding,
\(\varphi=-j^2-j^3\), \(\alpha=j+j^{-1}\), and \(F=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).
The relation \(1+j+j^2+j^3+j^4=0\) gives
\[
 \alpha=\varphi-1,\qquad \alpha^2=2-\varphi=1-\alpha,\qquad
 \alpha\varphi=1 .
\]
Multiplication by \(\alpha\) and by \(\tau=2\alpha+1\), respectively,
has the following matrices in the column basis \((1,\alpha)\) of
\(\mathbb Z[\alpha]\):
\[
 L=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 K=2L+I=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\2&-1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The identities for these matrices already appear in the registered
[GOLDEN-CLIFFORD-COIN-IDENTITY, T](https://github.com/mathorn1973/twist-j/blob/6f2e112aafc4f3aca4222bb4bf3dced1d79fe2ed/canon/CANON.md#L32319).
The identity \(\tau=j-j^2-j^3+j^4=2\varphi-1\), \(\tau^2=5\),
is part of the registered
[ALPHA-PREFACTOR-UNIFICATION, T](https://github.com/mathorn1973/twist-j/blob/6f2e112aafc4f3aca4222bb4bf3dced1d79fe2ed/canon/CANON.md#L23759).
The interpretation here uses the named multiplication basis and makes
no scattering or observable identification.

The nonidentity automorphism \(\sigma\) of \(F\) sends \(\alpha\) to
\(-1-\alpha\). Its column matrix and its composition law with \(K\) are
\[
 S=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 S^2=I,\qquad SK=-KS.
\]
Indeed, \(\sigma(\tau x)=\sigma(\tau)\sigma(x)=-\tau\sigma(x)\).
The trace product \(\operatorname{Tr}_{F/\mathbb Q}(xy)\) has positive
definite Gram matrix \(G\), and
\[
 G=\begin{pmatrix}2&-1\\-1&3\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 GK=\begin{pmatrix}0&5\\5&-5\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 GS=\begin{pmatrix}2&-1\\-1&-2\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Both operators are self-adjoint for \(G\). In the ordinary Euclidean
product on these coordinates \(K\) is symmetric and \(S\) is not.
After real scalar extension, if \(v\) is a trace-unit eigenvector
of \(K\) of eigenvalue \(+\sqrt5\), then \((v,Sv)\) is trace-orthonormal.
In this basis \(K/\sqrt5\) is \(Z=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)\) and \(S\)
is \(X=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}\).
Both \(S\) and \(K/\sqrt5\) are trace isometries because they are
self-adjoint involutions. Any two nonzero anticommuting real
self-adjoint operators squaring to positive scalar multiples of
\(I\) can be normalized and put in this form: the second involution
exchanges the first one's two eigenspaces. A third self-adjoint
operator anticommuting with \(Z\) must be a scalar multiple of \(X\).
Anticommutation with \(X\) then forces it to vanish. This capacity
bound concerns the fixed real space of dimension two.

In the displayed integral coordinates, the weight choice has a separate
elementary explanation. If one assumes \(K=aZ+bX\) with integers \(a,b\),
then \(K^2=5I\) gives \(a^2+b^2=5\). Integrality of \((K-I)/2\) forces
\(a\) odd and \(b\) even, hence \(|a|=1\), \(|b|=2\). The premise fixes
the matrix family. Anticommutation in the multiplication representation
uses conjugation. Two multiplication operators in \(F\) commute.

For the full cyclotomic module, \(j\) satisfies \(j^2-\alpha j+1=0\)
over \(F\), and \(\mathbb Z[j]=\mathbb Z[\alpha]\oplus
j\mathbb Z[\alpha]\). Generation follows from the quadratic relation
and \(\alpha\in\mathbb Z[j]\). Independence follows since \(j\) is
nonreal. The TWIST-J anchor \(J\varphi=j\) gives \(J=j\alpha\). In the
integral column basis \((1,\alpha,j,\alpha j)\) its multiplication
matrix is
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}0&-L\\L&L^2\end{pmatrix}
\]
by applying \(j^2=\alpha j-1\) to \(J(x+jy)\).
The extensions of \(\sigma\) to \(\mathbb Q(j)\) send \(j\) to \(j^2\)
and \(j^3\), respectively. Each has order four and its square is complex
conjugation. Thus the involution above acts on the real subfield.

Write \(g\) for the generator of \(C_5\) and
\(\Gamma=g+g^{-1}-g^2-g^{-2}\) in \(\mathbb Z[C_5]\). Collecting
coefficients using \(g^5=1\) gives
\[
 \Gamma^2=5-(1+g+g^2+g^3+g^4).
\]
The sum in parentheses annihilates the augmentation ideal. Evaluation
at \(g=j\) maps that ideal isomorphically onto
\((j-1)\mathbb Z[j]\). The kernel of evaluation on the full group ring
is the integer span of \(1+g+g^2+g^3+g^4\), which intersects the ideal
trivially. The image is generated by \(j-1\) because the ideal itself
is generated by \(g-1\). Under evaluation \(\Gamma\) maps to \(\tau\),
so its action is \(K\oplus K\) in the basis corresponding to
\((j-1)(1,\alpha,j,\alpha j)\).

## 2. Frobenius and an exact doubled record

For \(p\ne5\), the cyclotomic Frobenius automorphism sends \(j\) to
\(j^p\). The formula
\(\tau=\sum_{a\in(\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z)^\times}\chi_5(a)j^a\)
with \(\chi_5(a)=(a\mid5)\) gives
\[
 \sigma_p(\tau)=\chi_5(p)\tau
\]
by relabeling \(a\) by \(pa\). Its restriction to \(F\) is identity
when \(\chi_5(p)=+1\) and \(\sigma\) when it is \(-1\). These signs
distinguish primes such as \(2\) and \(11\), which both have Möbius
weight \(-1\). They also act invertibly, whereas \(\mu(p^2)=0\).
The squarefree support and parity must be encoded separately.

Here is an exact record on the actual finite coefficient inputs.
Write \(d^\circ=d/5^{v_5(d)}\), \(\eta(d)=\chi_5(d^\circ)\), and
let \(\sigma_d|_F\) act on \(\tau\) by \(\eta(d)\). At \(5\) this
defines the identity as a bookkeeping convention, with no Frobenius
claim. The function \(\eta\) is completely multiplicative, takes
values \(\pm1\), and separates ramification from the sign field.
For any fixed cut weights \(u_a=\mu(a)\mathbf 1_{a\le A}\) and
\(v_b=\mu(b)\mathbf 1_{b\le C}\), including \(C=A\), and any raw
denominator mask \(m(d)\in\{0,1\}\), set
\[
 b_d=m(d)\sum_{ab=d}u_a v_b,\qquad
 \mathbf b_d=b_d(1+\eta(d)\tau).
\]
For each pair \(ab=d\), the companion coefficient is
\((\eta(a)u_a)(\eta(b)v_b)\). Thus the first coordinate is exactly
the original sum and the second is exactly the product-twisted
convolution on the same mask. In field notation the first readout
is \(x\mapsto \operatorname{Tr}_{F/\mathbb Q}(x)/2\), and the second
is \(x\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}_{F/\mathbb Q}(\tau x)/10\). For a squarefree
factor, adding a new prime \(p\) to its divisor cube applies
\(-\sigma_p|_F\) to the fiber value \(\mu(a)(1+\eta(a)\tau)\).
The negation supplies Möbius parity and the automorphism supplies
the character sign. The update is an involution, so it also
represents deletion on that factor cube. The updates commute and
satisfy
\[
 (-\sigma_p|_F)K=\eta(p)K(-\sigma_p|_F).
\]
For cutoff cubes the same updates apply to each edge whose two
endpoints are retained. They describe signed coefficient transport
directly, without requiring the induced cube to have commuting
compressed toggles. More explicitly, on the full cube let \(T_p e_a\)
be \(e_{a\triangle p}\), where \(a\triangle p\) toggles the membership of
\(p\). Let \(P\) be the diagonal cutoff and \(U_p=-\sigma_p|_F\).
Then the signed exit is exactly
\[
 [P\otimes I,T_p\otimes U_p](e_a\otimes x)
  =\big(1_P(a\triangle p)-1_P(a)\big)e_{a\triangle p}\otimes U_px.
\]
Here \(1_P\) is the cutoff indicator. The two readouts of \(U_p x\)
are respectively minus the first coordinate and \(-\eta(p)\) times the
second. This preserves the actual sign of each crossed boundary in the
original readout. The new coloring appears in the companion coordinate.

The coordinate readouts commute with every linear time operation
used for these actual coefficients: applying a mask on raw \(d\),
primitive projection, forward averaging, subtracting the allocated
affine slope, and adding the allocated constant. Thus the signed
compensated carrier of [the pinned bilinear note](https://github.com/mathorn1973/twist-j/blob/ceb77e3632ec4617c54d8e1fd504d3b1c99dd106/notes/C-RH-THREE-INTERFACE-SYNTHESIS-N/BILINEAR-LOW.md)
is the first coordinate before its positive part is taken. The
companion separately has the same divisor majorant, because
\(|\eta(d)|=1\). The projection error bound from that note applies to
each coordinate with the same order. This equality describes a
linear readout of the entire signed carrier. In particular a positive
part of either coordinate alone cannot be substituted for the
positive part of an arbitrary readout.

## 3. Obstruction and the remaining boundary contract

Suppose \(T_p,T_q\) are commuting invertible operators on a real
arithmetic space, such as independent prime toggles on a full divisor
cube with a factor \(-1\) for Möbius parity. If a linear map \(D\)
intertwines each proposed Clifford step \(A_i\) from another real
space by \(DA_i=T_iD\), and \(A_pA_q=-A_qA_p\), then
\[
 T_pT_q D=DA_pA_q=-DA_qA_p=-T_qT_p D .
\]
Invertibility forces \(D=0\). This applies to a stepwise linear
intertwiner of two steps. It does not apply to compressed
noninvertible boundary steps or to readouts only at an endpoint.
The actual Galois updates above satisfy a mixed relation with \(K\). They commute with one another.

For the prior complete-window criterion, let \(\mathbf F_X\) be the
doubled compensated carrier on a slab, and \(\ell_0\) the first coordinate
readout just defined. A boundary construction would need an estimate
for its actual first component, including both signs of each weighted
exit, with the same slope and constant in the interior. One sufficient
contract is an error decomposition
\(\ell_0\mathbf F_X=G_X+E_X^{\rm bd}\) in which, on every sufficiently
late complete integer window, \(E^{\rm bd}\) has weighted norm
\(O_\epsilon(Y^\epsilon)\) for each \(\epsilon>0\), and \(G\) satisfies
the original arbitrarily-late positive-energy bound with its
quantifiers preserved. The weights here are \(1/[N(N+1)]\), with
each \(N\) assigned to its actual dyadic slab. A direct signed bound
for the pairing of the first component with nonnegative tests of
weighted norm one would also suffice. The Frobenius coloring fixes
the second component exactly but supplies neither estimate on the
first component. In particular an estimate only for its character
twist bounds the wrong readout. The boundary estimate and any
resulting prime rule from this representation remain **O**.
