# Minimal abelian CM selection contract

Status: **NON-CANONICAL FROZEN SELECTION CONTRACT**.

Authority baseline: **Public Canon v31**. The authority declaration remains
`STATUS.md`; this note changes no Canon claim, status, scope, dependency,
evidence, frontier item, or release metadata.

This document freezes the mathematical selection question that may be explored
manually in this notes directory. The owner approved the dependency split in
Section 5 on 2026-08-02. This is not a POLICY preregistration, a formal probe,
a public reproduction, or supplemental Lean evidence. In particular, the
labels `T-target`, `D-target`, and `H-target` below describe intended future
roles. They are not earned or registered public statuses.

## 1. Notation

Write \(C_2=\{+1,-1\}\) multiplicatively. For a finite Galois CM extension
\(K/\mathbb Q\), write

- \(G_K = \operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q)\);
- \(c_K\in G_K\) for canonical CM complex conjugation.

For any number field \(K\), `absDisc(K)` denotes the absolute field
discriminant \(\lvert D_K\rvert\). Here `Hom` always means group
homomorphisms.

The set \(\operatorname{Hom}(G_K,C_2)\) includes the trivial character. Thus
cardinality two means exactly one nontrivial quadratic character. All number
fields and all comparisons below are understood up to
\(\mathbb Q\)-isomorphism.

## 2. Admissible class

Define \(\mathcal A\) to consist of fields \(K/\mathbb Q\) satisfying all of
the following:

1. \(K/\mathbb Q\) is a finite Galois extension;
2. \(G_K\) is abelian;
3. \(K\) is a CM field;
4. \(\lvert\operatorname{Hom}(G_K,C_2)\rvert=2\); and
5. every quadratic character kills complex conjugation:

   \[
   \forall\chi:G_K\to C_2,\qquad \chi(c_K)=+1.
   \]

The unit-rank condition is deliberately absent. Positive unit rank is to be a
derived arithmetic consequence, not an admissibility premise.

## 3. Exact conditional theorem target

Define

\[
\operatorname{IsDiscMinimizer}(K)\;:\!\Longleftrightarrow\;
K\in\mathcal A\ \land\
\forall L,\ L\in\mathcal A\Longrightarrow
\operatorname{absDisc}(K)\le\operatorname{absDisc}(L).
\]

This is a property, not an `argmin` construction, and it asserts no existence
of a minimizer. The theorem target is

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{IsDiscMinimizer}(K)
\Longrightarrow
K\cong_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb Q(\zeta_5).
}
\]

Target classification: **T-target**, conditional on completing every stated
mathematical dependency. The physical assertion that Nature selects this
minimum is a separate **H-target**. No claim that \(J\) forces the selection is
included.

The Lean representation of this universal quantification is itself part of
the freeze. It must range over a bundled, universe-safe representation of
number fields up to \(\mathbb Q\)-isomorphism. Replacing the quantifier by a
finite catalogue of examples would weaken the theorem and is forbidden.

## 4. The four selection commitments

There are exactly four selection commitments. A defining restriction is not a
claim and has no status of its own; the last column falsifies its proposed
selection motivation, not the syntax of the definition.

| Commitment | Role in the contract | Intended classification | Rationale | Falsifier of the motivation or target |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Abelian CM extension | defining restriction | H-target motivation | closure under conjugation and character language without privileging one embedding branch | a nonabelian or non-CM field realizes the same architecture without an additional choice |
| Exactly one quadratic bit | defining restriction | H-target motivation | one binary orientation, with no choice among several nontrivial quadratic characters | a field with several bits canonically selects exactly one without extra data |
| Every quadratic bit kills \(c_K\) | defining restriction | H-target motivation | the bit is visible in a conjugation-invariant modular sector | an odd bit produces the same modular dichotomy |
| Minimal `absDisc(K)` | selection principle | H-target | choose the simplest admissible arithmetic house | some \(L\in\mathcal A\) has `absDisc(L) < 125`, or a non-isomorphic \(L\in\mathcal A\) also has `absDisc(L) = 125` |

The first three falsifiers are conceptual selection falsifiers. They are not
registry-ready until the corresponding notion of "same architecture" and the
claimed canonical choice have independent formal definitions.

## 5. Hypothesis-minimal arithmetic core

The general group-theoretic floor first records abelianization:

\[
\operatorname{Hom}(G,C_2)
\cong
\operatorname{Hom}(G^{\mathrm{ab}},C_2).
\]

This is why quadratic characters alone cannot control a nonabelian group. In
that setting, all such characters killing \(c\) implies only

\[
c\in [G,G]G^2,
\]

where \(G^2\) denotes the subgroup generated by all squares, not merely the
set of squares. This does not say that \(c\) itself has a square root of order
four. Abelianity is therefore an essential boundary of the following
argument.

Within that boundary, the proof graph must expose a redundancy that is easy
to hide in the phrase "one even bit." For an abelian group \(G\), classically,

\[
\bigcap_{\chi:G\to C_2}\ker\chi=G^2,
\]

or additively, the annihilator of all maps
\(G\to\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\) is \(2G\). Consequently,

\[
c\ne1,\qquad c^2=1,\qquad
\forall\chi:G\to C_2,\ \chi(c)=+1
\Longrightarrow
\exists\tau\in G,
\quad \operatorname{ord}(\tau)=4,
\quad \tau^2=c.
\]

Every \(\chi:G\to C_2\) in this group-theoretic section denotes an ordinary
group homomorphism, not a Dirichlet character. In Lean this is represented
multiplicatively by `MonoidHom` (`→*`) or additively by `AddMonoidHom` (`→+`);
Mathlib has no separate `GroupHom` type.

Neither finiteness nor
\(\lvert\operatorname{Hom}(G,C_2)\rvert=2\) is needed for this square-root
lemma. For finite \(G\), it immediately gives

\[
4\mid\lvert G\rvert.
\]

The original public-facing target remains a required corollary:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&G\text{ finite abelian},\quad
\lvert\operatorname{Hom}(G,C_2)\rvert=2,\\
&c\ne1,\quad c^2=1,\quad
\forall\chi:G\to C_2,\ \chi(c)=+1\\
&\hspace{3em}\Longrightarrow
\operatorname{Syl}_2(G)\cong C_{2^e}\text{ for some }e\ge2,\\
&\hspace{8em}\text{and}\quad
\exists\tau,\quad \operatorname{ord}(\tau)=4,\quad \tau^2=c.
\end{aligned}}
\]

No uniqueness of \(\tau\) is asserted; in general there can be two roots of
order four.

The proposed hypothesis-minimal internal theorem should use the additive
statement with no finiteness or character-cardinality hypothesis:

```lean
theorem exists_addOrderOf_four_of_all_quadratic_chars_vanish
    {G : Type*} [AddCommGroup G] (c : G)
    (hc_ne : c ≠ 0)
    (hc_two : 2 • c = 0)
    (hχ : ∀ χ : G →+ ZMod 2, χ c = 0) :
    ∃ τ : G, addOrderOf τ = 4 ∧ 2 • τ = c
```

A multiplicative wrapper may later translate this to \(\tau^2=c\). The
finite exactly-one-bit theorem is a separate result and may be used to audit
the selection predicate, not to prove this statement.

Freezing this dependency split does not remove or weaken the boxed
unique-even-bit corollary; it prevents an unused hypothesis from being hidden
inside its proof.

At order four, the element \(\tau\) could independently exclude
\(C_2\times C_2\). The frozen selection theorem nevertheless retains the
exactly-one-bit condition as an explicit premise and uses it in the selected
degree-four classification path:

\[
G\cong C_4\text{ or }C_2\times C_2,\qquad
\lvert\operatorname{Hom}(C_2\times C_2,C_2)\rvert=4.
\]

Thus cardinality two is not a premise of the square-root lemma, degree
divisibility, or positive unit rank. It remains a premise of the overall
selection theorem and the stated proof input that excludes the Klein group.
This deliberately records both selection commitments even though the retained
quarter-turn witness also supplies a stronger cross-check at that node. The
implementation should prove the stronger core theorem first and expose the
theorem for \(\mathcal A\) as a corollary.

The separate finite-abelian classification target remains useful:

\[
\lvert\operatorname{Hom}(G,C_2)\rvert=2
\Longleftrightarrow
\operatorname{Syl}_2(G)
\text{ is a nontrivial cyclic }2\text{-group}.
\]

It audits the exactly-one-bit selection condition, not the quarter-turn
implication.

## 6. Unit rank and the physical boundary

For a CM field of degree \(2m\), Dirichlet's unit theorem gives

\[
\operatorname{rank}\mathcal O_K^\times=m-1.
\]

The even-conjugation lemma gives \(4\mid[K:\mathbb Q]\), hence \(m\ge2\) and

\[
K\in\mathcal A
\Longrightarrow
\operatorname{rank}\mathcal O_K^\times\ge1.
\]

This is a **T-target** arithmetic statement. It proves infinitude of the unit
group. It does not select a fundamental unit and makes no claim about physical
motion, scale, or time.

The proposed reading

\[
\text{a non-torsion unit direction}
\longmapsto
\text{a physical scale or motion}
\]

is a separate **D-target** bridge. Its falsifier is either a rank-zero system
with the same nontrivial scale update, or a positive-rank system from which no
canonical physical step can be selected.

`CM-UNIT-RANK-POSITIVE` and `UNIT-RANK-SCALE-READING` are proposed labels only.
Neither identifier occurs in the Public Canon v31 registry, and this note does
not reserve or register either one.

## 7. Mandatory regression suite

The following examples are part of the frozen contract. A later definition
must reproduce these outcomes rather than narrow itself around the target.

1. **Imaginary quadratic fields.** Their unit rank is zero. Their Galois group
   is \(C_2\), and its unique nontrivial quadratic character is odd on complex
   conjugation. They illustrate both the absence of a non-torsion unit axis and
   the need to derive rank only after the even-conjugation condition.

2. **\(\mathbb Q(\zeta_8)\) and \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_{12})\).** Each has Galois
   group \(C_2\times C_2\), hence
   \(\lvert\operatorname{Hom}(G,C_2)\rvert=4\). Both fail the exactly-one-bit
   condition.

3. **\(\mathbb Q(\zeta_7)\).** Its Galois group is \(C_6\), so the character
   group has cardinality two, but the unique nontrivial quadratic character
   satisfies \(\chi(c)=-1\). It passes uniqueness and fails evenness.

4. **\(\mathbb Q(\zeta_{13})\).** Its Galois group is \(C_{12}\), its unique
   nontrivial quadratic character is even, and it belongs to \(\mathcal A\).
   Its absolute discriminant is \(13^{11}\), so it is removed only by the
   discriminant comparison.

5. **The quartic field of discriminant 117.** Let

   \[
   K_{117}=\mathbb Q[x]/(x^4-x^3-x^2+x+1).
   \]

   It has signature \((0,2)\), absolute discriminant \(117\), and unit rank
   one, but it is not Galois over \(\mathbb Q\); its normal closure has group
   \(D_4\). It therefore fails before the character conditions. Total
   imaginarity and positive unit rank do not imply admissibility. See
   [LMFDB 4.0.117.1](https://www.lmfdb.org/NumberField/4.0.117.1).

Positive control: \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_5)\) is an abelian CM field with Galois
group \(C_4\), exactly one nontrivial quadratic character, even quadratic bit,
and absolute discriminant \(125\).

## 8. Frozen proof checkpoints

The intended arithmetic route uses only the following stable checkpoints.

1. The positive control proves every minimizer has absolute discriminant at
   most \(125\).
2. For a totally imaginary field of degree \(n\), Minkowski gives

   \[
   \lvert D_K\rvert\ge
   \left(\frac\pi4\right)^n
   \frac{n^{2n}}{(n!)^2}.
   \]

   Using only \(\pi>3\), put

   \[
   M(n)=\left(\frac34\right)^n\frac{n^{2n}}{(n!)^2}.
   \]

   Since \(\pi>3\), the displayed Minkowski bound gives the strict comparison
   \(\lvert D_K\rvert>M(n)\).

   The exact checkpoint and ratio are

   \[
   M(8)=\frac{21233664}{1225}>125,
   \qquad
   \frac{M(n+1)}{M(n)}=
   \frac34\left(1+\frac1n\right)^{2n}\ge3.
   \]

   Together with \(4\mid n\), this reduces every candidate with
   \(\lvert D_K\rvert\le125\) to \(n=4\).
3. For the frozen target proof, the quartic-character module proves the
   standard dichotomy \(G_K\cong C_4\) or \(C_2\times C_2\), together with
   \(\lvert\operatorname{Hom}(C_2\times C_2,C_2)\rvert=4\). The unique-bit
   premise therefore excludes the Klein group and yields \(G_K\cong C_4\).
   The previously constructed quarter-turn is an independent stronger
   cross-check of the same conclusion, not a reason to erase the selection
   premise from the target theorem.
4. The isolated conductor block must supply all four results below. None is a
   definitional choice.

   **G1 — character correspondence.** Prove the existence of a primitive
   Dirichlet character \(\psi\) of exact order four whose kernel cuts out
   \(K\); equivalently, its induced character on
   \(\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q)\) is faithful. The two induced faithful
   characters are \(\psi\) and \(\psi^{-1}\). This is a theorem dependency,
   not an instruction to "choose a faithful character" on an arbitrary full
   unit group.

   **G2 — conductor-discriminant formula.** Prove

   \[
   \lvert D_K\rvert
   =f(\psi)f(\psi^2)f(\psi^3)
   =f(\psi)^2f(\psi^2).
   \]

   Here \(f(\psi^j)\) means the conductor of the primitive character
   associated to \(\psi^j\); in particular, \(\psi^2\) may be imprimitive at
   a displayed modulus.

   **G3 — small conductors.** Prove \(f(\psi)\ge5\) by excluding characters
   of order four at conductors \(1,2,3,4\). Put
   \(\varepsilon=\psi^2\). Prove \(f(\varepsilon)\ge5\) by excluding a
   nontrivial even quadratic primitive character at those same conductors.
   The parity bookkeeping is

   \[
   \psi(c)=-1,
   \qquad
   \varepsilon(c)=+1.
   \]

   The faithful quartic character \(\psi\) and quadratic bit
   \(\varepsilon\) must not be conflated.

   **G4 — equality.** Deduce \(\lvert D_K\rvert\ge125\). Equality forces
   \(f(\psi)=f(\varepsilon)=5\), and hence
   \(K\cong_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb Q(\zeta_5)\). This conclusion is a
   \(\mathbb Q\)-isomorphism; it is not literal equality of embedded subfields
   unless an embedding has first been fixed.

The conductor-discriminant block is the final theorem dependency. Until G1
through G4 are proved from accepted Mathlib dependencies, there is no local
`axiom`, `sorry`, `admit`, unlabelled "known fact", hidden instance, or
unconditional `ZetaFiveMinimal` conclusion. In particular, the
conductor-discriminant formula may not be replaced by an unnamed local
assumption. A conditional integration theorem taking G1--G4 as visible,
explicit hypotheses is permitted, but it carries no unconditional minimality
claim.

## 10. Explicit boundary at \(J\)

This program ends, conditionally, at the arithmetic identification

\[
K\cong_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb Q(\zeta_5).
\]

It does not derive \(J\). A separate bridge must first define a logarithmic
unit length, a mixed unit with non-torsion modulus and nontrivial phase, phase
primitivity, contraction orientation, and the equivalence relation generated
by Galois action, inversion, and central sign. Only then may it test

\[
J=\zeta_5\varphi^{-1}
\]

as a shortest oriented mixed step. That bridge remains a `D/H-target`; it is
neither a theorem consequence of admissibility nor part of the minimality
formalization.
