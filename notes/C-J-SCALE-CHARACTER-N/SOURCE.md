The present scope fixes an odd prime $q$ and uses the branch labels $(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^\times/\{\pm1\}$. The difference theorem uses two distinct labels and divides by the gap at input one. The product theorem uses the specified positive squared moduli. Its length condition is divisibility by the order of doubling on the label group. Its finer condition is equality of multiplicities within each doubling cycle.

## Published and instructional sources

- Brian Conrad, *Quadratic characters associated to quadratic fields*, §1, describes the quadratic character as restriction of cyclotomic Galois automorphisms. Keith Conrad, *Gauss and Jacobi sums on finite fields and Z/mZ*, Lemma 3.10, gives the Fourier identity for primitive characters, including its nonunit zero. These sources supply the classical comparison for the reader constructed from $J_n$. [Galois restriction](https://math.stanford.edu/~conrad/252Page/handouts/quadchar.pdf) · [Gauss sums](https://kconrad.math.uconn.edu/blurbs/gradnumthy/Gauss-Jacobi-sums.pdf)
- Allouche and Shallit, *The ubiquitous Prouhet–Thue–Morse sequence*, §2, give the initial word, digit-parity recursion and generating product. Grabner, *Completely q-multiplicative functions: the Mellin transform approach*, Acta Arith. 65 (1993), §3, Eq. (3.2), gives the block Fourier product for primes with primitive root 2. Gazeau and Verger-Gaugry, J. Théor. Nombres Bordeaux 20 (2008), Proposition 4.1 and §5.4.1, give the transfer and eigenvalues for that class, including five. For the original Goldstein–Kelly–Speer transfer, the sources consulted were their abstract and the attribution in the 2008 article. [Allouche–Shallit](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~shallit/Papers/ubiq15.pdf) · [Grabner](https://matwbn.icm.edu.pl/ksiazki/aa/aa65/aa6516.pdf) · [Gazeau–Verger-Gaugry](https://www.numdam.org/item/JTNB_2008__20_3_673_0.pdf) · [Goldstein–Kelly–Speer](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-314X(92)90104-W)
- Franz, *Über die Torsion einer Überdeckung*, J. Reine Angew. Math. 173 (1935), 245–254, is the historical source. The scan was located. Wang, *Cosmetic surgeries on genus one knots*, Algebr. Geom. Topol. 6 (2006), Theorem 3.2, supplies the checked statement with symmetric integer exponents, zero sum and the identity at every $m$-th root other than $1$. At prime $m$ those roots are Galois conjugate. Siksek and Visser, Algebra Number Theory 19 (2025), Lemma 9, supply an alternative basis modulo roots of unity via Washington's finite-index theorem. The cycle classification uses independence. The length proof uses residue reduction and telescoping.
