**Date:** 2026-09-11.

This note leaves the solved nondegenerate self-power family and opens the first
exceptional Hodge lane that uses the native TWIST-J CM field itself:

\[
K=\mathbf Q(j)=\mathbf Q(\zeta_5),
\qquad K^+=\mathbf Q(\sqrt5).
\]

No `canon/` file is changed, no public claim is registered, and no formal
public probe is started.

The point is not to claim progress on the Hodge conjecture. The point is to
construct an exact TWIST-seeded abelian variety of generalized Weil type for
`K`, identify its Weil-Hodge subspace exactly, and locate the first deformation
where those classes become exceptional, i.e. no longer lie in the algebra
generated by divisors.

---

## 1. Why dimension eight is the first direct CM5 exceptional lane

Let `A` be an abelian variety admitting an embedding

\[
\eta:K\hookrightarrow \operatorname{End}_{\mathbf Q}(A).
\]

Put

\[
d=\dim_K H^1(A,\mathbf Q),
\qquad e=[K:\mathbf Q]=4.
\]

For an abelian variety of generalized Weil type one requires, for every
embedding `sigma:K->C`,

\[
\dim H^{1,0}_{\sigma}
=
\dim H^{0,1}_{\sigma}
=
\frac d2.
\]

The associated Hodge-Weil space is

\[
HW(A,\eta)
=
\bigwedge_K^d H^1(A,\mathbf Q)
\subset H^d(A,\mathbf Q).
\]

It is one-dimensional over `K`, hence four-dimensional over `Q`, and is of
Hodge type `(d/2,d/2)`.

If `d=2`, then `dim A=de/2=4` and the Weil classes lie in `H^2`, so they are
ordinary `(1,1)` classes and therefore divisors. To reach classes beyond the
divisor level one needs

\[
d\ge4.
\]

The smallest case is therefore

\[
\boxed{d=4,\qquad \dim A=8,\qquad HW(A,\eta)\subset H^4(A,\mathbf Q).}
\]

Thus an abelian eightfold is the first place where the native quartic field
`Q(zeta_5)` can itself support generalized Weil classes in codimension two.

## 2. The TWIST CM surface used as the seed

Let

\[
B=\operatorname{Jac}(y^2=x^5-1).
\]

The previous notes identify

\[
H_1(B,\mathbf Z)\simeq \mathcal O_K
\]

with the order-five automorphism acting as multiplication by `j`, and the
principal polarization represented by the public form `Omega_1`.

The CM type is

\[
\Phi=\{\sigma_1,\sigma_2\},
\qquad
\sigma_a(j)=j^a.
\]

Let

\[
\tau\in\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbf Q),
\qquad
\tau(j)=j^2.
\]

Then `tau` has order four.

Gate E proved that every Hodge class on every self-power `B^n` is generated by
divisor classes. In particular this remains true for `B^4`.

That fact will become the special-point side of the deformation below.

## 3. Four Galois-twisted copies

Set

\[
A_0=B_0\times B_1\times B_2\times B_3,
\]

with each `B_k` a copy of `B`.

Define a single `K`-action on `A_0` by

\[
\eta(x)|_{B_k}=\tau^k(x),
\qquad x\in K,
\qquad k=0,1,2,3.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\eta(x)=\operatorname{diag}
\bigl(x,\tau(x),\tau^2(x),\tau^3(x)\bigr).
}
\]

Each summand `H^1(B_k,Q)` is rank one over `K` for this twisted action.
Therefore

\[
\boxed{\dim_K H^1(A_0,\mathbf Q)=4.}
\]

This is exactly the required `d=4` case.

---

## 4. Exact balanced-signature census

On the untwisted surface `B`, the holomorphic `K`-characters are

\[
\{1,2\}
\]

in exponent notation for `sigma_a(j)=j^a`.

On copy `B_k`, twisting the action by `tau^k` multiplies those labels by
`2^k mod 5`. Hence the holomorphic character sets are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
k & H^{1,0}(B_k)\text{ labels}\\
\hline
0 & \{1,2\}\\
1 & \{2,4\}\\
2 & \{4,3\}\\
3 & \{3,1\}.
\end{array}
\]

Every embedding `sigma_a`, `a=1,2,3,4`, therefore occurs exactly twice in
`H^{1,0}(A_0)`.

Its complex conjugate occurrence count in `H^{0,1}` is also exactly two.
Thus, for every embedding `sigma` of `K`,

\[
\boxed{
\dim H^{1,0}_{\sigma}(A_0)
=
\dim H^{0,1}_{\sigma}(A_0)
=2.
}
\]

So

\[
\boxed{(A_0,\eta)\text{ is of generalized Weil type for }K=\mathbf Q(\zeta_5).}
\]

This is an exact character count. No period calculation is involved.

## 5. Polarization compatibility

Give `A_0` the product of the four canonical principal polarizations of `B`.
On each copy the Rosati involution restricts to complex conjugation on `K`.
Because the Galois group of `K/Q` is abelian,

\[
\overline{\tau^k(x)}=\tau^k(\bar x).
\]

Hence the product Rosati involution satisfies

\[
\eta(x)^\dagger=\eta(\bar x).
\]

Therefore the product polarization is compatible with the displayed
`K`-action in the standard Weil-type sense.

This establishes a concrete polarized generalized-Weil seed, not merely a
balanced abstract Hodge structure.

## 6. The four-dimensional Weil-Hodge space

Since `d=4`, define

\[
HW_0
:=
HW(A_0,\eta)
=
\bigwedge_K^4H^1(A_0,\mathbf Q).
\]

It is one-dimensional over `K`, hence

\[
\boxed{\dim_{\mathbf Q}HW_0=4.}
\]

After complexification, write

\[
H^1(A_0,\mathbf C)
=
\bigoplus_{a=1}^4 H^1_{\sigma_a}.
\]

Each `H^1_{sigma_a}` has complex dimension four, two holomorphic and two
antiholomorphic dimensions. Thus

\[
HW_0\otimes\mathbf C
=
\bigoplus_{a=1}^4
\bigwedge^4 H^1_{\sigma_a}.
\]

Each summand is one-dimensional and has Hodge type `(2,2)`. Hence

\[
\boxed{
HW_0\subset H^4(A_0,\mathbf Q)\cap H^{2,2}(A_0).
}
\]

The four complex lines are permuted transitively by the arithmetic `C4`
Galois action.

## 7. An explicit basis of the complexified Weil space

For fixed `a` and copy `k`, let

\[
v_{a,k}
\]

be a nonzero vector in the unique `sigma_a` eigenspace inside
`H^1(B_k,C)` for the twisted `K`-action.

Then

\[
w_a
=
v_{a,0}\wedge v_{a,1}\wedge v_{a,2}\wedge v_{a,3}
\]

spans the `sigma_a` component of `HW_0 tensor C`.

To compare with the original untwisted `B` weights, note that on copy `k` the
underlying untwisted character is

\[
b_k=a\,2^{-k}\pmod5.
\]

Since

\[
(2^{-k})_{k=0}^3=(1,3,4,2)\pmod5,
\]

the four underlying characters in `w_a` are

\[
\{a,3a,4a,2a\}=\{1,2,3,4\}\pmod5.
\]

Thus every `w_a` contains exactly one copy of each original CM weight.
With the Gate-E weight notation

\[
\varepsilon_1,\quad
\varepsilon_2,\quad
-\varepsilon_2,\quad
-\varepsilon_1,
\]

the total weight is exactly zero.

Moreover the four factors pair canonically into the two conjugate pairs

\[
(1,4),\qquad(2,3).
\]

Therefore, over `C`, each `w_a` factors as a product of two degree-two
weight-zero `(1,1)` classes.

This gives a direct local explanation for the next fact.

## 8. At the TWIST CM seed the Weil classes are not exceptional

The seed is

\[
A_0=B^4.
\]

Gate E proved for every self-power of `B` that the complete rational Hodge
algebra is generated by divisor classes.

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
HW_0\subset
\mathbf Q[\operatorname{Hdg}^1(A_0)].
}
\]

So at this highly symmetric CM point the four-dimensional Weil-Hodge space is
fully decomposable in the divisor algebra.

This is load-bearing:

> **Weil class does not mean exceptional class at every special point.**

The exceptional phenomenon appears only after one removes the extra CM
symmetry while preserving the Weil-type `K` action.

## 9. The deformation out of the CM point

Keep fixed:

1. the field `K=Q(zeta_5)`;
2. the rank-four `K`-module structure on `H^1`;
3. the compatible polarization type;
4. the balanced signature `(2,2)` at every embedding.

Now deform the Hodge structure away from the special decomposable point
`A_0=B^4` inside the corresponding polarized Weil-type moduli space.

For

\[
e=[K:\mathbf Q]=4,
\qquad d=4,
\]

the standard dimension formula gives

\[
\boxed{
\dim_{\mathbf C}\mathcal M_{K,d}
=
\frac{ed^2}{8}
=8.
}
\]

Thus the TWIST CM seed lies inside an eight-complex-dimensional Weil-type
deformation space.

At a generic point of this moduli space the Neron-Severi rank is

\[
\boxed{e/2=2,}
\]

whereas the Weil-Hodge space remains

\[
\boxed{\dim_{\mathbf Q}HW=4.}
\]

The generic structural theorem for polarized Weil type gives

\[
HW(A,\eta)
\cap
\mathbf Q[H^{1,1}(A,\mathbf Q)]
=0.
\]

Therefore the same four-dimensional `K`-exterior-power package that was
absorbed by divisor products at the CM seed becomes an **exceptional** Hodge
subspace after generic Weil-type deformation:

\[
\boxed{
HW(A,\eta)\not\subset
\mathbf Q[\operatorname{Hdg}^1(A)].
}
\]

This is the first direct CM5 lane in the present programme where extra Hodge
classes survive but divisor generation fails.

## 10. What changed, and what did not

The important transition is

```text
special CM seed B^4
    K action present
    HW dimension 4
    many extra divisors
    HW absorbed into divisor algebra

        | deform preserving K-Weil type
        v

generic K-Weil eightfold
    K action still present
    HW dimension 4 still present
    generic divisor rank only 2
    HW intersects divisor algebra trivially
```

The exceptional classes are therefore not inserted by hand.

They are already present at the TWIST-seeded CM point as an exact
`K`-exterior-power subspace. What changes is the supply of divisor classes
available to decompose them.

This is a much sharper bridge than merely observing that `Q(zeta_5)` occurs in
Hodge theory.

## 11. Relation to degenerate CM

The seed `B^4` is highly nongeneric and has a much smaller Hodge group than a
generic polarized eightfold. It is built from one nondegenerate CM surface and
is therefore imprimitive as a higher-dimensional CM object.

The present construction deliberately avoids claiming that this special point
is the sought simple primitive degenerate CM example.

Instead it gives something more controlled for TWIST-J:

\[
\boxed{
\text{native CM5 carrier}
\longrightarrow
\text{explicit generalized-Weil package}
\longrightarrow
\text{generic exceptional Hodge classes}.
}
\]

A later lane may still search for a simple primitive degenerate CM point inside
or adjacent to this moduli problem.

## 12. Current algebraicity status and why this remains interesting

Exceptional means **not generated by divisors**. It does not mean
non-algebraic.

Recent work has substantially advanced the algebraicity of Weil classes. In
particular, the 2026 version of Markman's *Secant sheaves and Weil classes on
abelian varieties* states algebraicity for all abelian fourfolds of Weil type
and for split imaginary-quadratic sixfold cases, and presents a general
strategy for CM fields.

The same paper explicitly says that the full implementation discussed there is
for low-dimensional cases with an imaginary quadratic CM field, while the
general CM-field construction is broader.

The present lane has

\[
[K:\mathbf Q]=4,
\qquad
\dim A=8,
\qquad
d=4.
\]

Thus it is **not** one of the imaginary-quadratic fourfold cases whose
algebraicity is already supplied by that theorem.

This note does not claim that the algebraicity problem for this exact
quartic-CM eightfold family is open in the complete literature. It makes the
narrower and auditable statement:

> the algebraicity of this four-dimensional generalized-Weil subspace is not
> supplied by the low-dimensional imaginary-quadratic theorem used as the
> current external benchmark.

That makes it a legitimate next comparison target.

## 13. The next exact gate: splitness

For a polarized generalized-Weil variety the compatible polarization defines a
`K`-valued Hermitian form

\[
H_K:H_1(A,\mathbf Q)\times H_1(A,\mathbf Q)\to K.
\]

The variety is of **split Weil type** if this rank-four Hermitian space contains
a totally isotropic `K`-subspace of dimension two.

The next exact gate is therefore:

### Gate W1: CM5 Hermitian split test

For the explicit seed `(A_0,eta,h_0)` above:

1. write the product polarization as an exact `K`-Hermitian `4 x 4` matrix in
   the four Galois-twisted `K` summands;
2. determine its determinant/discriminant in
   `K^{+x}/N_{K/K+}(K^x)`;
3. decide exactly whether it admits a two-dimensional totally isotropic
   `K`-plane;
4. if split, give an explicit isotropic basis;
5. identify whether the resulting datum is equivalent to the split
   `X x Xhat` construction used in the modern Weil-class deformation theory.

A positive result would place the TWIST-seeded eightfold directly on the
split-Weil lane used by current algebraicity methods.

A negative result would be equally useful: it would show that this exact
TWIST seed lies in a different component and would force a different
algebraicity route.

## 14. Breakers and nonclaims

The construction above is falsified if any of the following fails:

1. one of the four `K` embeddings does not occur exactly twice in `H^{1,0}`;
2. `H^1(A_0,Q)` is not rank four over the displayed `K` action;
3. the product Rosati involution fails to restrict to complex conjugation on
   `K`;
4. `HW_0` is not four-dimensional over `Q` or fails to lie in `(2,2)`;
5. the Gate-E divisor-generation theorem fails when applied to `B^4`;
6. the generic generalized-Weil theorem does not give the stated rank and
   trivial intersection with the divisor algebra at `e=d=4`.

No such breaker is presently seen.

The note does **not** claim:

- algebraicity of the generic four-dimensional `HW` space;
- a new theorem on the Hodge conjecture;
- that the seed is simple;
- that the seed is already proved split;
- that the eightfold is selected by the physical TWIST decoder;
- any L1-to-L6 physical lift.

## 15. Verdict

Gate E showed that the original CM5 surface and all of its ordinary
self-powers are too nondegenerate to expose exceptional Hodge classes.

The Galois-twisted four-copy construction changes that conclusion without
leaving the native field:

\[
\boxed{
A_0=B^4,
\qquad
\eta(x)=\operatorname{diag}
(x,\tau x,\tau^2x,\tau^3x),
\qquad
\tau(j)=j^2.
}
\]

It produces a polarized generalized-Weil eightfold with

\[
\boxed{
HW_0=\bigwedge_K^4 H^1(A_0,Q),
\qquad
\dim_Q HW_0=4,
\qquad
HW_0\subset H^{2,2}.
}
\]

At the TWIST CM seed those classes are divisor-generated. In the generic
`K`-Weil deformation they persist while the divisor algebra shrinks, and the
same four-dimensional space becomes exceptional.

So the direct route is now

```text
TWIST CM5 surface
 -> four Galois-twisted copies
 -> exact Q(zeta5)-Weil eightfold
 -> four Weil-Hodge classes
 -> generic exceptional deformation
 -> splitness test
 -> algebraicity test.
```

That is a genuine next Hodge lane for the programme.
