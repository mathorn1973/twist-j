# What a fixed affine unit test can read inside one factor

**NON-CANONICAL. No authority. All deductions are candidate-D, L1.**
This is a shared-context paper argument for [claim #2090](https://github.com/mathorn1973/twist-j/issues/2090).

Let $\mathcal O=\mathbf Z[j]$, $j=\zeta_5$, $K=\mathbf Q(j)$,
$\phi=-j^2-j^3$, $\alpha=\phi^2$, $m=5^k$ with $k\ge1$, $z=\phi^m$,
and $f=F_m$. For $\sigma\in\{-1,1\}$ put $A_\sigma=5f^2+\sigma5f+1$.
For $Y\in\mathcal O$, its content is the nonnegative gcd of its coordinates
in an integral basis, with $\operatorname{cont}(0)=0$.

A fixed test is a Laurent polynomial $P(Z)\in\mathcal O[Z,Z^{-1}]$,
evaluated at $Z=z$. This includes every finite sum of signed units
$\epsilon_i j^{c_i}\phi^{u_i m+v_i}$ with fixed integer data, including
negative slopes and offsets. The flag at a prime $p$ is divisibility by the
whole ideal $p\mathcal O$.

## The exact dichotomy [candidate-D]

Define the monic quartic
\[
 Q_\sigma(Z)=(Z^2+\sigma\phi^{-1}Z+1)(Z^2+\sigma\phi Z+1)
 =Z^4+\sigma\sqrt5\, Z^3+3Z^2+\sigma\sqrt5\, Z+1 .
\]
For each choice of sign the following alternatives are exhaustive.

1. If $Q_\sigma$ divides $P$ in $\mathcal O[Z,Z^{-1}]$, then
   $\gcd(A_\sigma,\operatorname{cont}P(z))=A_\sigma$ for every $k\ge1$.
2. Otherwise there is a fixed, nonzero integer $M=M(P,\sigma)$ such that
   $\gcd(A_\sigma,\operatorname{cont}P(z))$ divides $|M|$ for every $k\ge1$.
   This gcd equals one for all sufficiently large $k$.

Thus a finite list of these tests eventually gives identical flags at every
prime dividing the same side. The two sides can have different flags.

### The four roots and the four coordinates

Use $\chi(a)=(a/5)$ for $a\in\{1,2,3,4\}$. The four roots of $Q_\sigma$
in $\mathcal O$ are
\[
 c=\delta j^a,\qquad \delta\in\{-1,1\},\quad
 \delta\chi(a)=-\sigma.
\]
Indeed $j+j^{-1}=\phi^{-1}$, $j^2+j^{-2}=-\phi$, and conjugate pairs
in this list give the two quadratic factors. As $m$ is odd,
$z+z^{-1}=\sqrt5 f$, so
\[
 Q_\sigma(z)/z^2=(z+z^{-1})^2+\sigma\sqrt5(z+z^{-1})+1=A_\sigma.
\]

We recall the order calculation for this tower. Put $w=z^2$ and
$t=w+w^{-1}=5f^2-2$. Then
$w^{-2}\Phi_5(w)=t^2+t-1=A_+A_-$. Both factors are odd and one
modulo five. Also $t(t+1)$ cannot be one modulo three, so every prime
$p\mid A_\sigma$ exceeds five. Modulo $p\mathcal O$, $w^5=1$ and
$w\ne1$ since $\Phi_5(1)=5$. Every proper divisor of $5m$ divides $m$,
so $\alpha$ has order $5m$. In the quadratic algebra generated by $\phi$,
the norm-one group has order $p-\chi(p)$, where $\chi(p)=(p/5)$.
This follows from the quadratic field norm in the inert case and the
pairs $(x,x^{-1})$ in the split case. The algebra embeds in
$\mathcal O/p\mathcal O$ by the basis $1,\phi$.
Consequently $5\mid p-\chi(p)$. This implies $p\equiv\pm1\pmod5$,
hence $\chi(p)=1$ and $p\equiv1\pmod5$. In particular $p$ splits
completely in $\mathcal O$, and $10m\mid p-1$ since $5m$ is odd.
[Earlier order and factorization argument](https://github.com/mathorn1973/twist-j/pull/2088).

To describe the four coordinates of $\mathcal O/p\mathcal O$, let one
coordinate send $j$ to a primitive fifth root $y\in\mathbf F_p$. The other
three send $j$ to $y^{-1},y^2,y^{-2}$. Each coordinate has $w$ of order
five, since the two real components of $w$ are inverses. In the first
coordinate the value of $z$ has the unique form $\delta y^a$ with
$a\not\equiv0\pmod5$. At $y^{-1}$ the value of $z$ is the same. At
$y^2$ and $y^{-2}$ the value is $-z^{-1}$, because the real conjugate
of $\phi$ is $-\phi^{-1}$ and $m$ is odd. Relative to the image of $j$
in each coordinate, the four pairs are therefore
\[
 (\delta,a),\quad(\delta,-a),\quad(-\delta,2a),\quad(-\delta,-2a)
 \qquad (a\bmod5).
\]
These pairs are distinct. Since $Q_\sigma(z)=0$ in the whole quotient,
each pair gives a root of $Q_\sigma$ in its coordinate. The eight
elements $\pm y^b$, $1\le b\le4$, are distinct in characteristic $p>5$.
Thus these four pairs exhaust the four listed roots for this sign.
This is why testing a general coefficient from $\mathcal O$, even with
nontrivial fifth-root phase, requires the whole quotient.

### Divisibility with multiplicity

If $P$ vanishes on all four roots, clear a Laurent monomial denominator.
Over $K$ the resulting polynomial is divisible by $Q_\sigma$, since the
roots are distinct. Monic polynomial division gives the quotient in
$\mathcal O[Z]$. Conversely divisibility implies all four vanish.
In this case $Q_\sigma(z)=z^2 A_\sigma$ proves the first alternative
with all multiplicities.

Otherwise choose a root $c$ with $P(c)\ne0$ and take
$M=N_{K/\mathbf Q}(P(c))$. Let $p^e$ divide both $A_\sigma$ and
$\operatorname{cont}P(z)$. Choose the coordinate at $p$ where $z=c$,
and write $\mathfrak p$ for its prime ideal. Every other root differs
from $z$ by a unit at $\mathfrak p$. Factoring $Q_\sigma(z)=z^2A_\sigma$
at $\mathfrak p$ therefore gives $z\equiv c\pmod{\mathfrak p^e}$.
Both are units, so also $P(c)\equiv P(z)\equiv0\pmod{\mathfrak p^e}$,
even for Laurent exponents. As $N(\mathfrak p)=p$, this implies
$p^e\mid M$. The same $M$ works for every $p$, proving the divisibility
of the entire gcd. Each prime dividing $M$ can occur at at most one index
$k$ because its order would have to equal $5^{k+1}$. This proves the
eventual assertion.

## Scalar polynomial tests [candidate-D]

For an ordinary fixed polynomial $P_0(X)\in\mathbf Z[X]$ acting on
$f=F_m$, there is a shorter proof. The primitive polynomial
$a_\sigma(X)=5X^2+5\sigma X+1$ is irreducible over $\mathbf Q$ since
its discriminant is five. If it divides $P_0$, then $A_\sigma$ divides
$P_0(f)$ for every $k$. Otherwise the nonzero integer resultant of
$a_\sigma$ and $P_0$ is an integral polynomial combination of the two,
so $\gcd(A_\sigma,P_0(f))$ divides this one constant for every $k$.

One can also test any fixed integer polynomial in $F_m,F_{m-1}$.
For odd $m$ these are $(z+z^{-1})/\sqrt5$ and
$(\phi^{-1}z-\phi z^{-1})/\sqrt5$, respectively. Multiplication by a
fixed power of $\sqrt5$ clears the denominator and gives a Laurent
polynomial over $\mathcal O$. Since $5\nmid A_\sigma$, this multiplication
preserves whole-ideal divisibility at every prime power of the side.

## What follows for all prime pairs [candidate-D]

A finite menu of fixed integer-affine tests separates all distinct
equal-order prime pairs in this tower if and only if only finitely many
sides $(k,\sigma)$ have at least two distinct prime divisors. Indeed,
beyond the finite exceptional indices above each test is constant within
each side. Conversely $Q_+(z)$ separates opposite sides at every index, since
$A_+$ and $A_-$ are coprime. Indeed they are odd, one modulo five, their
difference is $10f$, and each is one modulo $f$. For each of finitely
many remaining sides use constant integers equal to their prime divisors
as additional tests. Such constants are finite sums of copies of the
unit one. This establishes existence without giving a method to locate
all exceptional sides. Whether infinitely many sides contain two
distinct prime divisors remains open here.
