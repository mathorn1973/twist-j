## 1. Result

Let

\[
C:\quad y^2=x^5-1,
\qquad
A=\operatorname{Jac}(C),
\qquad
K=\mathbf Q(j)=\mathbf Q(\zeta_5),
\]

with CM type

\[
\Phi=\{\sigma_1,\sigma_2\},
\qquad
\sigma_a(j)=j^a.
\]

For every integer `n >= 1` and every codimension `0 <= r <= 2n`, the rational
Hodge classes on `A^n` have dimension

\[
\boxed{
 d_{n,r}
 =\sum_{a+b=r}
   \binom na^2\binom nb^2,
}
\]

where a binomial coefficient is zero outside `0 <= a,b <= n`.
Equivalently, if

\[
D_n(t)=\sum_{a=0}^n\binom na^2t^a,
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{r=0}^{2n}d_{n,r}t^r=D_n(t)^2.
}
\]

Moreover the entire rational Hodge algebra of every self-power is generated by
divisor classes:

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Hdg}^*(A^n)
 =\mathbf Q[\operatorname{Hdg}^1(A^n)].
}
\]

Thus this CM5 family has no exceptional cohomological Hodge classes on any
self-power. Gate E closes **AGREE** at note level against the known classical
answer.

The proof below is exact and symbolic. No period integration, numerical
approximation, floating point, or finite-range inference is used.

## 2. The two-dimensional Lefschetz torus

The previous calibration identifies the integral polarized carrier with

\[
(\mathcal O_K,m_j,\Omega_1).
\]

The CM type is primitive. Indeed `K=Q(zeta_5)` has only the proper real
quadratic subfield `Q(sqrt5)` and no proper CM subfield from which this CM type
could be induced. Hence the associated abelian surface is simple, and its CM
endomorphism algebra is the quartic field `K`.

For the principal polarization, the Rosati involution restricts to complex
conjugation on `K`. The Lefschetz group is therefore the norm-one torus

\[
S(A)(R)
 =\{x\in(K\otimes_\mathbf Q R)^\times:x\bar x=1\}.
\]

Since `K/K^+` is quadratic and `[K^+:Q]=2`,

\[
\dim S(A)=2.
\]

Over `C`, conjugation pairs the four embeddings as

\[
\sigma_1\leftrightarrow\sigma_4,
\qquad
\sigma_2\leftrightarrow\sigma_3,
\]

so

\[
S(A)_\mathbf C\simeq\mathbf G_m^2.
\]

## 3. Exact nondegeneracy of the CM type

Write

\[
G=\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbf Q)=\langle\tau\rangle\simeq C_4,
\qquad
\tau(j)=j^2.
\]

Then complex conjugation is `tau^2`, and the chosen CM type is

\[
\Phi=\{1,\tau\}.
\]

The two odd characters of `C4` are determined by

\[
\chi_+(\tau)=i,
\qquad
\chi_-(\tau)=-i.
\]

Their CM-type sums are exactly

\[
\sum_{\phi\in\Phi}\chi_+(\phi)=1+i\ne0,
\]

and

\[
\sum_{\phi\in\Phi}\chi_-(\phi)=1-i\ne0.
\]

Thus the CM type is nondegenerate by the standard character criterion for
abelian Galois CM types. Equivalently, the Hodge group has the maximal possible
dimension `g=2`.

Because

\[
\operatorname{Hg}(A)\subseteq S(A)
\]

and both groups have dimension two,

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Hg}(A)=S(A).}
\]

This is the only external structural theorem needed for the census. It is the
special `C4` instance of the classical Ribet nondegeneracy criterion. Milne's
Lefschetz-class theorem independently implies the same final divisor-generation
conclusion once this group equality is known.

## 4. Weight decomposition on `A^n`

Let

\[
U_a=H^1(A,\mathbf C)_{\sigma_a}.
\]

Each `U_a` is one-dimensional. For the self-power `A^n`, put

\[
U_a^{(n)}=U_a\otimes\mathbf C^n,
\qquad
\dim U_a^{(n)}=n.
\]

Then

\[
H^1(A^n,\mathbf C)
 =U_1^{(n)}\oplus U_2^{(n)}\oplus U_3^{(n)}\oplus U_4^{(n)}.
\]

The Hodge types are

\[
H^{1,0}=U_1^{(n)}\oplus U_2^{(n)},
\qquad
H^{0,1}=U_4^{(n)}\oplus U_3^{(n)}.
\]

Choose the two character generators `epsilon_1,epsilon_2` of
`X^*(S(A)_C)`. Up to replacing both by their negatives, the four weights are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{block}&\text{dimension}&S(A)_C\text{ weight}\\
\hline
U_1^{(n)}&n&+\epsilon_1\\
U_2^{(n)}&n&+\epsilon_2\\
U_3^{(n)}&n&-\epsilon_2\\
U_4^{(n)}&n&-\epsilon_1.
\end{array}
\]

Since `Hg(A)=S(A)`, rational Hodge classes are exactly the rational classes
whose complexifications have total torus weight zero.

## 5. Complete invariant-monomial classification

The cohomology algebra of an abelian variety is the exterior algebra on first
cohomology:

\[
H^*(A^n,\mathbf C)=\Lambda^*H^1(A^n,\mathbf C).
\]

Take an exterior basis monomial and let

\[
k_a
\]

be the number of selected basis vectors from `U_a^(n)`. Its total torus weight
is

\[
(k_1-k_4)\epsilon_1+(k_2-k_3)\epsilon_2.
\]

The two characters are independent, so the monomial is invariant if and only
if

\[
\boxed{k_1=k_4,\qquad k_2=k_3.}
\]

Write

\[
k_1=k_4=a,
\qquad
k_2=k_3=b.
\]

Then its cohomological degree is

\[
2(a+b).
\]

Therefore there are no odd-degree invariants, and a codimension-`r` Hodge
class has

\[
a+b=r.
\]

For fixed `(a,b)`, the number of invariant exterior basis monomials is

\[
\binom na\binom na\binom nb\binom nb
 =\binom na^2\binom nb^2.
\]

Summing over `a+b=r` proves

\[
\boxed{
 d_{n,r}=\sum_{a+b=r}\binom na^2\binom nb^2.
}
\]

This is a complete all-`n`, all-codimension census, not a finite check.

## 6. Why every invariant is generated by divisors

Choose bases

\[
x_1,\ldots,x_n\in U_1^{(n)},
\qquad
y_1,\ldots,y_n\in U_4^{(n)},
\]

and

\[
p_1,\ldots,p_n\in U_2^{(n)},
\qquad q_1,\ldots,q_n\in U_3^{(n)}.
\]

The complete degree-two invariant space is spanned by

\[
x_i\wedge y_j,
\qquad
p_i\wedge q_j,
\qquad 1\le i,j\le n.
\]

Hence

\[
\dim H^2(A^n,\mathbf C)^{\operatorname{Hg}(A)}=2n^2,
\]

in agreement with the general formula

\[
d_{n,1}=2n^2.
\]

Now take any invariant exterior basis monomial. The invariant criterion gives
equal numbers of `x` and `y` factors and equal numbers of `p` and `q` factors.
Pair the selected `x` factors with the selected `y` factors and the selected
`p` factors with the selected `q` factors. After reordering the wedge factors,
the monomial is, up to sign, a product of degree-two invariants of the displayed
form.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
H^*(A^n,\mathbf C)^{\operatorname{Hg}(A)}
\text{ is generated by its degree-two invariant subspace.}
}
\]

The rational degree-two Hodge classes are exactly

\[
H^2(A^n,\mathbf Q)\cap H^{1,1}(A^n),
\]

and the Lefschetz `(1,1)` theorem identifies them with rational divisor
classes. Complexifying the rational divisor algebra therefore gives the full
complex invariant algebra above. Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Hdg}^*(A^n)
 =\text{the }\mathbf Q\text{-algebra generated by divisors}
}
\]

for every `n >= 1`.

This proves the calibration target directly in this CM5 case. Ribet's theorem
and Milne's Lefschetz-group theorem provide independent classical checks of the
same conclusion.

## 10. What is next

This result also shows why this particular CM5 surface is a calibration target
rather than an attack surface for the Hodge conjecture. The weight lattice has
full rank two, so zero-weight monomials are forced to pair opposite weights and
no exceptional Hodge tensor survives.

To encounter genuinely new algebraicity content one must break that mechanism.
The next Hodge-side frontier is therefore a **degenerate CM type or a Weil-type
construction** where the Hodge group is strictly smaller than the Lefschetz
group and additional zero-weight tensors appear.

The Tate-side calibration remains independently useful: good reduction of this
same solved CM5 object can test whether the TWIST-J `C4` bookkeeping reproduces
Frobenius and Tate-class counts before moving to exceptional classes.
