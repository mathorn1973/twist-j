# Pinned matter support and the local radiation response

Consider the vertical target column of height four. Its only nonzero initial matter is the antipodal pair $(L,-L)$ at each of $y=1,3$, where $L=1-\rho$. In either matching each occupied site is paired with an empty site. Since $L\not\equiv0\pmod{2}$, both matter contacts refuse. The B rule leaves zero matter zero, preserves the center, and multiplies a nonzero half-gap by $1$, $J$, or $J^{-1}$. All three units preserve a nonzero residue modulo two. By induction the matter support is exactly $\{1,3\}$ at every native substep, and each material current across a matching edge of this column is zero, for any native radiation history with the same initial matter in this column.

At either occupied site the half-gap is $d(t)=J^{n(t)}L$. Its Hermitian norm is $h(d(t))=h(L)\theta^{n(t)}$, with $\theta=h(J)=2-\varphi$, $\varphi=-(\rho+\rho^{-1})$, and $0<\theta<1$ in the principal embedding. Equal Hermitian norms across arms or cuts at the same site force equal $n$ and equal $d$. Even where scalar traces agree, a changed phase in this orbit entails a changed Hermitian norm and a changed modulus at either ordered embedding. For a ratio between two occupied half-gaps that start at $L$, the same argument uses the difference of their exponents.

## Exact two-tick comparison

At the end of tick one the resident and reference both retain $L$. At the second C the resident receives $\mathrm{CORR}=-2LJ$ in the B1 arm and $-2LJ^{-1}$ in the B2 arm, with $\mathrm{CLK}=0$. Its field half-gap is $LJ$ or $LJ^{-1}$, respectively. The B branches give resident half-gap $LJ$ or $LJ^{-1}$ and field half-gap $L$ in both arms. The reference at $y=3$ retains $L$ through tick two, so the resident/reference ratios are $J$ and $J^{-1}$.

The matter contribution at one cell is $H_{\rm mat}=2h(d)$. The identities $h(L)=2+\varphi$, $h(LJ)=3-\varphi$, $h(LJ^{-1})=3+4\varphi$, and $\varphi^2=\varphi+1$ give

Use the unwrapped representatives $y=1,3$ on this torus and define the matter-Q-weighted centroid as $(q_1+3q_3)/(q_1+q_3)$. At tick one both sites have $q=10$, giving centroid $2$. Tick two retains $2$ for B1 and gives $5/3$ for B2, with weights $20,10$. The support stays fixed with zero edge flux, so this centroid shift records local matter/radiation reweighting.

These conclusions concern explicit H3 preparations and native L1 accounting. The centroid depends on the chosen coordinates and weights. An inertial test requires a mobile localized component together with a defined momentum exchange or a validated momentum-dependent branch.
