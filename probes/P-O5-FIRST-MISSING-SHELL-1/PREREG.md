face included.

For a face `J` define

```text
j(J) = product_(p in J) p,
M_J  = floor(N/j(J)),
r_J  = least split prime not in J,
L_J  = link_(Delta_5(N))(J).
```

Thus `L_J` consists exactly of split-prime supports `F` disjoint from `J`
with `product(F)<=M_J`.

Define

```text
C_J(N) = {
  F in L_J :
  r_J notin F and r_J*product(F)>M_J
}.
```

The empty support is permitted. It lies in `C_J(N)` exactly when `r_J>M_J`.

The theorem package is:

```text
A. Every split prime smaller than r_J lies in J. Hence every allowed
   link vertex other than r_J is strictly larger than r_J.

B. If r_J<=M_J, let A_J be the augmented chain subcomplex of L_J
```

## 1. Link arithmetic

By definition `r_J` is the first split prime not belonging to `J`. Therefore
every split prime smaller than `r_J` belongs to `J` and is forbidden in
`L_J`. Every allowed link vertex distinct from `r_J` is larger than `r_J`.

Write `M=M_J` and `r=r_J`.

If `r>M`, no allowed split prime is at most `M`, because all other allowed
split primes are larger than `r`. Hence `L_J` contains only the empty face.

Assume `r<=M`. A face of `L_J` not containing `r` lies in
`link_(L_J)(r)` exactly when

```text
r*product(F)<=M.
```

Every face containing `r` lies in the cone on this link. Therefore all
nonterminal faces belong to the augmented cone subcomplex `A_J`.

## 2. Terminal quotient and integral homology

Let `F` be terminal. Then `r notin F`,
`product(F)<=M`, and `r*product(F)>M`.

For every `p in F`, Section 1 gives `p>r`, so

```text
product(F\{p}) <= M/p < M/r.
```

Hence every codimension-one face of `F` lies in `link_(L_J)(r)`, and therefore
in `A_J`. Repeating this argument shows every proper face lies in `A_J`.

The augmented cone `A_J` is contractible and its augmented chain complex is
acyclic over `Z`. The quotient augmented chain complex has precisely the
terminal faces as basis and zero differential. The long exact homology
sequence of

```text
0 -> C~_*(A_J;Z) -> C~_*(L_J;Z) -> quotient -> 0
```

therefore gives

```text
H~_d(L_J;Z) ~= Z^(c_d(J,N)).
```

No Smith-form assumption is needed and no torsion can occur.

When `r>M`, `L_J={empty}` and its augmented homology is `Z` in degree `-1`,
which is exactly the same formula with `C_J(N)={empty}`.

## 3. Direct terminal-pair involution

The squarefree split sum is

```text
S_5^sum(N)=sum_(S in Delta_5(N)) (-2)^|S|.
```

Expand the factor `2^|S|` by all ordered partitions `S=J disjoint-union F`:

```text
S_5^sum(N)
  = sum_(J,F disjoint split supports, j(J)product(F)<=N)
      (-1)^(|J|+|F|).
```

For each pair let `r=r_J`.

On every pair for which either `r in F` or
`r notin F` and `r*j(J)*product(F)<=N`, toggle membership of `r` in `F`
while keeping `J` fixed.

If `r in F`, removing it is always valid. Applying the rule again re-adds it,
because the original pair was valid. If `r notin F` and adding it is valid,
the next application removes it. Thus the map is an involution and changes
`|F|` by one, so it reverses the sign.

The unmatched pairs are exactly

```text
r_J notin F,
j(J)product(F)<=N<r_J*j(J)product(F).
```

These are exactly the terminal pairs of Sections 1-2.

## 4. First-missing support fibres

For an unmatched pair set

```text
n=j(J)product(F).
```

Every split prime below `r_J` lies in `J` and hence divides `n`, while `r_J`
lies in neither `J` nor `F`. Therefore

```text
r_J = r(n).
```

Conversely suppose `n<=N` is squarefree split and `n*r(n)>N`.

Every split prime below `r(n)` is forced into `J`. The prime `r(n)` is absent
from `n`. Every prime divisor of `n` larger than `r(n)` may independently be
put into `J` or `F`. Thus the terminal-pair fibre above `n` has exactly

```text
2^t(n)
```

elements. All pairs in the fibre have the same sign `(-1)^omega(n)`.
This proves E.

## 5. Primorial terminal shells

Let `r(n)=q_k`. Then `n` contains `q_1,...,q_(k-1)`, omits `q_k`, and can be
written uniquely as

```text
n=Q_(k-1)m
```

where `m` is squarefree and all its prime divisors are split and greater than
`q_k`.

The terminal condition is

```text
N/(Q_(k-1)q_k) < m <= N/Q_(k-1).
```

Moreover

```text
omega(n)=k-1+omega(m),
t(n)=omega(m).
```

Substitution into E gives F after taking integer floors.
