# The stationary primitive material support

Put \(\pi=1-\rho,\ \rho=j^2\). Evaluation at \(j=1\) modulo five identifies \(\mathcal O/(\pi)\) with \(\mathbb F_5\). Indeed \(\rho\) is a primitive fifth root, \(N(1-\rho)=5\), and \(\Phi_5(1)=5\). Thus the residue of a word in the stored \(1,j,j^2,j^3\) basis is its coefficient sum modulo five. Complex conjugation is trivial in this quotient; \(J=1+\rho\) reduces to 2 and \(J^{-1}\) to 3. Two is invertible. In particular if \(\pi^k\) divides \(2a\) then it divides \(a\).

**Statement.** On the unwrapped lattice, take any nonzero matter configuration of finite support, with arbitrary surrounding radiation. Let \(k\) be the largest integer for which every matter word is divisible by \(\pi^k\). The set of sites where the divided matter pair is nonzero modulo \(\pi\) is a nonempty finite set that stays at exactly the same coordinates at every C and B, for every subsequent step. The same statement applies to the CLK/CORR pair with its *own* common exponent. On a torus the marked set is stationary as a subset of that torus. A putative translation must stabilize that particular set.

**Proof.** At an admitted C the change of the first endpoint of a rail is \(-\pi(a-b)/2\), and the second change is its negative. A refusal leaves both words fixed. If all words of the sector lie in \((\pi^k)\), each C changes them by multiples of \(\pi^{k+1}\), preserving their individual residues after division. At B the matter center is unchanged and the half-difference is multiplied by \(1,J\) or \(J^{-1}\); radiation uses the reciprocal multiplier. Each selected two-by-two map is invertible over the local ring at \((\pi)\), since its determinant is a unit and its only denominator is 2. In ordinary integral words, the output is legal and every output word of the sector remains divisible by \(\pi^k\). Modulo \(\pi\), a divided pair is zero after B exactly when it was zero before B. This argument is conditional on whichever B the *actual full state* selects. It does not assume independent rescaling of matter and radiation would reproduce its branches. The marked sites exist because a nonzero word has finite \(\pi\)-valuation. The same nonempty set stays marked, so \(k\) itself cannot change.

**Translation.** Suppose the complete compact matter profile becomes a translate by a nonzero vector, even allowing a common ring unit or an internal transformation preserving the primitive zero/nonzero test in both directions. Its nonempty finite marked set would equal the same set plus that vector. Iterating this set equality is impossible on a torsion-free spatial lattice. This excludes even a single exact translated copy at any later cut, for either matching color, with arbitrary radiation and all possible parity refusals. A spatially constant background with both matter rails equal to the same word may be subtracted: both C and every matter branch B commute with that addition, including the original guard which uses the rail difference. Uniform matter with a nonzero half-gap, arbitrary background matter, a proper subprofile with a stationary remainder, approximate motion of a density, and motion of a decoded observable are separate questions. Modulo a period, a marked set can have a nontrivial translation stabilizer.

**Local finite classification at the undivided level.** For the four-word residue write \(m,c\) for matter and radiation centers and \(d,f\) for their half-differences. C is pointwise the identity in this quotient. At B the two centers remain fixed. An active branch requires \(f^2=4d^2\) over \(\mathbb F_5\), hence \(f=\pm2d\). Both native branches give the same necessary modular test. Their actions on \((d,f)\) are \((2d,3f)\) and its inverse. For \(d,f\ne0\) passing the modular test, either neighbor is attained by some actual integer lift: take \(f=\pm Jd\) for B1 or \(f=\pm J^{-1}d\) for B2 and lift the chosen centers. For any residue a silent lift exists by taking both gaps to be nonzero rational integers in their prescribed classes: the exact real norm factor \(h(J)=2-\varphi\) is irrational. At \(d=f=0\) even an active lift fixes the residue. Reachability here means the union of allowed edges over lifts; it assigns no branch probabilities or deterministic residue-only clock.

For each of the 25 center pairs there are 17 fixed vertices and two four-cycles of possible nonidentity updates, distinguished by \(df=2,3\). This gives 475 local classes among 625 residues. The frozen audit enumerates the graph from exact H3 collisions on witnesses for all centers/gaps, checks the predicted neighbors and component histogram, and uses the full native evolution for a dedicated sector-divisibility test. A discrepant count, edge or moving primitive mark falsifies the corresponding assertion. These labels are stationary local information. No assignment of elementary electric charges to them is part of this proof.

## Finite sector test

On an 8 by 8 torus prescribe the cut directly before B. At (2,2) put matter half-gap \(d=2\pi^k\), zero matter center, field center 1 and field half-gap \(Jd\) or \(J^{-1}d\) for \(k=0,1,2,3\). At (2,4) put matter half-gap \(\pi^{k+1}\) and zero in the other roles. Elsewhere use zero. Recover the full initial state with inverse C at bit zero; every scored forward update thereafter uses the native law. At every cut of twelve ticks score both sector exponents and both complete marked sets against their initial values, the prescribed first branch, whole accounts and return to the starting state via the native inverse. Count cuts where the undivided matter support occupies a site outside its primitive marked set. This last count is descriptive. The integer normalization uses \((4+3\rho+2\rho^2+\rho^3)\pi=5\).
