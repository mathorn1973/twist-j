# Stage 1. Directed material current

Use a 16 by 8 even torus, matching bit zero. Set \(L=1-\rho\), \(u=j+j^{-1}\), \(\varphi=-(\rho+\rho^{-1})\), \(\theta=h(J)=2-\varphi\), \(h(w)=w\bar w\). The code sign is the sign of the coefficient of \(\varphi\) in the indicated launch material account.

Each source tile occupies columns \(x,x+1\), rows 0 and 1. Each site holds \((d,-d,4,-4)\). In the negative source use \(d=4u\), in the positive source use \(d=4/u\). Put the primary and complementary tiles at columns starting at 0 and 6 in the near geometry, 6 and 12 in the far geometry. The nearest column of the far source is outside the decision cone.

In column 2 use consecutive rows \((-1,0,1,2)\) modulo 8. The negative test packet has gaps \((3JL,3JL,L,L)\), the positive packet \((3JL/u,3JL/u,uL,uL)\). A gap \(d\) means words \((d,-d,0,0)\). Put the complementary packet in column 10 and zero elsewhere. The two packets have material Hermitian stocks \(116-32\varphi\) and \(84+32\varphi\), each with scalar stock 200. Cross both code signs and both geometries, then repeat under reflection \(y\mapsto1-y\) with the same native matching bit. The common launch ledger is \(M=U=0,\ H=840,\ Q=1680\).

Use exactly three forward ticks. At 1B the primary source emits \(-4V\) with \(V=J\) for the negative source and \(J^{-1}\) for the positive source. At 2C only CORR changes in the test column, to \(-2LV\) in rows 0 and 1 in near runs. In far runs its value is zero. At 2B the negative test's row 1 takes B1 or B2 for the negative or positive near source, respectively. The reflected active row is 0. The opposite test code and all far arms are silent at this cut.

Score the increase in material H at the fixed coordinate row 1 across edge (0,1) in column 2, from 2B to 3C. Its real trace gives the signed scalar flux. Check local material conservation across C edges, opposite material and radiation work at every B, all complete accounts, inverse cuts and terminal reversal from 3B. In the active unreflected arm, the input gaps \((3JL,JL)\) become \(((2+\rho)JL,(2-\rho)JL)\). Since \((4+2\varphi)\theta h(L)=10\), the row gains 20 in H and 40 in Q. The reflected arm gives -40 on this fixed directed edge. The other fourteen fluxes are zero. Test the parity, complete edge words and signed flux independently. A differing scored value fires the relevant prediction. The edge measures transport between two matter sites.
