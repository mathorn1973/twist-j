# Exact rigid motion in the fully admitted free sector

Consider an unwrapped line, finite support, zero field in the matter experiment (or zero matter in the CORR experiment), with all relevant C pairs admitted for the stipulated number of ticks. Use two-site cells and return to the original matching color after \(2m\) ticks, \(m\geq1\). A nonzero finitely supported pair of words is a nonzero two-component Laurent polynomial \(v(z)\) in either complex embedding. Write \(M(z)\) for the two-tick matrix in the [public sector proof](../../canon/CANON.md#3-a-zero-matter-invariant-sector-of-the-unchanged-law). On one matter rail the same walk acts along the vertical axis.

With \(U=M/\rho\), the determinant is one and the trace is \(a+b(z+z^{-1})\), where \(b=(1-\rho)^2/(4\rho)=-\nu\) and \(0<\nu<1\) at either embedding. By Cayley-Hamilton the traces of \(U^m\) obey \(t_m=t_1t_{m-1}-t_{m-2}\) with \(t_0=2\). For all \(m\ge1\) their extreme powers \(z^m,z^{-m}\) each have coefficient \(b^m\).

Exact translation by \(d\) whole cells, up to any common nonzero constant on the words, would make \(\gamma z^d\) an eigenvalue of \(U^m\) over the rational function field, because the polynomial vector is nonzero. Its other eigenvalue must be \(\gamma^{-1}z^{-d}\). The two extreme degrees force \(|d|=m\), including the exclusion of the constant case. The product of their coefficients would have to be one. Its actual value is \(b^{2m}<1\). This rules out exact rigid translation or recurrence in this class. An odd-site translation returning a profile up to a scalar at one cut is outside this particular whole-cell argument. Finite periodic wraparound, refusals by the parity gate, changing shape, and dressing by interacting fields each enlarge the carrier. The two finite free seeds in the test experience parity refusals and are scored separately. This algebraic obstruction does not decide effective mass for a dispersive packet.
