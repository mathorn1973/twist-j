# Auxiliary surfaces from triples of involutions

**NON-CANONICAL; L1; candidate-T conditional combinatorial argument.**
This proof uses the public-source matrices specified in [PROOF.md](PROOF.md).
The census supplies candidate-C finite inputs until the pinned public replay
has verified them. The argument below does not establish an unverified
subgroup order or product order by assumption, and creates no Canon promotion.
It constructs an auxiliary surface, not physical geometry or a realization
of native U or J.

## 1. Complete construction and hypotheses

Let H be a finite group generated by three pairwise distinct nonidentity
involutions g_1,g_2,g_3. For i<j, put

```text
m_ij = ord(g_i g_j) >= 2.
```

Take one closed triangle T_h for every h in H. Its vertices have types
1,2,3, and its side of color i is opposite its vertex of type i. Identify
side i of T_h with side i of T_(h g_i) by the affine map matching endpoint
types. This specifies the gluing, including its endpoint convention; no
additional identifications or permutation of types are admitted.

Because g_i^2=1, this pairing is consistent. Because g_i!=1, every side is
paired with a side of a different triangle. Different colors cannot identify
vertices of different types. The resulting finite quotient is a compact
triangular cell decomposition. It need not be an abstract simplicial complex:
different cells can have the same incident vertex set.

The chamber adjacency graph has vertex set H and colored edges
h <-> h g_i. It is connected because the three involutions generate H.
Consequently the glued space is connected.

## 2. Every vertex link is a circle

For two distinct nonidentity involutions x,y, set r=xy and m=ord(r).
The relations xrx=r^-1 and y=xr put every element of <x,y> in one of the
forms r^a or r^a x, with 0<=a<m. These two sets are disjoint: otherwise
x, and then y, would belong to the cyclic group <r>, which cannot contain
two distinct nonidentity involutions. Thus <x,y> has exactly 2m elements.
Its Cayley graph with generators x,y is connected and has two distinct
neighbors at every vertex, so it is a cycle of length 2m, alternating colors.
In particular, m=2 gives a four-cycle.

Fix a vertex of type k, and let {i,j,k}={1,2,3}. Only sides i,j contain that
type. The triangles incident to the vertex represented in T_h are exactly

```text
h <g_i,g_j>.
```

These are left cosets, arising as orbits of right multiplication. Each
incident triangle contributes one interval to the vertex link. Its two
endpoints are glued to the intervals from h' g_i and h' g_j, respectively.
The link is therefore the alternating Cayley cycle of <g_i,g_j>, of length
2m_ij. The corner graph has no additional identifications: side k contains
no vertex of type k, and the prescribed gluing preserves every vertex type.

A cone on this link is a disk. Triangle interiors also have disk
neighborhoods, and an interior point of a paired side has a disk neighborhood
formed from two half-disks. Every point therefore has a disk neighborhood.
The finite glued space is a connected closed topological 2-manifold, with
no boundary and no singular vertices.

## 3. Exact counts and Euler characteristic

There are |H| triangles. For each of the three colors there are |H|/2
paired sides. Vertices of type k correspond to the left cosets of
<g_i,g_j>, each of size 2m_ij. Hence

```text
F = |H|,
E = 3|H|/2,
V = |H|/(2m_12) + |H|/(2m_13) + |H|/(2m_23),
chi = V-E+F
    = |H|/2 * (1/m_12 + 1/m_13 + 1/m_23 - 1).
```

All orders in these formulas refer to this subgroup H and its selected
generators. The chambers are the group elements themselves. The construction
does not use the original action on 15625 states as its chamber set; that
action need not be free, and its orbit sizes cannot replace |H| here.

## 4. The common covector gives orientability

For the five supplied generators a,...,e, [PROOF.md](PROOF.md) gives the
direct matrix checks over F5 for the nonzero row covector

```text
ell = (1,-1,1,-1,0,0),
ell M_g = -ell                         for every g in {a,b,c,d,e}.
```

Here M_h is the linear part of the affine map h, so M_(hk)=M_h M_k.
The sign character on H is obtained through this homomorphism; the linear
action need not be faithful.
For any word h of length r in these generators, repeated substitution gives
ell M_h=(-1)^r ell. Since ell and -ell are different over F5, the parity
does not depend on the word representing h. It therefore defines a
well-defined homomorphism

```text
epsilon:H -> {+1,-1},
ell M_h = epsilon(h) ell,
epsilon(hk) = epsilon(h)epsilon(k),
epsilon(g_i) = -1.
```

This character is surjective on every selected triple subgroup H. Its
kernel H^+ consists exactly of the elements represented by even words,
and [H:H^+]=2. The scalar signs encode a parity character; they are not
orientations inherited from the finite-field state space.

Give every triangle the reference vertex ordering (1,2,3), then choose its
orientation with sign epsilon(h). Adjacent triangles h and h g_i have
opposite signs. Their type-preserving side identification consequently
matches opposite induced boundary orientations. These triangle orientations
give one coherent orientation of the entire surface. Thus all ten triples
are orientable once the five common-covector identities and the distinct
involution hypotheses have been checked.

For each such closed connected orientable surface, its genus is

```text
genus = 1-chi/2
      = 1 + |H|/4 * (1 - 1/m_12 - 1/m_13 - 1/m_23).
```

## 5. Triangle groups, regularity and the boundary of the claim

The extended triangle group relevant to the chosen triple has presentation

```text
W = <s_1,s_2,s_3 |
     s_i^2=1, (s_i s_j)^m_ij=1 for i<j>.
```

Sending s_i to g_i gives a surjection W -> H. The orders m_ij in H are
the exact product orders, not merely divisors of a prescribed exponent.
The even subgroup H^+ is generated by

```text
r=g_2 g_3,  s=g_3 g_1,  t=g_1 g_2,
rst=1.
```

It is accordingly a quotient of the ordinary orientation-preserving
triangle group
<r,s,t | r^m_23=s^m_13=t^m_12=rst=1>. The whole H is the quotient of the
extended group W; it must not be substituted for the even triangle group.
No commutation relation between a distinguished pair of generators is
assumed. Pair order two is an admitted special case.

Left multiplication by H acts regularly on the triangle chambers and
preserves their types and gluing. Conversely, a type-preserving combinatorial
automorphism is determined by the image of one chamber: every colored
neighbor is then determined, and the chamber graph is connected. Its action
is therefore left multiplication. The right multiplications h -> h g_i
specify adjacency and are not generally color-preserving automorphisms.

This is the colored triangle description of a regular hypermap, with its
dual cubic graph having three face types. It does not require the additional
commuting-pair convention for an ordinary map. The relation to extended
triangle groups, the even subgroup and the hypermap Euler formula agrees
with [Jones, Macaj and Siran, arXiv:2508.10434v1, sections 1-2](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.10434v1).
The local manifold proof and all counts needed here are given above.

If 1/m_12+1/m_13+1/m_23<1, this is hyperbolic triangle-group type and chi<0.
The corresponding extended and ordinary triangle groups are infinite;
the enumerated H and H^+ are their finite quotients. The auxiliary surface
does not identify the original state action with a geometric tessellation,
and supplies no physical locality, event structure, metric calibration,
time coordinate or spacetime interpretation. No native U/J operation or
physical realization follows from the group census.
