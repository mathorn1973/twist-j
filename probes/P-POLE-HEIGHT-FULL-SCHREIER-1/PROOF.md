Status: NON-CANONICAL / L1. These are exact analytical candidate lemmas
for the stated integral contact architecture. A future successful program

construction is assumed below. Physical preparation, funding, a clock,

## 1. Fixed carrier and chronological action

Let O=Z[j], Phi_5(j)=0, p=j^2, u=1-j, B=1-p and X=1+j-2j^3.
On a labelled pair, C is the identity unless x-y belongs to 2O. If admitted,

    C(x,y) = (s+p d,s-p d),   s=(x+y)/2, d=(x-y)/2.

The preparation and exact target are

    I_t=(tB,-tB,u,u,X,2),
    G_t=(u+tB,u-tB,0,0,X,2),        t=-2,-1,0,1,2.

The common program is a fixed word in the fifteen labelled contacts.
Admission may differ between preparations, but the program has no access
to the input label. The helpers X and 2 must return exactly.

Every contact preserves the complex sum, sum of x conjugate(x),
Q_total=sum Tr(x conjugate(x))/2, and every labelled residue modulo u.
It satisfies C^5=identity on both branches. Inverse C^-1 is C^4.
Refused contacts still count as positions in an expanded program. A word
and its inverse are algebraic programs, not declarations of physical time.

For x=sum_(i=0)^3 a_i j^i,

    2Q(x)=5 sum a_i^2-(sum a_i)^2 >= sum a_i^2.

Thus fixed-Q lattice shells are finite. Here Q_total=10t^2+33, at most 73.
The relevant group action can therefore be regarded as a finite permutation
action on the union of these shells; it need not be faithful on any one
orbit. C^5 alone would not prove finiteness of the generated action.

Write chronological right action: z.(vw) means first v, then w. The inherited

## 2. The complete zero stabilizer

Let g=G_0 and let its complete K0 orbit be Omega. For every x in Omega
choose a word T_x from g to x, with T_g empty. The zero bridge is

    a = T_(r_0)^-1,       provided r_0 belongs to Omega.

It sends r_0 to G_0 and preserves both odd targets. If a completed K0 orbit
excludes r_0, it proves a K0 obstruction. A resource limit does not.

For every x in Omega and original generator m, put y=x.m and form

    L_(x,m) = T_x m T_y^-1.                                       (1)

Every L fixes g. The full family, including self-loop edges, generates
K1=Stab_K0(g). Indeed, for any stabilizer word m_1...m_n, follow its orbit
vertices x_i. The product of the corresponding expressions (1) telescopes,
since the successive T_x^-1 T_x cancel and x_0=x_n=g. It is exactly the
original word. No faithfulness assumption is used.

The family is inverse-closed: the edge (y,m^-1,x) gives L_(x,m)^-1.
Valid contact identities may shorten words, and equal formal normalized
words may be stored once. A DAG representation changes neither their
endpoints nor their chronological meaning. Nonempty kernel words must
not disappear merely because they fix a root or agree on sampled inputs.

This probe retains the complete family represented by the zero tree and
all original-generator edges. It does not first replace that family by a
fixed number of shortest loops. Nevertheless, applying each generator once
at a root is not the same as exploring the whole orbit of the group they
generate. This distinction remains after the complete family is retained.

## 3. Direct minus-two transport and all target collisions

Put s_-=r_-2.a, s_+=r_2.a and h_-=G_-2. Let F consist of the full family
(1), with the identity included. A direct meeting

    s_-.F0 = h_-.G0,       F0,G0 in F,

gives the bridge

    b=F0 G0^-1,            s_-.b=h_-.                             (2)

Both factors belong to K1, so b preserves zero and both odd targets.
The symbols F0,G0 here denote words, not preparations. All matching
endpoints must be exact whole six-slot states.

If the direct pass finds no minus-two bridge, the frozen fallback uses
bounded bidirectional walks on the entire family F, with at most 32768
steps per side and restart depth eight. Equation (2) applies unchanged to
the actual source and target path words at a meeting. Let P be the finite
collection of target words actually scanned, including identity. It contains
F when the direct target pass completes, and can grow with fallback paths.

The target scan supplies stabilizer generators. For each image y
of h_- under P, choose one actual representative word R_y with
h_-.R_y=y. Initialize the root image by the empty word. For every U in P
with image y retain the star collision

    H_U = U R_y^-1.                                              (3)

Each fixes h_-, zero and the odd targets. The empty-root initialization
ensures that a nonempty U already fixing h_- is retained as a collision,
rather than silently discarded as an uninteresting root image.

These star collisions generate every pairwise collision from the finite
target-word collection actually scanned. If U and V have the same image y, then

    U V^-1 = (U R_y^-1)(V R_y^-1)^-1.                             (4)

Consequently all pairwise collisions can be represented with a linear
number of star generators; a quadratic pair enumeration is unnecessary.
Keeping one representative per image is safe here precisely because the
other words are retained through (3), not declared globally equal.

Let H be the group generated by these collisions and their inverses. Then

    H <= Stab_K1(h_-).                                          (5)

Equality is not asserted. Although F generates all K1, a finite target-word
scan generally does not cover the whole K1 orbit of h_- or its complete
Schreier edge graph. The all-collisions statement concerns the finite scan,
not the entire point stabilizer.

One target representative also suffices to express the corresponding
finite-scan transporter coset. For U with image y,

    F0 U^-1 = (F0 R_y^-1)(R_y U^-1),

and the last factor belongs to H by (3). This exact factorization does not
make a bounded later search over H complete.

## 4. Plus-two transport completes a common program

For any retained minus-two bridge b, put q=s_+.b and h_+=G_2. Let D be the
finite collision-generator family from (3), with inverse directions and
identity. A final direct meeting

    q.L = h_+.M,              L,M in D,

gives c=L M^-1. Since c belongs to H, it preserves the already solved
minus-two, zero and odd states. Equations (2)-(5) therefore prove

    I_t.(W38 a b c)=G_t       for all five t.                       (6)

The literal expanded word must still pass the independently coded exact
forward and inverse checks on all five preparations before (6) is reported
as a completed construction. No shortest-word assertion follows.

The bounded construction may keep up to eight bridges b_i with distinct
resulting q_i. They share the same target-root collision family because
all b_i land at h_-. A final meet for q_i selects W38 a b_i c_i. If a new
bridge arrives during the streamed scan, all previously retained collision
generators must be applied to its new q_i as well. Conversely, each newly
retained collision is applied to every retained q_i. This backfill is needed
to claim coverage of the declared finite root pass when it completes; a
budget stop can leave that coverage incomplete.

If direct collision-generator meetings do not succeed, the plus-two
fallback uses the same bounded walk scheme on all retained collision
loops, with at most 32768 steps per side, restart depth eight and up to
eight source roots. Every such loop fixes h_-, zero and the odd states,
so equation (6) also holds for a meeting of these longer path words.
Choosing at most eight bridges is a constructive selection, not an
enumeration of the full transporter coset. A miss for every chosen root
remains inconclusive about other bridges and unrestricted products.

All generator applications are charged by their declared expanded contact
length independently of cache hits. A DAG can share word storage and reuse
exact results, but it supplies no exemption from the logical search budget.
PREREG.md fixes 300000000 search contacts and the 100000-contact final-word
limit. A budget or word-length stop is not an algebraic obstruction.

## 5. What can and cannot be concluded from a miss

The zero stage is exact when its complete orbit and all edges are available.
The next two stages test explicit finite word families: direct generator
meetings and bounded restarted walks in the relevant generator banks.
They do not enumerate all products in the generated groups. Thus even a
completed root pass and exhausted walk allowance with no meeting do not
prove that the roots lie in different group orbits. A resource-limited
pass can additionally leave its declared finite word family unfinished.

There is a valid exact alternative if later needed: complete the orbit A
of a root under a subgroup of K1. If some full-family generator maps some
x in A outside A, adjoin it and its inverse and complete the enlarged orbit.
Each such adjunction strictly enlarges A. Only checking all full-family
generators on all points of A certifies that A is the full K1 orbit.
Checking generators just at the root would miss kernel effects. This is a
separate exact algorithm, not a completeness claim for the present passes.

The external observation that individual even targets are K0-reachable
never implies that one word reaches them simultaneously. Likewise,
exchanging slots 1 and 2 conjugates the contact word. It gives a relabelled
word for the other sign, generally not the same word. This probe retains
both signs as separate endpoint obligations.

## 6. Conditional size bound for a direct paired-state search

The preceding completed probe reports the exact zero-orbit size n=1556.
A supplied external review reports M=1627606 for each individual even
K0 orbit. The following conclusion is conditional on that reported M;
this probe does not adopt or rerun that complete external census merely
by using the conclusion to motivate its search design.

For a group G acting on a zero point z and an even point e, write
Z=Stab_G(z), E=Stab_G(e), n=[G:Z], M=[G:E]. If m is the size of the Z orbit
of e, the ordered-pair orbit has size

    N=[G:Z intersect E]=n m.

Also M divides N, by the orbit-stabilizer formula. Therefore

    M/gcd(M,n) divides m,       1<=m<=M.                           (7)

For n=1556 and the reported M, gcd(M,n)=2. Hence every full zero-stabilizer
orbit inside that even G orbit has size either 813803 or 1627606. There
are at most two such orbits, and the paired G orbit has at least

    lcm(1556,1627606)=1266277468

states; its other possible size is 2532554936. These large lower bounds
explain why a direct full paired-state traversal can be unattractive.
They do not decide whether a specific source and target lie in the same
zero-stabilizer orbit, and do not guarantee that the finite root-meeting
construction succeeds. No claimed obstruction or successful program in
this probe depends on accepting the external M premise.
