# Where the fine-structure constant enters the comparison

## Physical normalization and a radius-pair observable

In SI, \( \alpha=e^2/(4\pi\epsilon_0\hbar c) \). Taking the electrostatic interaction of two unit like charges relative to infinity, \(U(r)=e^2/(4\pi\epsilon_0 r)\). Thus \(U(r)r/(\hbar c)=\alpha\). Equivalently, hold the two alternative separations \(r\) and \(2r\) for \(T=2r/c\). The dimensionless difference \([U(r)-U(2r)]T/\hbar=\alpha\) is independent of the additive potential zero. In action-phase evolution \(\exp(-iUT/\hbar)\) this corresponds to a signed relative phase \(-\alpha\) radians for like charges and \(+\alpha\) for opposite charges with the stated ordering. This is the matter interaction phase during a time set by light travel. A free photon acquires a phase set by frequency and path. The identity \(Z_0/R_K=2\alpha\) uses \(Z_0=\mu_0c\) and \(R_K=h/e^2\). All of these statements use the electromagnetic dictionary; a graph step has no supplied SI or action value. ## A restriction for the specific Canon formula

Write \(\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2,\ t=\pi^2,\ b=(160\varphi^4)^{-1},\ g=(3-\varphi)^{1/4}\). The adopted ALPHA-FORM then reads
\[
\alpha_{\rm form}={5\over64g}\,{t^4\over(t+b)^5}.
\]
The finite H3 words, their embeddings and norms, and the finite grounded D3 solutions of this test are algebraic. Fixed root-of-unity angles in radians contribute rational multiples of \(\pi\); a sphere of algebraic radius contributes \(4\pi r^2\). Consider the *restricted* class of finite sums of algebraic coefficients times rational powers of \(\pi\). This includes combining the preceding elementary geometry factors by multiplication, division of individual monomials and finite sums. The committed \(\alpha_{\rm form}\) lies outside this class. Take a common denominator \(D\) of the exponents and put \(X=\pi^{1/D}\). Every expression in the class is a Laurent polynomial in \(X\). The candidate target is \(5X^{8D}/[64g(X^{2D}+b)^5]\), a rational function with nonzero finite poles, whereas a Laurent polynomial can have a finite pole only at zero. Equality at the transcendental \(X\) would force identity of the rational functions over the algebraic numbers after clearing denominators. This is impossible. The same reasoning with a constant Laurent polynomial gives transcendence of the *specific adopted expression*. It makes no arithmetic claim about a measured physical coupling.
