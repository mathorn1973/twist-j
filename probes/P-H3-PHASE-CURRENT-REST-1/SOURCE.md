# How the signed source writes a phase

In the principal embedding define φ=−(ρ+ρ⁻¹), u=j+j⁻¹ and θ=h(J)=2−φ. Then u=1/φ, J=ju, θ⁻¹=1+φ and θ+θ⁻¹=3. On the real subring the coefficient ν(a+bφ)=b is an additive integer. Applied to the total Hermitian account it is globally conserved. At a single site one must include both material and radiation rails to account for B.

The two source encodings use real positive gaps 2u and 2/u and the same radiation gap 2. Under the other embedding the two material gaps are both negative. Their complex phases thus agree within each embedding before collision. The matter contributions to the local H account are 8θ and 8/θ. They have equal rational trace and their φ-coefficients are −8 and +8. The identical radiation adds zero to either φ-coefficient. An opposite tile pays the complete global stock in each experiment. This comparison varies the full Hermitian input. The signed component is its label on this two-element codebook.

The two material outputs differ by the exact unit j²=ρ and have equal Hermitian norm. On the next material bond both words admit on parity. The receiver reference is 2j. Its own initial B2 has prepared the same four-rail state in both histories. The incoming CLK/CORR refuse their second contacts, retaining common radiation half-gap 2J. The emitted 2j then selects B1, and 2/j selects B0. Interchanging just these two matter rails in the two measurements preserves the complete account and reverses the reader decision. Both uninterrupted histories continue by the single native rule.

On the perpendicular bond the initially empty neighbor receives a CORR value of −(1−ρ)J or −(1−ρ)/J. Its Hermitian norm is 3−φ or 3+4φ, with signed component −1 or +4. These are observable distinctions already at the next C. The radiation words are not opposite additive field amplitudes. Both source encodings have zero linear matter current in this circuit. This example exhibits local transfer of a different signed quantity into radiation, followed by spatial propagation, and a phase decision at another cell.
